# Concept Interplay: How Swing Structure, Volume Profile and Fibonacci Reinforce or Contradict Each Other Across Timeframes (Confluence Methods)

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers only the *interactions* between three dimensions: (1) price action / swing structure, (2) volume profile (VP: POC, VAH/VAL, HVN/LVN, anchored or fixed-range profiles, plus related volume-price lenses such as VSA/Wyckoff and AVWAP), and (3) Fibonacci retracements and extensions. Each dimension on its own is covered elsewhere. Source quality is flagged throughout. Most practitioner material is educational or vendor content with no out-of-sample testing. The only peer-reviewed or central-bank evidence concerns support/resistance in general (Osler; Kavajecz and Odders-White; Chung and Bellotti), not Fibonacci or volume profile specifically. Research current as of November 2026. Nothing I found from 2025–2026 changes the evidence picture. The main change is that "Fib + volume profile confluence" TradingView scripts and AI-written education pages have multiplied, and none of them comes with independent validation.

## 1. Swing structure + Volume profile (pairwise interaction)

### Takeaway
Swing structure decides **where to anchor** a profile (swing low to swing high, or a whole range). The profile then sorts the prices inside that swing into "accepted" prices (POC/HVN), which act as magnets or reaction zones, and "rejected" prices (LVN, single prints), which price tends to cross quickly. Profile edges and poor highs/lows also mark where swing-based liquidity (stops) is likely to sit. The two are mostly complementary: structure gives direction and anchors, the profile gives a price ranking inside the anchors. They contradict each other when a structural level (a prior swing high, say) sits inside an LVN, so price has no "acceptance" there.

### Cited Findings
**Anchoring profiles to swings**
- Practitioner guides tell you to draw a fixed-range profile from one swing point to another: swing low to swing high in an uptrend, or across a whole consolidation. — [ForexTester: Fixed Range Volume Profile](https://forextester.com/blog/fixed-range-volume-profile/)
- An ES/NQ-focused guide tells swing traders to profile "the last major swing high to swing low." — [FuturesHive: Volume Profile Trading for ES/NQ](https://www.futureshive.com/blog/volume-profile-trading-strategy-2025)
- Automated versions exist. One open-source TradingView script builds a separate profile for each completed swing leg, starting at one pivot and ending at the next, so an algorithm can do "profile the leg" with no manual anchoring. — [TradingView: Swing Volume Profile Pro](https://id.tradingview.com/script/7yozLqCv-Swing-Volume-Profile-Pro-WillyAlgoTrader/)
- Trader Dale (a full-time volume-profile educator, ~16 years using VP by his own account) uses a "Flexible Volume Profile" placed on a specific part of the chart rather than a fixed session. His flagship setup: find a volume cluster where price reacted before ("two reactions, which is even better than one"), wait for the pullback, and go long "from the beginning of that volume cluster." — [Trader Dale blog: My #1 Volume Profile Setup (Dec 2025)](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/12/my-1-volume-profile-setup-step-by-step.html) (page returned HTTP 503 on fetch; rules taken from the search-index snippet)
- Dale's confluence examples pair a volume cluster with a **Weekly POC** or with a resistance-turned-support flip, which combines a structural level and a profile level. — [Trader Dale blog: Trade of the week, perfect confluence (Oct 2025)](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/10/trade-of-week-perfect-confluence-volume.html)

**POC/HVN = acceptance; LVN = rejection (what the profile adds inside a swing)**
- The value area is conventionally the ~70% of volume built outward from the POC, adding the larger adjacent row each step; VAH/VAL are where traders watch for acceptance or rejection. — [TradersAgency: Volume Profile Analysis](https://tradersagency.com/blog/volume-profile-analysis); [NexusFi wiki: Volume Profile](https://nexusfi.com/wiki/trading-wiki/Volume-Profile)
- HVNs are described as acceptance zones where price consolidates and that act as S/R. LVNs are "areas of rejection where price moved through quickly." — [TradersAgency](https://tradersagency.com/blog/volume-profile-analysis); [LuxAlgo: Fixed Range Volume Profile](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/4r2CZGol-fixed-range-volume-profile/)
- Typical fixed-range sequence: mark the POC/VAH/VAL of the swing, wait for price to come back to the POC. In one ES example, after a rally well above the POC the trader waits for a pullback toward it, so the POC serves as a mean-reversion target or entry. — [ForexTester](https://forextester.com/blog/fixed-range-volume-profile/); [FuturesHive](https://www.futureshive.com/blog/volume-profile-trading-strategy-2025)
- Caveat: these sources give no consistent entry/stop/target rules and no backtests. — [ForexTester](https://forextester.com/blog/fixed-range-volume-profile/); [Ironbeam](https://www.ironbeam.com/?p=11196)
- Unverified statistics circulate, such as "65–70% of session time is spent inside the value area" attributed to CME, or a "2020 Tradamaker study" with a 65% VAH/VAL respect rate. I could not trace either to a primary source, so do not use them. — [search-result aggregation; see TradersAgency/NexusFi above]

**Poor highs/lows (profile) vs. equal highs/lows (price-action liquidity)**
- A *poor high* in Market Profile terms is a session high printed by two or more TPOs with no single-print tail above it. It reads as an "unfinished auction" and tends to be revisited. *Excess* is a single-print tail at the extreme, showing clear rejection. The concept comes from Dalton, Jones and Dalton, *Mind Over Markets* (1990). — [TheRightTrader: Poor highs and lows](https://therighttrader.com/learn/volume-and-order-flow/poor-highs-and-lows); [O'Reilly: Mind Over Markets](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/mind-over-markets/9781118659762/)
- The same source warns: "do not rename a double top as a poor high because the highs match." The TPO letter count matters, so a price-action equal high and a profile poor high are **different definitions** that sometimes coincide. — [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/volume-and-order-flow/poor-highs-and-lows)
- Context changes how a poor high reads: "a poor high far above value is a different trip than a poor high sitting on the value-area high of a balanced day." — [NexusFi: Single prints, poor highs/lows](https://nexusfi.com/a/market-structure/single-prints-poor-highs-poor-lows-market-profile)

**Break of structure confirmed by value migration**
- Value migration compares today's developing or completed value area with yesterday's. A POC that migrates in the same direction as the value area strengthens the read. — [Bookmap: Value Migration Explained](https://bookmap.com/blog/value-migration-explained-how-markets-shift-from-one-area-of-acceptance-to-another); [LuxAlgo: Value Migration](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/value-migration/)

**Swing failure / sweep beyond value edges (Wyckoff framing)**
- A Wyckoff *spring* undercuts a range low. The usual confirmation is spring, then a lower-volume test that holds above the spring low; a close back below the spring low cancels it. An *upthrust* is a push above resistance that closes back inside the range. In VSA (Tom Williams' later bar-level reading) it is a wide-spread bar closing near its low, often on high volume. — [LuxAlgo: Spring](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/spring/); [LuxAlgo: Upthrust](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/upthrust/)
- A valid VSA "test" is a quiet bar: lower volume than the prior two bars, narrow spread, coming out of immediate strength. "A test with high volume isn't a test — it's a failed test." — [LuxAlgo: VSA Test Bar](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vsa-test-bar/)
- Win rates of ~72–75% quoted on some sites for spring/test setups are unsourced marketing claims. Volume thresholds are subjective; sources recommend comparing against recent average volume rather than eyeballing. — [LuxAlgo: VSA](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/volume-spread-analysis/) and search aggregation

**Microstructure reason swing extremes attract sweeps (links to Section 7)**
- In bank order data, stop-loss buy orders cluster just *above* round numbers and stop-loss sell orders just *below*. Stops propagate trends, and the response to stop orders is larger and longer-lasting than the response to take-profit orders. — [Osler, FRBNY SR125 (2001), later J. Finance 2003](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf); [Osler, FRBNY SR150 (2002)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Mechanism linking the two dimensions.** Swing extremes are where stops sit (Osler's finding is about round numbers, but the logic of "stops just beyond an obvious level" carries over to obvious swing highs and lows). Profile edges (VAH/VAL, poor highs) are where the auction ran out of opposite-side participation. A sweep *beyond a swing extreme that is also beyond a VA edge or poor high*, followed by a fast return inside value, is the cleanest "structure + profile" reversal: stops fire (positive feedback) and find no acceptance (no new HVN forms outside). The opposite reading is a break of structure where price *builds volume* outside the old value area and value migrates, which counts as continuation. This turns the ambiguous sweep-vs-breakout question into a testable "acceptance" rule, for example "N consecutive volume bars, or X minutes, closing outside VAH plus a developing POC shift."
- **LVN inside a swing leg** has two possible roles depending on structure. With trend and structure intact, a pullback that slices through an LVN tends to reach the next HVN/POC below (the "thin area is crossed fast" claim), so the LVN is a poor place to buy and the HVN beneath it is the location. On a breakout out of a range, the LVN between the old and new value areas is the "air pocket" that should not be re-accepted; re-acceptance (volume building in the LVN) means the breakout failed. These are practitioner heuristics, not tested results.
- **POC of the impulse leg as "fair price of the impulse".** For automation: compute a fixed-range profile from the confirmed swing low to the confirmed swing high (anchors from a pivot algorithm, e.g., N-bar fractal or ATR-ZigZag). The POC is a candidate pullback location, and so are the HVN closest to the 50–61.8% retracement and the VAL of the leg profile.
- **Profile anchors must not be optimized.** Use one predeclared pivot rule (fixed N, or a fixed ATR multiple) across all instruments and timeframes, so the profile is not fitted to the outcome.
- **Contradiction cases to code explicitly:** (a) a structural level (prior swing high turned support) sits in an LVN, so expect weaker support and require a stronger trigger or skip; (b) the POC of the leg sits above the 38.2% retracement in a strong trend, so price may not reach deeper Fib levels and you should favor the shallower POC; (c) a poor high sits just above current price with no structural reason to fade it, so profile logic says it is likely to be revisited and fading below it is lower quality.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, out-of-sample study of "fixed-range profile of a swing leg" levels (POC/LVN of the leg) in futures. Everything is practitioner or vendor material.
- Dalton's primary text was not accessible. Poor high/excess definitions come from secondary summaries that consistently attribute them to *Mind Over Markets* (1990).
- Trader Dale's full step-by-step rules (stop and target placement) could not be fetched (503). Only the snippet-level rule is recorded.
- Futures-specific issues with no source: ETH vs RTH session choice, and how contract roll (back-adjusted vs unadjusted continuous contracts) changes weekly/monthly/composite profiles and swing anchors. These are material for ES/NQ/CL and should be decided explicitly.

## 2. Swing structure + Fibonacci (pairwise interaction)

### Takeaway
Fibonacci is *derived from* swing structure. Every retracement and extension is a function of two or three confirmed swing points, so Fib levels are **not an independent confirmation** of structure. Their value is as a **measurement convention**: how deep the pullback is (trend strength) and where the measured-move target sits. Pullback depth, Elliott ratios, Sperandeo's 1-2-3, Brooks' measured moves and ICT's OTE are all different rule-sets placed on the same swing anchors.

### Cited Findings
**Fib anchored to confirmed swings**
- ICT/OTE convention: draw the retracement across the impulse leg, swing low to swing high for longs and swing high to swing low for shorts. The leg must break structure and have clear swing points; "a drifting, overlapping move is not an impulse." Invalidation is a move beyond the leg's origin. — [LuxAlgo: Optimal Trade Entry](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)
- DiNapoli Levels (third-party implementation): retracements are measured from the "focus number" (the extreme of the current move) back to *each* qualifying reaction (counter-swing), up to a cap of 4. A longer swing-length parameter "pick[s] larger reactions and a longer-term focus number." — [LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)

**Pullback depth as a trend-strength gauge**
- Common heuristic: retracements of ~23.6–38.2% read as trend strength, deep ones near 61.8–78.6% as fading momentum. "The 38.2% works best in strong momentum trends; the 61.8% works best in trending-but-not-momentum markets." — [LuxAlgo: Fib Retracement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-retracement/); [TheRightTrader: Deep vs shallow pullback](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/deep-vs-shallow-pullback)
- Sources contradict each other. Some call 61.8% the "strongest and most reliable" S/R level where many continuations begin; others read a 61.8%+ pullback as a sign the trend may be weakening. "Depth is a measurement, not a signal by itself," and 50% is not a Fibonacci ratio. — [LuxAlgo: Fib Retracement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-retracement/); [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/deep-vs-shallow-pullback)
- Classical TA lineage: Pring's "50 percent rule" (a major move is often reversed ~50% in the first correction) and Fibonacci 38.2/61.8% are listed by Osler as standard ways that FX firms derive published S/R levels. — [Osler, FRBNY Economic Policy Review, July 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf); see also [StockCharts: John Murphy's Law #4, Retracements](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/stockcharts-insider-john-murphys-law-4-know-how-far-to-backtrack-retracements)

**DiNapoli's F38 / F61 rules (structure plus Fib as an explicit entry/abandon rule)**
- "The first pullback into the F38 of a fresh thrust is the with-trend entry setup." A second visit to the same F38 ("double penetration") is treated as a stronger variant. "A 61.8% penetration warns the setup is reaching the zone where it is usually abandoned." — [LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/) (third-party interpretation, not DiNapoli's own text)
- Objective points: COP (closest), OP (equals the thrust), XOP (extends it by the golden ratio), sized from the A-B thrust and anchored at the end of the reaction. — [LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)

**Extensions as measured-move targets**
- Boroden's first objective in a with-trend (Type 1) trade is the 1.272 extension of the prior swing. — [TradingMarkets: Carolyn Boroden on Fibonacci Price Clusters](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Al Brooks' Leg 1 = Leg 2 measured move: a second swing roughly equal to the first after a pullback. The main difficulty is locating where each leg starts. After a breakout from a 20–40-bar range, the first target is the range height, then a Leg1=Leg2 projection (secondary summaries of Brooks course lessons 20A/20B). — [Brooks Trading Course forum: measured move from failed leg 1](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/measured-move-from-failed-leg-1-leg-2/); [LuxAlgo: Measured Move](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/measured-move/); [Brainscape flashcards on Brooks](https://api.brainscape.com/flashcards/20-20688917/packs/23133273)
- ICT OTE targets: "the swing itself first, then extension levels beyond it." Other ICT sources use −27% and −62% projections. — [LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)

**Sperandeo 1-2-3 (structure-only trend change) and where Fib fits**
- Sperandeo's trend-change criteria (*Trader Vic*, 1991): (1) trendline break, (2) failed test of the extreme, (3) break of the correction low (in a top). Secondary sources say Sperandeo favored entering *before* condition 3 completes; conservative traders wait for the break. — [RoboForex: Trading like Sperandeo, 1-2-3 and 2B](https://roboforex.com/blog/education/trading-like-sperandeo-1-2-3-reversal-and-2b-pattern/); [LuxAlgo: 1-2-3 Reversal](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/1-2-3-reversal/)
- Bulkowski's close-based version: (1) close beyond the trendline, (2) test of the extreme A (may slightly exceed it "but it must be clear that price is moving"), (3) close beyond intervening swing B. His stats: bullish (downtrend reversal) **74 of 101 (73%)** samples rose ≥20% from low A; bearish (uptrend reversal) only **29 of 67 (43%)** fell ≥20%. He notes trendline choice can "dramatically change the trading landscape." — [Bulkowski, ThePatternSite: 1-2-3 Trend Change](https://www.thepatternsite.com/123tc.html) (stocks, daily scale; small samples; not futures)
- I found no primary Sperandeo text combining the 1-2-3 with Fibonacci. Pairing them (e.g., the point-2 retest stalling at 61.8–78.6% of the 1→2 leg) is a practitioner combination, not his stated rule.

**Elliott wave ratio guidelines**
- Frost and Prechter (*Elliott Wave Principle*, 1978) are credited with organizing the ratio guidance. Only three statements are hard *rules*; Fibonacci proportions are *guidelines*. — [Wikipedia: Elliott wave principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_wave_principle); [TheRightTrader: Elliott wave Fibonacci ratios](https://therighttrader.com/learn/elliott-wave-and-cycles/elliott-wave-fibonacci-ratios)
- Commonly cited: wave 2 retraces ~50–61.8% of wave 1 (some sources list 50/61.8/65/78.6/88.6), wave 3 ≈ 1.618 × wave 1 projected from the end of wave 2 (extended variants 2.0/2.618), wave 4 ≈ 38.2% of wave 3 (some sources: 14.6/23.6/38.2, "no more than 50%"). Ranges differ by source, and guidelines "never cancel a count on their own." — [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/elliott-wave-and-cycles/elliott-wave-fibonacci-ratios); [ChartingLens: Elliott Wave guide](https://chartinglens.com/blog/elliott-wave-theory-guide)

**ICT OTE zone (structure plus Fib plus liquidity); treat critically**
- OTE = the 0.62–0.79 band of the impulse leg with 0.705 as preferred midpoint (classical 0.618/0.786). "The band, not any single ratio, is the OTE zone." Textbook sequence: liquidity sweep, then market-structure shift with displacement, then retracement into OTE, best when it overlaps an order block or FVG. An OTE tag in a still-ranging market without a prior stop run is "a much weaker setup." — [LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)
- Same source: "The ratios themselves have no mechanical force; the zone is a place to look for evidence," and "price is under no obligation to reach it or to hold there." It cites no statistical evidence. — [LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)
- A vendor blog reports that Crossref searches found no journal article testing ICT concepts, and that its own tests found swept levels predicted *continuation* more than the textbook reversal. These tests are unverified and come from a vendor. — [ThorTradeCopier: Does ICT/SMC work?](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)

### Inferences
- **Dependence:** a Fib level and the swing it was drawn from carry the *same information*, which is the location of two pivots. Counting "Fib 61.8% + prior swing low" as two confirmations double-counts unless the prior swing low comes from a *different* leg than the Fib anchors. Fib clusters from *multiple different swings* (Boroden, DiNapoli confluence) are partly independent because they use different anchors, but all of them still come from one price path.
- **Most robust and automatable use:** pullback depth as a *regime classifier*, not an entry trigger. E.g., in an HTF uptrend (higher highs and higher lows on the pivot algorithm), measure the current pullback as a fraction of the last impulse. ≤38.2% means a strong trend: favor shallow-zone entries (DiNapoli F38, leg POC). 50–61.8% is normal: the standard zone. Beyond 78.6% means the impulse thesis is degraded (DiNapoli's "abandon" zone, ICT invalidation near the origin), so stand aside or switch to reversal logic (1-2-3 / spring).
- **Unified invalidation:** nearly every method converges on one structural invalidation, a break beyond the origin of the impulse (OTE), or the 61.8–78.6% zone as "usually abandoned" (DiNapoli). For automation, stops belong beyond the leg origin or beyond the confluence zone's far edge (Boroden: "a few ticks above the extreme of the cluster zone"), not at an arbitrary Fib line.
- **Asymmetry worth noting:** Bulkowski's 1-2-3 stats (73% bullish vs 43% bearish success at a +/-20% threshold on stocks) suggest that reversal patterns do not behave symmetrically. On equity-index futures with long-term upward drift, a bearish structure-reversal setup should probably carry a lower prior. This is an inference from equity data only.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed test shows that 38.2/61.8 levels outperform arbitrary retracement levels. The one quantitative test found (FX) says they do not (Section 7).
- DiNapoli's own book text was not retrieved. The F38/F61 and COP/OP/XOP rules come from a third-party indicator description. From memory (unverified): DiNapoli distinguishes "confluence" (retracements from different reactions coinciding) from "agreement" (a retracement coinciding with an objective-point expansion), and uses displaced MAs 3x3, 7x5, 25x5. Verify against *Trading with DiNapoli Levels* (1998) before relying on it.
- Primary Sperandeo text was not retrieved. Whether he ever paired 1-2-3 with Fib is unconfirmed.
- Primary Brooks text was not retrieved. The "magnet" wording is unverified.

## 3. Volume profile + Fibonacci (pairwise interaction)

### Takeaway
This is the pairing where the "independent dimensions" argument is strongest. Fib levels are pure geometry from two price points, while a profile is built from *where volume actually traded*. A Fib retracement that coincides with the impulse leg's POC/HVN or with a prior period's naked POC/VAH/VAL is a genuine two-dimension agreement. In practice the profile is used to **choose which Fib level to trust** (the one sitting on an HVN or POC) and to **reject Fib levels sitting in LVNs**. Extensions are validated by whether they land on HTF HVNs or naked POCs. Every source for this pairing is an indicator description or educational page; none is a validated study.

### Cited Findings
- A Fib level "is theoretical" and volume "helps verify it." The basic confluence check is whether the POC of the swing aligns with a key Fib level (e.g., 0.618). — [LuxAlgo: Auto-Anchored Fibonacci Volume Profile](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/z8gaWHWQ-auto-anchored-fibonacci-volume-profile/)
- One open-source tool combines ATR-filtered swing-pivot detection with a bar-by-bar volume profile to "automatically identify and score every Fibonacci retracement level." This shows the combination is mechanizable (anchor by pivot algorithm, profile the same leg, score Fib levels by volume at price). — [TradingView: Fibonacci Volume Confluence Engine (PhenLabs)](https://de.tradingview.com/script/8EJkZCuY-Fibonacci-Volume-Confluence-Engine-PhenLabs/)
- Practitioner checklist (indicator descriptions and guides): (1) measure the Fib and the profile over the **same leg**; (2) look for a POC or HVN inside the 0.618–0.65/0.66 "golden pocket"; (3) favor trend direction; (4) note LVN "valleys" that price may cross fast; (5) confirm with declining volume during the retracement and a volume spike on the reversal. — [TradingView script KPUDW7dp](https://www.tradingview.com/script/KPUDW7dp/); [LuxAlgo: Fibonacci Volume Profiles](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/rtqVynGv-fibonacci-volume-profiles/)
- A NexusFi article describes a confluence score with "minimum 4.0 required to trade." The factor weights could not be retrieved (403). — [NexusFi: Fibonacci confluence cluster zones](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/fibonacci-confluence-cluster-zones)
- Unsupported claims to avoid: the "golden pocket is mathematically the highest probability reversal zone for algorithms," and a quoted "71% win rate with 3.1:1 R:R." Neither has a verifiable source. — [search aggregation of TradingView/education pages above]
- Golden pocket boundaries differ by source (0.618–0.65 vs 0.618–0.66), and the ICT OTE band (0.62–0.79) is wider than the golden pocket. — [LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/); [TradingView KPUDW7dp](https://www.tradingview.com/script/KPUDW7dp/)
- Boroden, the best-known Fib-cluster practitioner, explicitly does *not* use volume, though "low-volume trading makes her less likely to act." So the classic Fib-cluster school is a single-dimension method, and the volume layer is an add-on by later practitioners. — [TradingMarkets: Boroden interview](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Trader Dale's confluence style ties volume clusters to higher-timeframe profile references (Weekly POC) and to structure (R→S flips). The search found no Dale material using Fibonacci, so attributing "VP + Fib" to Dale is **not supported**. — [Trader Dale blog Oct 2025](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/10/trade-of-week-perfect-confluence-volume.html); search result summary

### Inferences
- **How the profile picks the Fib level.** For a pullback in an uptrend, the leg's profile gives a volume ranking of the 38.2/50/61.8/78.6 levels. A rule such as "the active entry level is the Fib level with the highest volume within ±k ticks, provided it sits in an HVN and not an LVN" turns a five-level Fib grid into one location. This also *reduces* the multiple-comparisons problem, because you trade one level per leg instead of whichever level happened to hold.
- **Where they disagree.** If the leg POC sits near 23.6–38.2% (volume concentrated near the top of the impulse, e.g., a grind-up after a gap), the shallow level is the profile's preferred location and deeper Fibs are in thin territory. A break below the POC then means sliding to the next HVN, which is often the leg origin. If the POC sits low in the leg (near the origin, typical of a breakout from a base), the deep 61.8–78.6% zone coincides with "value" and the shallow levels sit in LVNs that price should cross. Either way, follow the profile on *which* level to use and keep Fib as the depth label.
- **Extensions meeting HTF profile levels.** For targets, an extension (1.272/1.618 of the leg, or DiNapoli OP/XOP) that lands within tolerance of a prior week's naked POC, VAH/VAL, or a composite HVN is a stronger magnet or take-profit candidate than either alone. Osler's finding that take-profit orders cluster at salient levels and *reflect* trends gives a mechanism for exits at such levels, and the round-number layer also applies (Section 7).
- **Independence caveat.** If the same swing anchors define both the Fib and the fixed-range profile, the two share *time* anchors but measure different things (geometry vs volume distribution). That is less dependence than Fib + swing levels, but it is not full independence. Composite, weekly, or session profiles that are *not* anchored to the swing are more independent of the Fib than a leg profile is.

### Gaps
- No academic or independent study of Fib-level × volume-node coincidence was found.
- The NexusFi scoring system's weights were not retrievable.
- No reputable, rule-based "VP + Fib" article from an established author (Dalton, Steidlmayer, Dale, etc.) was found. Content for this pairing is dominated by TradingView scripts and AI-generated education pages.

## 4. All three together, and the Roles framework (context → location → trigger → management)

### Takeaway
The cleanest way to combine the three without blending them into one indicator is to give each a **different job on a different timeframe layer**. HTF swing structure (and HTF value migration) supplies **context and bias**. Profiles (weekly/composite/leg) and Fib zones jointly supply **location**, with the profile picking the level and Fib labeling the depth. LTF price action (sweep/reclaim, MSS/BOS, 1-2-3, spring/test, VSA test bar) supplies the **trigger**. Structure (leg origin, cluster edge) supplies **invalidation**, and extensions plus opposing profile levels supply **targets**. Boroden's Type 1/Type 2 split, ICT's sweep→MSS→OTE sequence, Shannon's multi-timeframe AVWAP stacking, and Dale's HTF-POC + cluster + pullback are all versions of this layered decomposition.

### Cited Findings
- **Boroden's explicit layering:** Type 1 (highest probability) trades go *with* the immediate trend (judged on a 2- or 5-minute chart) into a cluster plus symmetry projection, and "a type 1 trade does not require a trigger." Type 2 trades take an HTF cluster *against* the immediate trend and require an LTF trigger: "Trigger means take action." Listed triggers: violation of a prior swing low/high, MA crossover, Haggerty 1-2-3, or a double top/bottom into the cluster confirmed by breaking the middle swing. Entry is on the break or the pullback after it. — [TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- **Boroden's management:** Type 1 initial risk is "just a few ticks above the EXTREME of the price cluster zone," moved to breakeven "ASAP" and then trailed. First objective is the 1.272 extension. — [TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- **ICT as a context→location→trigger chain:** sweep (liquidity), then MSS with displacement (structure), then retracement into the 0.62–0.79 OTE (Fib location), ideally overlapping an order block or FVG. Stop beyond the anchoring swing; targets are the swing extreme, then extensions. The ICT layer has **no published evidence**. — [LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/); [ThorTradeCopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)
- **Brian Shannon (Alphatrends):** popularized anchoring VWAP to meaningful swing highs/lows or news events and stacking several anchored VWAPs across timeframes. Multiple anchors form confluence zones. Stops go under "the most recent and relevant higher low." His method is taught in *Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes* (2008), with swing holds of roughly 3–6 days using weekly-down-to-intraday charts (secondary summaries). — [Alphatrends: Anchoring the VWAP for better timing](https://alphatrends.net/?p=623098); [Alphatrends podcast (NinjaTrader, May 2025)](https://alphatrends.net/archives/podcast/brian-on-ninjatrader-05-18-25/); [Yahoo Finance video: How to chart anchored VWAP across multiple time frames](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/video/chart-anchored-vwap-multiple-time-140029041.html)
- **Trader Dale:** HTF reference (Weekly POC) plus a volume cluster with prior reactions (location), then wait for the pullback and enter at the start of the cluster. — [Trader Dale blog Dec 2025](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/12/my-1-volume-profile-setup-step-by-step.html); [Oct 2025](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/10/trade-of-week-perfect-confluence-volume.html)
- **Wyckoff/VSA as trigger and management:** spring (sweep below range) then a low-volume test holding above the spring low (trigger). A close back below the spring low invalidates (management). — [LuxAlgo: Spring](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/spring/); [LuxAlgo: VSA Test Bar](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vsa-test-bar/)
- **DiNapoli as location + management:** F38 of a fresh thrust = location; 61.8% penetration = abandonment zone; COP/OP/XOP = targets. — [LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)

### Inferences
**Proposed role-and-layer map** (synthesis, not any one source's rule):

| Role | Best-suited dimension | Swing-trade layer (hold hours–days; max 1W candles) | Scalp layer (hold seconds–minutes; volume bars) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context / bias | Swing structure (HH/HL vs LH/LL on pivot algorithm), HTF value migration | Weekly + daily structure; weekly/monthly value-area migration; location vs composite value | 60m/240m structure + prior-day/overnight value relationship (open inside/outside value) |
| Location | Volume profile (POC/HVN/VAH/VAL/naked POC, leg-profile POC) **filtered by** Fib depth zone | Weekly/composite HVN or naked POC within the daily-leg 38.2–61.8% (or 61.8–78.6% in weaker trends) | Session/leg profile HVN or POC inside the 5–15m leg's retracement zone; prior-session VAH/VAL |
| Trigger | LTF price action (sweep & reclaim, BOS/MSS, 1-2-3, spring/test, VSA test bar) | 60m/240m 1-2-3 or BOS back in trend direction after tagging the location | Volume-bar swing failure / reclaim of the level; first LTF higher low after the sweep |
| Invalidation | Structure (beyond impulse origin, or cluster far edge + buffer) | Beyond daily leg origin, or beyond the 78.6% / zone edge | Beyond the sweep extreme + a few ticks |
| Targets / management | Fib extensions + opposing profile levels + round numbers | 1.272/1.618 extension meeting a weekly HVN/naked POC; prior swing extreme | Leg extreme, then the opposite side of the session value area / next HVN |

- **Rules to keep the dimensions "separate but cooperating":**
  1. Each dimension answers a different question, and none is allowed to answer another's. Fib never supplies bias. Profile never supplies the trigger. Price-action trigger never supplies location.
  2. A setup needs at least one condition from each *role*, not N confluences of any kind.
  3. Within the location role, count *independent dimensions* (one geometric and one volume-based), not the number of lines in the zone.
  4. Context must come from a higher timeframe than the trigger, with a fixed ratio (for example, about 4–6× between layers, in the spirit of Shannon's weekly→daily→65m→15m→5m stack) declared in advance.
- **Example fully specified (automatable) swing template, illustrative only:** Daily pivot structure is bullish (last confirmed pivot high exceeded). The current daily pullback is 38.2–78.6% of the last impulse. A weekly or composite HVN/POC, or a naked weekly POC, sits within the zone ±0.25 daily ATR. The 60m chart prints a sweep below a 60m swing low inside the zone, then closes back above it, then a 60m BOS. Enter on the BOS or its first pullback. Stop beyond the sweep low or zone far edge, whichever is further, plus a buffer. Target 1 is the prior daily swing high. Target 2 is the 1.272 extension if it lies within tolerance of an HTF HVN or naked POC, otherwise the swing high only.
- **Scalp analogue:** 15m/60m structure bullish. Current 5m leg retraces into a zone where the leg-profile POC (or the prior session's VAH) and the 50–61.8% band overlap within ~4–8 ES ticks. The trigger is on volume bars: a sweep of the most recent volume-bar swing low and a reclaim, or a VSA-style low-volume test that holds. Stop a few ticks beyond the sweep. Target the leg high, then the next HVN.

### Gaps
- No source empirically compares "one condition per role" against "N confluences anywhere" designs. The roles map above is a synthesis.
- No primary text from Shannon's book was retrieved; holding period and timeframe-stack details come from secondary summaries.

## 6. Confluence math, weighting and pitfalls

### Takeaway
Confluence helps only when the agreeing signals are **independent** and the rule is **fixed before looking at outcomes**. Fib levels and swing levels share anchors, so they are largely one signal counted twice. Volume-at-price is a genuinely different dimension. Every extra condition shrinks the sample and widens the room for hindsight fitting. Backtest-overfitting research shows the false-discovery risk rises with the number of configurations tried, and this applies equally to "eyeballed" confluence rules refined by chart review. Use a small number of predeclared, role-separated conditions with explicit tolerances, and prefer "one per role, required" over large weighted scores.

### Cited Findings
- **Tolerance conventions in use:** Boroden wants cluster width ideally ≤2 points in ES and 3–4 points in NQ. A near-miss of ~2 points short of the zone was "within reason." — [TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586). The LuxAlgo DiNapoli implementation groups levels from different reactions within **0.5 × ATR(14)** by default. — [LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)
- **No standard exists** for swing selection, ratio sets or cluster tolerance, so different implementations produce different zones (search-result summary of the LuxAlgo concept page; the varying tolerances above, points vs ATR, illustrate this). — [LuxAlgo: Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters/)
- **Scoring vs requiring:** practitioner scoring systems exist (e.g., "minimum 4.0 required to trade"), but published weights were not retrievable and none are validated. — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/fibonacci-confluence-cluster-zones)
- **Backtest overfitting:** Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu (2014, *Notices of the AMS*) show the probability of a spurious in-sample result rises with the number of configurations tried. Investors cannot judge this because the number of trials is rarely disclosed. Under memory effects, overfitting can make expected out-of-sample performance *negative*, not zero. — [WMU ScholarWorks: Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/); companion "Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (J. Computational Finance, 2017) — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- Secondary summary of the same work: trying ~45 configurations on 5 years of data can almost guarantee an in-sample Sharpe of ~1.0 when the true out-of-sample Sharpe is zero. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/insidiousness-of-overfitting-investment-strategies-via-iterative-backtests) (secondary; check the original for exact figures)
- **Decay and repetition of levels:** prices arriving at an S/R level after more prior bounces are more likely to bounce again, and the bounce probability *decays over time*. — [Chung & Bellotti (2021), arXiv:2101.07410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410v1). Osler found published S/R levels kept predictive power for at least five business days. — [Osler, FRBNY EPR 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)
- **Base rate matters:** in Osler's test, FX rates bounced off *arbitrary* levels 56.2% of the time vs 60.8% for published levels. Any "level held" statistic has to be compared against a high chance baseline. — [Osler, FRBNY EPR 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Independence accounting (count dimensions, not lines):**
  - *Same anchors, highly dependent:* Fib retracement of leg A, the swing low that starts leg A, and a Fib extension of the prior leg using the same pivots. Treat all of these as one "geometry" vote.
  - *Partially independent:* Fib levels from *different* swings (cluster), and AVWAP anchored at the same swing (it shares the time anchor but weights by volume).
  - *More independent:* volume-at-price levels from a profile not anchored to the current swing (prior week POC/VAH/VAL, composite HVN), round numbers (an order-clustering mechanism, per Osler), and session reference levels (prior-day high/low, overnight high/low).
  - *Trigger dimension:* LTF order-flow or price-action events (sweep/reclaim, BOS, VSA test). These are independent in *time* from the location levels.
- **Tolerance rule:** state it once as an ATR fraction of the *timeframe that defines the zone* (0.25–0.5 ATR is consistent with DiNapoli's 0.5 ATR default and with Boroden's 2-point ES spread on intraday charts). Avoid fixed ticks across regimes: a 2-point ES zone is tight in a 60-point-ATR day and loose in a 15-point day. Use the same tolerance for all instruments; per-instrument tuning invites fitting.
- **Requiring vs scoring.** For a non-backtesting, visual-review user, *required* one-per-role conditions are easier to audit and harder to overfit than a weighted score. A score has as many free parameters as it has weights, plus a threshold. If a score is used, give equal weights to independent dimensions, cap the number of factors (for example 4 or 5), and fix them before review.
- **Visual review is itself a fitting loop.** Reviewing proposals and keeping the ones that "look right" on past charts is an informal multiple-testing process (the Bailey et al. logic carries over). Mitigations: (1) freeze rules before reviewing a batch of charts; (2) record every instance the rule fires, including ugly ones, not only exemplary ones; (3) judge the rule on forward or paper-traded instances.
- **Sample-size arithmetic.** If each of four roughly independent filters passes 30% of candidate bars, the joint pass rate is about 0.8% (0.3⁴), so a high-confluence setup on a daily chart may occur only a few times a year per instrument. That is too few to form any statistical opinion and an invitation to remember only the winners. Swing-trading confluence systems should therefore be run across several instruments (ES, NQ, RTY, YM, CL, GC, ZN…) with identical rules to accumulate instances.
- **Prefer "one per role" over "more lines in the zone."** Because of the dependence structure, five Fib lines in a 2-point zone are still one geometric fact. One Fib zone plus one profile node plus one LTF trigger covers three dimensions.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly measures whether S/R levels flagged by *multiple independent methods* outperform single-method levels. The "more confluence = higher probability" claim is untested in the literature I found.
- No published validation exists for any specific weighting scheme.

## 7. Evidence base: support/resistance, order clustering, self-fulfilling levels, microstructure of volume nodes

### Takeaway
Credible evidence (central-bank and peer-reviewed) supports three narrow claims. (1) Published S/R levels do produce bounces modestly more often than chance (about 61% vs 56% in FX). (2) Stop and take-profit orders cluster at salient prices (round numbers), with take-profits *reflecting* trends and stops just beyond the level *accelerating* them. (3) Technical S/R levels coincide with peaks in limit-order-book depth. None of this evidence tests Fibonacci ratios or volume-profile nodes specifically. The one quantitative Fib test found is low-quality and negative. The "self-fulfilling" story for Fib and VP is plausible but **unproven**.

### Cited Findings
**Osler (FRBNY), the strongest relevant evidence**
- *Support for Resistance* (FRBNY Economic Policy Review, July 2000): tested daily S/R levels published by **six firms, Jan 1996–Mar 1998** (USD/JPY, USD/DEM, GBP/USD). Rates bounced off published levels **60.8%** of the time vs **56.2%** off arbitrary levels. In **all 16 firm-currency pairs** the published levels beat the artificial ones. The chance of the month-by-month outcome under no information was about **1.8%**. Predictive power lasted **at least five business days**. Firms differed markedly in skill. — [Osler (2000), FRBNY EPR](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)
- The same paper notes firms derive S/R levels from trendlines, the 50% rule and "Fibonacci series… 38.2 percent and 61.8 percent retracements," as well as private order information. Published levels are round numbers ending in 0 or 5 "much more often than they would if they were chosen at random." — [Osler (2000)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)
- *Currency Orders and Exchange-Rate Dynamics: Explaining the Success of Technical Analysis* (FRBNY SR125, 2001; J. Finance 2003): based on ~**9,700 stop-loss and take-profit orders** at a large dealing bank. Requested execution rates cluster strongly at round numbers. About **8.7%** of orders sit at levels ending in **00**, with declining clusters at 50, other 0s, and 5s, and **no special tendency at 25/75**. Take-profit orders cluster *more* strongly at round numbers, so round numbers act as "partially reflecting barriers." Stop-loss buys cluster just *above* and stop-loss sells just *below* round numbers, so rates "trend strongly after crossing round numbers." — [Osler SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)
- *Stop-Loss Orders and Price Cascades in Currency Markets* (FRBNY SR150, 2002): (1) trends are unusually rapid when rates reach levels where stop-loss orders cluster; (2) the response to stop-loss orders is larger than to take-profit orders; (3) it lasts longer. Most results are significant **for hours, not days**. The paper also finds rates respond to non-informative order flow. — [Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)
- Osler cautions that comparable orders data is generally unavailable, so she cannot prove the bank's patterns represent all FX conditional orders. — [Osler SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)

**Kavajecz & Odders-White (2004, Review of Financial Studies)**
- Support and resistance levels coincide with **peaks in limit-order-book depth**, and moving-average signals reveal information about the relative position of depth. The authors argue the relationships arise because technical rules *locate depth already in the book*, so TA has value as a liquidity-locating tool even in an efficient market. — [RePEc/IDEAS: Technical Analysis and Liquidity Provision](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html)
- Follow-up studies on Taiwan Stock Exchange and Chinese A-share order data reportedly find S/R levels related to depth peaks (abstract-level only). — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4471-4600-1_44); [Sinoss PDF](https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2013/0813/20130813093423563.pdf)

**Chung & Bellotti (2021, arXiv q-fin.ST)**
- A heuristic algorithm finds S/R levels in intraday series. More prior bounces raise the probability of another bounce. Bounce probability decays over time. S/R effects are not explained by AR(1) and add temporary predictability. — [arXiv:2101.07410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410v1) (preprint; full methods not reviewed)

**Fibonacci specifically**
- A ForexOp analysis of G10 FX pairs found price behavior at Fib retracement levels not significantly different from nearby non-Fib levels, consistent with chance. Blog-quality with unverified methodology. — [ForexOp: Fibonacci Retracement, Myth or Reality?](https://forexop.com/strategy/fibonacci-fact-or-fiction/)
- A 2011 Macalester honors thesis, "Fibonacci Retracements and Self-Fulfilling Prophecy" (N. Gupta), exists. Its findings could not be retrieved (download failed). — [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/46721086.pdf)
- Self-fulfilling explanations for Fib are asserted in practitioner material without data. — [NexusFi: Fibonacci trade management](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/fibonacci-trade-management); [StockAlarm guide](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/fibonacci-retracement-trading-guide)

**Volume nodes**
- The microstructure rationale offered by practitioners: HVN = prices where both sides transacted extensively (acceptance, consolidation); LVN = prices crossed quickly (rejection). This comes from Auction Market Theory and is **descriptive**. I found no peer-reviewed test of VP node predictive power. — [TradersAgency](https://tradersagency.com/blog/volume-profile-analysis); [NexusFi wiki](https://nexusfi.com/wiki/trading-wiki/Volume-Profile)

### Inferences
- **What the evidence licenses for confluence design:**
  1. *Salient-level effects are real but small* (about 4.6 points above a ~56% base rate in FX), so no single level should be treated as high-probability on its own. This supports needing a trigger.
  2. *Reflect vs break asymmetry:* at a salient level, take-profits (reflecting) sit *at* the level and stops (accelerating) sit *just beyond*. Mechanistically this is the "first touch often holds; a clean break runs" pattern, and the reason to place your own stop *beyond* the obvious cluster plus a buffer, not at it.
  3. *Effects are short-lived* (hours for cascades in Osler SR150; days for S/R predictive power in Osler 2000; decay over time in Chung & Bellotti). Recent levels should outweigh stale ones, and swing-trade location levels should be refreshed (for example, weekly).
  4. *Liquidity-locating view* (Kavajecz & Odders-White): levels "work" partly because they mark where resting depth already sits. In CME futures, where the book is centralized and visible (Rithmic/Tradovate DOM), visible depth and executed volume at price give *direct* measurement of what Fib/swing levels only proxy. That is an argument for letting volume-based evidence break ties between geometric levels.
- **Transferability caveat:** Osler's order data is FX from 1999–2000 at one bank. Kavajecz–Odders-White is equities. Neither is CME futures, and both predate today's HFT-dominated books. Treat the mechanisms (order clustering, stop cascades, depth at salient levels) as plausible in ES/NQ/CL, not as measured there.
- **Round numbers as a fourth, independent "level" source:** Osler's data shows the strongest clustering at "00" levels with nothing special at 25/75. For ES/NQ the analog would be whole-handle and 50/100-point levels, and whole dollars for CL. This is cheap, independent confluence that does not share anchors with Fib or swings. (Inference; untested in futures.)

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study tests Fibonacci ratio levels against arbitrary retracements in futures.
- No peer-reviewed study tests volume-profile POC/HVN/LVN predictive power in futures.
- I did not find recent (2023–2026) futures-microstructure work on stop clustering at swing extremes specifically (as opposed to round numbers).

## 8. Scalping vs swing trading: how the interactions change

### Takeaway
The roles stay the same across horizons, but the **inputs and their reliability change**. In scalping, the profile (session/leg, built from tick volume) and the immediate trigger dominate. Swings are small and noisy, so Fib on tiny legs is weak and should be at most a coarse depth band. Stop-cascade and sweep dynamics are closest to the hours-scale evidence (Osler). In swing trading, HTF structure and multi-week/composite profiles dominate, Fib on large daily/weekly legs becomes more meaningful as a widely watched reference, and extensions meeting HTF HVNs/naked POCs are the main target logic. Confluence tolerance must scale with the timeframe's ATR.

### Cited Findings
- Boroden uses 2- and 5-minute charts to judge the "immediate trend" for Type 1 trades and an HTF cluster plus LTF trigger for Type 2. Her stated ES/NQ tolerances (≤2 pts / 3–4 pts) are intraday-scale. — [TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Shannon's swing holds run roughly 3–6 days using weekly down to intraday (65/15/5-minute) charts with stacked AVWAPs (secondary summary). — [Alphatrends](https://alphatrends.net/?p=623098); [Yahoo Finance video](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/video/chart-anchored-vwap-multiple-time-140029041.html)
- Swing traders using fixed-range VP are told to cover "the last major swing high to swing low," and intraday traders to use session or leg ranges. — [FuturesHive](https://www.futureshive.com/blog/volume-profile-trading-strategy-2025); [ForexTester](https://forextester.com/blog/fixed-range-volume-profile/)
- Stop-cascade effects are statistically significant for hours but not days, and S/R predictive power in FX persisted about five business days. — [Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf); [Osler (2000)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)
- A practitioner claim holds that retracements "work somewhat better on shorter timeframes than on daily or weekly ones," attributed to wide adoption. Unsupported by data. — [Engineer the Forex: Fibonacci retracements](https://sites.google.com/view/engineer-the-forex/fibonacci-retracements)
- The Bulkowski 1-2-3 method "works on any timeframe," but his statistics are for large (≥20%) moves in stocks. — [ThePatternSite](https://www.thepatternsite.com/123tc.html)

### Inferences
**Scalping (seconds–minutes; volume bars; session profiles)**
- *Context:* 15m–60m pivot structure plus where price sits relative to the prior session's value area and the developing session POC (open inside value = rotation bias; acceptance outside = directional bias).
- *Location:* session/overnight profile levels (prior VAH/VAL/POC, naked POCs from the last few sessions, overnight high/low) and the leg-profile POC of the most recent 5–15m impulse. Use Fib only as a coarse band (for example "in the 50–78.6% half of the leg") because small-leg pivots are noisy and anchor choice dominates.
- *Trigger:* volume-bar events. Volume bars equalize the traded quantity per bar, so "a sweep of the last volume-bar swing low and reclaim within N bars" or "a VSA test bar (lower volume per unit of range than the prior two bars, narrow spread) at the level" are well-defined. (Note: on constant-volume bars, raw bar volume is constant, so VSA-style "low volume" must be redefined as bar *duration* or volume-per-tick-of-range. This adaptation is my inference, not from a source.)
- *Management:* stop a few ticks beyond the sweep extreme (Boroden-style "few ticks beyond the zone extreme"). First target is the leg extreme or the opposite side of the current value area. Because stop-cascade effects are fast (hours), scalps that do not work within a defined number of bars should be cut.
- *Pitfalls:* the profile and the trigger are both built from the same intraday prints, so make sure the "trigger" is a *new* event, not the location restated. Overnight ES/NQ volume is thin, so ETH profiles have more LVN noise. RTH vs ETH choice must be fixed in advance.

**Swing trading (hours–days; daily/weekly structure; weekly/monthly/composite profiles)**
- *Context:* weekly and daily pivot structure plus weekly value-area migration (higher/overlapping/lower value). A 1W cap means the top layer is weekly candles and weekly/monthly profiles.
- *Location:* weekly/monthly/composite HVN, VAH/VAL or naked weekly POC that falls inside the daily-leg retracement zone. On large legs, the Fib zone is a more widely watched reference (one plausible reading of Osler's finding that published S/R, including Fib-derived levels, had value for days).
- *Trigger:* 60m/240m structure events (1-2-3 / BOS back in the trend direction, or spring/test) at the location, not 1-minute noise.
- *Management:* stop beyond the daily leg origin or the zone far edge plus an ATR buffer. Targets are the prior swing extreme, then a 1.272/1.618 extension *only if* it meets an HTF HVN/naked POC or round number.
- *Pitfalls:* few instances per instrument (sample-size arithmetic in Section 6), so run identical rules across many futures. Roll handling changes weekly/monthly profiles and pivots. CL's sensitivity to inventory and OPEC news means profile levels can be gapped through.

**Cross-horizon interaction**
- Use the swing-layer location as a *filter* for scalps (only take scalp longs while price is above or inside HTF value in a bullish HTF structure, or at an HTF location). This is the cleanest multi-timeframe confluence because the HTF level and the LTF trigger are independent in timescale.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how Fib or VP level reliability varies by timeframe in futures.
- No source addresses VSA adaptation to constant-volume bars. The note above is an inference.
- No source found on RTH vs ETH profile choice for confluence systems in ES/NQ, or on roll handling for weekly/composite profiles.
