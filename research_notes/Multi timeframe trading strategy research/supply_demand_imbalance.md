## 1. Supply and demand zone frameworks with precise rules (Seiden/OTA, Moreno "Set and Forget", Wyckoff; base, basing count, departure strength)

### Takeaway
The OTA/Seiden framework is the parent of almost every retail supply/demand method. A **zone** is the small base (one to a handful of overlapping candles) that a strong, fast move left from. It is graded with "odds enhancers": strength of departure, time at level, freshness, profit margin to the opposing zone, and location on the higher-timeframe "curve". OTA never published the full scored checklist. Exact numbers such as "base body ≤ 50% of range", "2 extended-range candles" or "2:1 imbalance" come from community scripts and course copies, not from a canonical rulebook. They are usable conventions, not standards.

**Base patterns, drawing and invalidation (OTA-descended conventions)**
- Four leg-base-leg sequences. Rally-base-rally (RBR) and drop-base-rally (DBR) create demand. Drop-base-drop (DBD) and rally-base-drop (RBD) create supply. RBR and DBD are continuations; DBR and RBD are reversals, and "many practitioners grade reversal formations at range extremes highest." The base is "a compact consolidation, often just one to a handful of candles of overlapping range." The departure must be "a run of strong, one-directional candles leaving an area quickly"; a slow drift does not qualify. "Departures that break a prior swing point" get more weight. Distal line = the base's far extreme (lowest wick for demand, highest wick for supply). Proximal line = the near edge, commonly the candle bodies. Set-and-forget = limit at the proximal edge, stop beyond distal. Targets are the next opposing zone. LuxAlgo says the method "guarantees nothing" and that impulsive departures are "far easier to see in hindsight than in real time." [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Supply & Demand Zones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/supply-and-demand-zones.md)
- Zone scoring per LuxAlgo, with no numeric thresholds: "Small bodies and a short stay score high; long, messy congestion scores low." "Full-bodied consecutive candles or a gap leaving the base imply genuine imbalance." "Untested scores best, and a zone that has been deeply penetrated is usually discarded." Overlapping zones: "Refinement merges the overlaps into one wider zone or keeps only the freshest, best-formed candidate." The suggested automation proxy for departure is "candle range against average range". LuxAlgo credits Seiden with the odds-enhancer checklist and says none of it is "tested science." [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Zone Scoring & Refinement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/zone-scoring-and-refinement.md)
- Drawing conventions conflict. Wick-to-wick (whole basing structure) is aggressive. Body-to-wick is conservative. One tutorial draws the proximal line at the highest wick of the base candles only, and the distal line at the lowest point of the whole formation including leg-in and leg-out. Another uses base bodies for proximal and the extreme base wick for distal. "Neither is objectively correct; pick one convention, apply it consistently, and size the stop from the distal line it produces." [PRACTITIONER] — [MAK Trading School: proximal/distal](https://maktradingschool.com/?p=3615); [El Trader Financiado](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/supply-and-demand-zones); [TradingView idea: Proximal and Distal Line Plotting](https://tr.tradingview.com/chart/HDFCBANK/EpzdjqTF-Proximal-and-Distal-Line-Plotting-For-Supply-and-Demand-Zones)

**Base ("boring") and leg ("exciting/explosive") candle definitions — community code conventions**
- A LuxAlgo-library script classifies a candle as boring/base when its body is ≤ 50% of its high-low range. Exciting/explosive needs body > 60% of range **and** a range above an ATR-based threshold. A separate LuxAlgo-library demand-zone script uses leg = body ≥ 50% of range, base = below that. It recognizes sequences such as LBLB and LBBLB (L = leg, B = base). Other TradingView scripts use "< 50%" (strict), a body ≥ 70%, or a leg range ≥ 1.9× the base candle's range. [VENDOR/community code] — [LuxAlgo library: Demand and Supply Candles](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/KRlFYBWy-demand-and-supply-candles-open/); [LuxAlgo library: Demand Zone Candles](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/vphHn1sk-demand-zone-candles/); [TradingView: Boring Explosive Candle](https://www.tradingview.com/script/aoBHw1zq-Boring-Explosive-Candle-Muthu-S/?amp)

**Wyckoff accumulation/distribution as a "zone" concept**
- Phase A: preliminary support and the selling climax, "where a widening spread often climaxed and where heavy volume or panicky selling by the public is being absorbed by larger professional interests", followed by an automatic rally. A secondary test should come on "significantly reduced" volume and spread near the selling climax. Phase B "builds the cause". Phase C contains the spring (a move below range support that quickly reverses back into the range, a bear trap); "the volume signature is the final arbiter of the type of Spring". Phase E is markup. Schematic #2 has no spring, only higher-low tests. StockCharts' Wyckoff authors advise initial purchases "on a rising scale" during Phases C and D. [PRACTITIONER] — [StockCharts: Wyckoff Power Charting, Let's Review](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-07-wyckoff-power-charting-lets-review); [StockCharts: Reaccumulation Roundup](https://my.stockcharts.com/articles/wyckoff/2015/07/reaccumulation-roundup.html); [TradingView: Accumulation Wyckoff Events](https://tradingview.com/chart/EURUSD/xBjKhniw-Accumulation-Wyckoff-Events); [LuxAlgo: Wyckoff Accumulation Schematic](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/wyckoff-accumulation-schematic.md)

### Inferences
- **Codable OTA-style zone definition.** This is a synthesis; the parameters are my defaults, chosen from the conventions above and not taken from a canonical source.
  - *Base candle:* body ≤ 50% of range (the most common community cutoff).
  - *Base:* 1 to N_max consecutive base candles whose ranges overlap. N_max = 6 is a reasonable default; "time at level" scores 1–3 candles best. Score it rather than hard-filter.
  - *Departure (leg-out):* ≥ 1 leg candle with body ≥ 60–70% of range **and** range ≥ k×ATR (k ≈ 1.5–2.0). Alternatively, a two-candle leg whose net displacement ≥ 2× base height (Moreno's "2:1 imbalance").
  - *Additional validity:* at least one full candle entirely outside the zone ("consolidate away") before the zone is armed.
  - *Bonus:* the departure leg breaks a confirmed prior swing (BOS) and/or leaves an FVG.
- **Lines.** Proximal = extreme body edge of the base (or the highest base-candle wick for demand). Distal = extreme wick of base ∪ leg-in/leg-out boundary candle. Fix one convention globally. The stop depends on it, and the user's visual review needs consistency.
- **Odds-enhancer scorecard as code.** strength = leg range / ATR and whether an FVG exists between base and departure. time = number of base candles. freshness = touch count. profit margin = distance from proximal to the opposing fresh zone's proximal, divided by zone height (risk). curve = (price − HTF demand proximal) / (HTF supply proximal − HTF demand proximal). Each maps naturally to a 0/1/2 score. The weights and cutoffs are unvalidated choices.
- **Wyckoff is the "large base" version of the same idea.** An accumulation range is a long base (the opposite of OTA's "less time is better"). The spring is a sweep of the range low, and the LPS/test is a retest of demand. For automation, Wyckoff maps onto: range detection (see sibling volume_profile.md for balance detection) + sweep-and-reclaim of the range extreme + volume-signature test (lower volume on the test). That makes it a structure-plus-volume pattern rather than a separate zone type.
- **Tension to note.** OTA says a *short* stay at the base implies more unfilled orders. Auction theory (volume profile) says *long* time/volume at a price implies acceptance (an HVN), which also acts as support/resistance. Both can be true in different roles. A brief base launching a fast leg is an "origin" level. A long base is a value area. Do not merge the two scores.

### Gaps
- I could not access OTA's own full odds-enhancer scoring sheet (FXStreet direct pages returned HTTP 403; BabyPips returned 403). Commonly circulated tables, such as "1–3 basing candles = full score, 4–6 = half, >6 = zero" or "profit margin ≥ 3:1", could not be verified. Treat any such numbers as community lore.
- "RTM"-school supply/demand rules (fresh zones, "quasimodo", "flag limit", engulfing) were not researched. I found no reliable primary source in this pass.
- No source gives a canonical maximum number of basing candles or an ATR multiple for departure. The numbers above are design choices.

## 2. Price-action imbalance: displacement, fair value gaps (FVG), inverse FVG, volume imbalance, liquidity voids, order blocks, breaker/mitigation blocks, consequent encroachment — rule-explicit definitions and how "mitigation/fill" is defined

### Takeaway
An FVG is pure 3-candle arithmetic: bullish if candle 3's low > candle 1's high. Implementations differ on the extra conditions: a middle-candle close beyond candle 1's extreme (LuxAlgo) vs. a middle candle that is simply bullish (python `smartmoneyconcepts`). They also differ on a size filter (percent of price, ATR, or a cumulative-mean "Auto" threshold). "Mitigation" has at least four incompatible meanings: first touch, 50% (consequent encroachment) touch, full fill, and close-through. A registry must track them as separate states. Order blocks have no single definition. In the most-used open code, an OB exists only after a close beyond the last swing high/low (a BOS), and the OB candle is the extreme candle between that swing and the break. There is no peer-reviewed evidence for any of these ICT/SMC constructs (see §7).

### Cited Findings

**FVG family — LuxAlgo concept page**
- FVG: "the middle candle moves so fast that the first candle's high and the third candle's low never overlap." Displacement: "a move aggressive enough to outrun available liquidity." Consequent encroachment (CE): the midpoint of the gap, a "decision line". Inversion FVG (IFVG): "a fair value gap that price has closed through", whose role flips. Balanced price range (BPR): two opposing FVGs overlapping, "the same span rebalanced from both directions". Volume imbalance: candle *bodies* leave a gap but wicks overlap. Liquidity void: "usually refers to a larger untraded span". [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Fair Value Gap concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap.md)
- Fill semantics per LuxAlgo: a midpoint touch is "filled enough" for many models. Fills can be quick, partial ("often to the midpoint"), or never. "Raw three-candle gaps print constantly", so tools filter by minimum size (ATR or percent) or structure. "A fill is a scenario to plan around, not a certainty." The concept is timeframe-agnostic, and HTF gaps are often mapped and then executed on LTFs. No statistics are cited. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Fair Value Gap concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap.md)

**FVG / OB — python `smartmoneyconcepts` (joshyattridge), code read directly**
- `fvg(ohlc, join_consecutive=False)`. Bullish if `high.shift(1) < low.shift(-1)` **and** the middle candle `close > open`; bearish mirrored. Top/bottom are candle-3 low and candle-1 high (bullish). The signal is **indexed at the middle candle** using `shift(-1)`, i.e., future data relative to that row. **MitigatedIndex** = first candle from i+2 with `low <= top` (bullish) or `high >= bottom` (bearish), i.e., **first touch of the proximal edge**. `join_consecutive` merges consecutive same-direction FVGs into one using the highest top and lowest bottom. [CODE] — [GitHub: smart-money-concepts](https://github.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts); [raw smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)
- `swing_highs_lows(ohlc, swing_length=50)`. A swing high is the highest high within `swing_length` candles before **and after**, implemented with `shift(-(swing_length))` and a rolling max. It is labeled at the pivot bar but only knowable `swing_length` bars later. [CODE] — [raw smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)
- `ob(ohlc, swing_highs_lows, close_mitigation=False)`. A bullish OB is created when `close > high[last swing high]` and that swing has not already been crossed (a close-based BOS). The OB candle is the candle with the **lowest low between the swing high and the breaking candle** (last occurrence on ties; default = previous candle). Zone = that candle's [low, high]. Mitigation: `low < bottom` (wick) or, with `close_mitigation`, `min(open, close) < bottom`. A mitigated OB becomes a **breaker**, and the breaker is removed when `high > top`. Outputs include OBVolume (volume + 2 prior volumes) and a "Percentage" strength. The code finds `last_top_index` by binary search over **all** swing indices below the current bar, **without checking the swing's confirmation time**. A naive historical run therefore uses swings that were not yet knowable. [CODE] — [raw smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)
- Other signatures: `bos_choch(ohlc, swing_highs_lows, close_break=True)` and `liquidity(ohlc, swing_highs_lows, range_percent=0.01)`. The latter clusters swing highs/lows within 1% of range as liquidity and records the "Swept" index. [CODE/README] — [GitHub: smart-money-concepts](https://github.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts)

### Inferences
- **Four mitigation states, not one.** Implementations disagree: python = first touch; LuxAlgo FVG = far edge; LuxAlgo concept = CE often enough; LuxAlgo OB tools = wick vs close. Track each FVG/OB through `ARMED → TOUCHED (proximal) → CE_TOUCHED (50%) → FILLED (distal touched) → INVALIDATED/INVERTED (close beyond distal)`, with timestamps and bar indices for each transition. Every popular definition can then be reproduced and compared visually without re-detecting.
- **Detection-time discipline.** An FVG becomes known at the **close of candle 3**, not at candle 2 where the python lib indexes it. An OB becomes known at the **close of the BOS candle**, and only if the swing it breaks was already **confirmed** (pivot index + right-side lookback ≤ current bar). Python-style code that ignores confirmation time produces look-ahead OBs.
- **Displacement as a definable bar.** Displacement is not formally defined by ICT-derived sources beyond "aggressive". A codable version: range ≥ k×ATR(n) (k ≈ 1.5–2), body/range ≥ 0.6–0.7, closes in the outer 20–25% of its range, and preferably produces an FVG and/or a BOS. This is the same object as OTA's "strength of departure" leg-out and Moreno's "ERC candle". The vocabulary differs; the geometry is the same.
- **Order block ≈ the zone's base candle.** In the open python code, the OB candle is the extreme candle between the broken swing and the break, which is effectively the swing low/high candle. A bullish OB is therefore the last/extreme candle of an OTA demand base whose departure caused a BOS. Treat "OB" as a *subtype* of demand/supply zone (origin of a BOS-producing displacement) rather than a separate concept. Breakers and IFVGs are both "role reversal after close-through". They can share one state machine (see §4).
- **Filter by ATR, not percent of price.** Percent-of-price thresholds (LuxAlgo default mode) behave differently for ES (≈6,000+), NQ (≈20,000+) and CL (≈60–80). ATR-relative gap height (e.g., ≥ 0.25–0.5×ATR of the detection timeframe) keeps one parameter meaningful across instruments.

### Gaps
- I did not read the LuxAlgo FVG Pine source directly. Whether its mitigation test uses wick or close, and the exact Auto-threshold formula, are inferred from page text and search snippets. Defaults changed in July 2023 and are not listed.
- LuxAlgo SMC default internal/swing lengths (often quoted as 5 and 50) were not confirmed from the source.
- No rule-explicit, primary ICT source (Michael Huddleston's own material) was reviewed. Definitions here are vendor restatements.

## 3. Order-flow imbalances (footprint diagonal/stacked imbalance, absorption, delta/CVD divergence, unfinished auctions) — platform definitions, use as zones, and the distinction from price-action imbalance

### Takeaway
"Imbalance" means two different things. **Price-action imbalance** (FVG, displacement, volume imbalance) is inferred from OHLC geometry: price moved without overlap, so trade was presumably one-sided. **Order-flow imbalance** is measured directly from aggressor-classified volume at each price. The footprint version is a heuristic: ask volume at price P vs bid volume at P−1 tick, flagged above a ratio of 150% to 300% plus a minimum-volume or minimum-difference filter, with "stacked" meaning about 3 or more consecutive levels. The academic version, Cont et al.'s OFI, is built from best-bid/ask queue changes (adds, cancels, trades), not just trades. Platform defaults vary a lot: NinjaTrader 1.5× ratio with min delta 10; ATAS 150% (older default); Quantower users "around 200–300%". Stacked-imbalance zones are used as support/resistance until price trades back through them, but I found no published evidence that they carry an edge.

**Academic notions of order-flow imbalance (not the footprint heuristic)**
- Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov define OFI from changes at the best bid and ask (limit orders, cancels, market orders). Published in *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 12(1), 47–88 (2014). [ACADEMIC] — [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jfinec/v12y2014i1p47-88..html)
- Chordia & Subrahmanyam define order imbalance as buyer- minus seller-initiated trades, classified with Lee–Ready (1991), at a daily horizon for individual stocks (*JFE* 72, 2004, 485–518). The positive relation between lagged imbalance and returns reverses once current imbalance is controlled for (inventory/price-pressure effect). Imbalance-based strategies earn statistically significant returns; later work notes transaction costs may erode them. [ACADEMIC] — [Chordia & Subrahmanyam PDF (UPenn mirror)](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/stock-misbalance.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cdl:anderf:qt34k8f3pv)

### Inferences
- **Crisp distinction for the build.**
  - *Price-action imbalance (PA-IMB):* computed from OHLC of time or volume bars. It is detectable on any data feed. It asserts one-sidedness by *absence of overlap*. Its natural output is a price **range** (gap) with a CE midpoint.
  - *Footprint imbalance (FP-IMB):* computed per bar per price row from aggressor-tagged prints (Rithmic LastTrade aggressor flag → exact buy/sell volume, better than Lee–Ready or tick-rule inference). It asserts one-sidedness by *measured ratio*. Its natural output is a **set of price rows**, and when ≥ 3 consecutive, a stacked zone.
  - *Book OFI (Cont):* computed from top-of-book queue changes. It needs depth/quote data, not just trades. Its natural output is a **time series** for sub-minute prediction, not a zone.
  - These can coincide. A displacement bar that leaves an FVG often contains stacked ask imbalances inside the gap. That coincidence is a testable, codable confluence flag: "FVG range contains ≥ 3 stacked same-side FP-IMB rows".
- **Starting parameters (choices, not standards).** Diagonal ratio 3.0 (300%) with a minimum-volume floor on the larger side. NT's "min delta 10" is too low for ES during RTH. Scale the floor to the instrument, e.g., a percentile of per-row volume over the last N bars. Stacked = 3 rows. Ticks per row = 1 for ES/MES. Consider 2–4 for NQ and CL, where single-tick rows are sparse (NT's "Ticks per level" setting exists for this). Zero-denominator handling must be explicit (Sierra's "zeros as ones" option): a 25-vs-0 cell otherwise becomes infinite.
- **Absorption and exhaustion as codable bars.** Absorption at a low: aggressive sell volume at or near the bar low ≥ k× its rolling average, while the bar fails to extend more than X ticks beyond the prior low and closes off the low. Exhaustion: thin, one-sided prints at an extreme. Unfinished auction: both bid and ask volume > 0 at the bar's high (or low). A finished auction has one side at zero. All of these are intrabar features. On constant-volume bars they are cleaner because each bar holds the same participation.
- **Delta/CVD divergence ties to swing points.** Define it only at *confirmed* swing points from the swing module: price makes a higher swing high while CVD (or the swing leg's summed delta) makes a lower high. Per LuxAlgo, divergence should be read with structure, not traded alone.
- **Stacked-imbalance zones in the same registry.** Treat a stack as a zone (top/bottom = the stack's price rows) with the same state machine as FVGs/OBs. Quantower's note ("if price slices back through the stack, the aggression was genuine") is ambiguous about whether a retest that holds or a slice-through is the signal. Pre-register one interpretation per setup.

### Gaps
- Jigsaw Trading's definitions (absorption, "reload", "iceberg") were not retrieved in this pass.
- Current ATAS and Quantower default ratios were not confirmed from live docs. The ATAS 150% figure is from a 2018 blog.
- I found no peer-reviewed study that tests footprint diagonal or stacked imbalances, or unfinished auctions, as predictors of returns in futures. The academic OFI literature uses different constructions (see §7).

## 4. Relationship to swing points and market structure (zones originate at swings; BOS vs non-BOS zones; flips/role reversal; equal highs/lows and sweeps into zones)

### Takeaway
Every zone type here is anchored to a swing. A demand base *is* the swing-low region, an OB is the extreme candle before a BOS, and an FVG sits inside the displacement leg that created a new swing. Structure therefore supplies both the *qualifier* (did the departure break a confirmed prior swing?) and the *confirmation clock* (a zone or OB cannot exist before the swing it depends on is confirmed). Role reversal (breaker/IFVG/"flip zone") has a plausible microstructure story but no direct evidence. Osler's stop-loss clustering beyond levels supports "sweeps of obvious highs/lows accelerate price", which is the mechanism behind sweep-into-zone entries.

### Cited Findings
- In LuxAlgo's S&D framework, "departures that break a prior swing point" carry more weight, and zones at the *origin* of strong moves are preferred. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Supply & Demand Zones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/supply-and-demand-zones.md); [NexusFi (OTA-linked)](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=150046)
- In the python `smartmoneyconcepts` code, an OB is created *only* when a close exceeds the last swing high (bullish). The OB candle is the lowest-low candle between that swing high and the break, i.e., the swing-low candle of the leg. Mitigation through the far edge converts it to a breaker of opposite polarity. [CODE] — [raw smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)
- LuxAlgo defines a breaker as a formerly valid OB "broken and now acting as the opposite polarity (support flipped to resistance)". It defines a mitigation block by a failed swing (lower high without trading above the reference high); a sweep of the extreme reclassifies it as a potential breaker. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Mitigation Block](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/mitigation-block.md); [LuxAlgo: OB & Breaker Blocks](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/Order-Blocks-Breaker-Blocks)
- The IFVG is a gap "that price has closed through", whose role flips. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Fair Value Gap concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap.md)
- Equal highs/lows: LuxAlgo SMC marks EQH/EQL with a "Bars Confirmation" setting. The python lib's `liquidity(range_percent=0.01)` groups swing highs/lows within 1% of range and records when they are swept. [VENDOR/CODE] — [TradingView: SMC [LuxAlgo]](https://www.tradingview.com/script/CnB3fSph-Smart-Money-Concepts-SMC-LuxAlgo/); [GitHub: smart-money-concepts](https://github.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts)
- Osler (*Journal of Finance* 58(5), 2003): take-profit orders cluster at round numbers, which can explain reversals at support/resistance. Stop-loss orders cluster just *beyond* round numbers, which can explain faster moves after a level breaks. Stop-loss buys and sells cluster differently. Data: about 9,700 orders at one dealing bank, 1 Sep 1999 – 11 Apr 2000, in USD/JPY, USD/GBP and EUR/USD. [ACADEMIC] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html); [NY Fed Staff Report 125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)
- The Wyckoff spring (a break below range support that quickly reverses into the range) is a "bear trap", a sweep-and-reclaim at a structural extreme. [PRACTITIONER] — [StockCharts: Wyckoff Power Charting](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-07-wyckoff-power-charting-lets-review)

### Inferences
- **Zone-structure taxonomy for the registry.** Tag every zone with the following (each is a cheap boolean or enum from the swing module; see sibling swing_point_theory.md):
  - `origin_swing_id`: the confirmed swing the base sits at.
  - `bos_on_departure`: did the departure leg close beyond the last confirmed opposite swing? Yes means a "BOS zone" (OB-equivalent); no means "internal".
  - `swing_level`: internal or swing, matching LuxAlgo's internal (short lookback) vs swing (long lookback) structure split.
  - `swept_liquidity_before_departure`: did the base's wick take out a prior equal-low or swing low before the departure? This is the Osler stop-run mechanism.
- **Flip/role-reversal state.** On a *close* beyond the distal line, mark the zone INVALIDATED for its original polarity. Optionally spawn a child zone of opposite polarity with the same bounds (breaker / IFVG / flip zone), tagged `flipped_from = parent_id`. Keep flips off by default in automated triggers, since there is no evidence, but show them in visual review.
- **Sweep into zone.** The cleanest structure-plus-zone trigger is: a fresh HTF demand zone lies just below an EQL/swing-low cluster. Price sweeps the cluster (Osler stop cluster), trades into the zone, then reclaims (closes back above the swept level) on the trigger timeframe. The stop goes beyond the distal line. Every element is objectively codable from the swing module plus the zone registry.
- **Confirmation-clock rule.** A zone that depends on a swing (OB, BOS zone) has `known_at = max(departure_close_time, swing_confirmation_time, bos_close_time)`. Use `known_at`, not the base candle's timestamp, for any alert, trade or statistics.

### Gaps
- I found no study testing role reversal (broken support becoming resistance) at the zone level. Osler's order-clustering evidence supports acceleration through levels, not polarity flips.
- No evidence was found on whether "BOS zones" outperform "internal zones". Both rest on vendor reasoning.

## 5. Multi-timeframe: nesting HTF zones (W1/D1/H4) with LTF refinement; HTF curve (premium/discount); which TF supplies zones vs triggers; overlapping FVGs across TFs

### Takeaway
All frameworks agree on the layering: HTF zones supply location and bias ("larger time frame levels trump smaller"), and an LTF zone or structure shift *inside* the HTF zone supplies the trigger and a tighter stop. The one quasi-test I found (a blog, EURUSD) had daily-FVG context with 4H execution as its only configuration with any edge before costs. The same test showed trading daily FVGs on daily bars lost money, and that OHLC-bar backtests overstate performance relative to tick replay.

### Cited Findings
- Seiden: use the larger timeframe to see where price is on the HTF supply/demand curve, which decides buys vs sells on the smaller chart. "Larger time frame levels trump smaller time frame levels." [PRACTITIONER] — [FXStreet/OTA Q&A](https://www.fxstreet.com/education/lessons-from-the-pros-201106070000)
- LuxAlgo: "a lower-timeframe zone nested inside a higher-timeframe one can fine-tune the entry while the larger zone frames the trade." Skip "a demand zone directly beneath heavy higher-timeframe supply." HTF zones are treated as more meaningful, with entries timed on a lower timeframe. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Zone Scoring & Refinement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/zone-scoring-and-refinement.md); [LuxAlgo: Supply & Demand Zones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/supply-and-demand-zones.md)
- The curve is the range between the freshest HTF supply and the freshest HTF demand. Low on the curve means near HTF demand relative to the distance to HTF supply. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Curve Position](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/curve-position.md)
- LuxAlgo SMC draws Premium/Discount/Equilibrium zones with 50% as equilibrium, and lets FVG detection run on a user-selected (higher) timeframe. [VENDOR] — [TradingView: SMC [LuxAlgo]](https://www.tradingview.com/script/CnB3fSph-Smart-Money-Concepts-SMC-LuxAlgo/)
- The FVG definition is timeframe-agnostic; HTF gaps are often mapped and then executed on LTFs. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Fair Value Gap concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap.md)
- Curupira blog test (EURUSD, Feb 2026). Daily-FVG context with 4H entries gave +2,505 pips, PF 1.17, over 1,034 out-of-sample trades, before costs. Trading daily FVGs on daily bars lost 216 pips. Daily-bar OHLC backtests showed PF 4.28, which the author calls an artifact; tick-replay PFs ranged 0.80 (5-min) to 1.17 (4H). The best R:R (1.7:1) gave PF 1.114 before costs, and the author estimates costs push it to breakeven. [BLOG-TEST] — [Curupira: FVG Magnetism](https://curupira-blog.pages.dev/blog/fvg-magnetism-fair-value-gaps)

### Inferences
- **Role assignment by timeframe.** Pick a timeframe ratio of about 4–6× between layers (consistent with sibling multi_timeframe_price_action.md).
  - *Location/bias:* W1 and D1 zones (base/departure), plus D1/H4 FVGs, define the curve and the eligible areas. Premium/discount = position in the range between the freshest opposing HTF zones, or the HTF dealing range from confirmed swings.
  - *Setup:* H4/H1 (or a large constant-volume bar) zone or OB nested inside an HTF zone.
  - *Trigger:* M5/M15 (or a small constant-volume bar) structure shift or reclaim, an LTF FVG formed by the reaction, or FP-IMB stacking/absorption at the zone edge.
  - *Risk:* stop beyond the *refined* LTF distal line, with an outer failsafe beyond the HTF distal line.
- **Refinement algorithm.** When price first enters an HTF zone Z, scan the LTF bars that formed Z (same timestamps) for an LTF base/departure or OB inside [Z.distal, Z.proximal]. If one exists, use it as the refined entry. Its proximal/distal must lie within Z. Otherwise use Z's CE or the proximal edge. This avoids look-ahead because the LTF bars that formed Z are already closed.
- **Overlapping FVGs across timeframes.** An H1 FVG inside a D1 FVG is not independent evidence; it is the same displacement sampled at two resolutions. Count "HTF FVG present" once, and use the LTF FVG only for entry precision (its CE or edge). The same applies to an OB inside an FVG inside a zone: one event, three labels.
- **Weekly (W1) caveat.** On W1, a 3-candle FVG spans about three weeks and often coincides with a large macro event. Sample size is tiny, so use W1 only as the "curve" (location), never as a trigger.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study tests MTF nesting of zones or FVGs in futures. The only quantitative signal is one blog test on EURUSD.
- No source defines how to "set the curve" mechanically when multiple fresh HTF zones exist (nearest? strongest? most recent?). This is a design decision.

## 6. Links to the other dimensions (FVG/voids vs LVNs and single prints; zones vs HVNs; Fibonacci OTE overlap/double-counting; volume bars and FVGs)

### Takeaway
FVGs, liquidity voids, single prints and LVNs all describe the same physical fact: little trade happened at those prices. They are measured with different instruments: candle overlap, time (TPO) and contracts (volume profile). On **constant-volume bars**, an FVG is close to an LVN by construction, so treating them as separate confluences double-counts. A supply/demand base is typically a micro-HVN at the origin of an LVN leg. Fib OTE (62–79%), OB (leg origin) and FVG (inside the leg) all derive from one swing leg, so a three-way confluence is mostly one piece of information.

### Cited Findings
- LuxAlgo: single prints are "close cousins" of LVNs and the price-action FVG. "A low-volume node is thinness measured in contracts traded; a single print is thinness measured in time spent." An FVG "is candle arithmetic… visible on any chart; singles are session-profile structures confirmed only at the close." LuxAlgo treats the LVN as the closest analog to a single print; both mark prices the market did not accept. [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Single Prints](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/single-prints.md); [LuxAlgo: High/low-volume Nodes](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/high-low-volume-nodes.md)
- Other educators call single prints "the Market Profile equivalent of an FVG", or the same phenomenon viewed through time-based vs price-based lenses. NexusFi defines LVNs as valleys between HVNs on a volume profile. A liquidity void is described as broader than a single FVG. [PRACTITIONER] — [Recapio digest: ICT FVG vs Single Prints (Chart Champions)](https://recapio.com/digest/ict-fair-value-gap-smc-vs-single-prints-by-chart-champions); [NexusFi: Single Prints](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/single-prints); [NexusFi: Fair Value Gaps/Price Imbalances](https://nexusfi.com/a/market-structure/fair-value-gaps-price-imbalances); [The Right Trader: Liquidity Void](https://therighttrader.com/learn/smart-money-concepts/liquidity-void)
- ICT's OTE is the 0.62–0.79 band of the impulse leg (0.705 midpoint), "best when it overlaps an order block or FVG". (Verified by the sibling concept_interplay.md researcher.) [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Optimal Trade Entry](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)
- Fib clusters treat confluence with non-Fib references (including supply/demand) as stronger. (Verified by the sibling fibonacci.md researcher.) [VENDOR] — [LuxAlgo: Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- Volume-weighted bar construction: NinjaTrader volumetric bars let delta and imbalance be computed per price row inside any bar type, including volume bars. [PLATFORM DOC] — [NinjaTrader 8: Volumetric Bars](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/order_flow_volumetric_bars.htm)

### Inferences
- **FVG on constant-volume bars ≈ an LVN detector.** With fixed volume V per bar, a bullish FVG means the middle bar alone traded the gap prices [H1, L3]; bars 1 and 3 did not. If the middle bar's range is R ticks and the gap is G ticks, volume at gap prices is at most about V and typically about V×G/R. That is much less than a balanced price, which receives volume from many bars. So on volume bars, an FVG is nearly a sufficient condition for a local LVN in the session or composite volume profile. On time bars it is not. A 1-minute news bar can hold enormous volume across its range, so a time-bar FVG may sit on a *high*-volume price band. **Recommendation:** when using volume bars, merge "FVG" and "LVN" into one confluence factor ("thin-traded band"). When using time bars, require LVN confirmation from the profile before treating an FVG as thin.
- **Volume bars change FVG geometry.** A displacement that is one time bar becomes several volume bars, because volume clock speeds up in fast markets. The time-bar FVG then appears as several consecutive smaller FVGs, or none if the volume bars overlap. Use `join_consecutive` merging (python lib behavior) and a gap-height filter relative to *volume-bar* ATR, not time-bar ATR. Expect fewer, smaller FVGs per displacement on high-volume volume bars.
- **Zones vs HVNs.** A short OTA base on a time chart often shows as a small HVN in the anchored profile of the leg (price paused, so volume accumulated), with an LVN above it (the departure). "Demand zone = small HVN at the lower end of an LVN leg" is a profile-native way to detect zones. It can be used as a cross-check of the candle-based detector (see sibling volume_profile.md for HVN/LVN algorithms).
- **Fib/OB/FVG double-counting.** For one impulse leg from swing A to swing B: the OB is at A's candle (Fib 100% retrace region, or 78.6–100%); the FVG is within the leg (often mid-leg, whose CE frequently lands near 50–62%); the OTE is 62–79%. A "triple confluence" of OB + FVG + OTE is therefore largely produced by the geometry of a single leg. Score it as **one** location event with sub-labels, not three independent votes. True independence comes from *different* inputs: an HTF zone from a *different* leg, a composite-profile LVN/HVN from a different period, footprint absorption (order flow), or options levels (see sibling options_gamma_equity_context.md).

### Gaps
- No study quantifies the overlap rate between FVGs and LVNs on volume bars vs time bars. The "≈ LVN" claim above is a derivation, not a measurement. It is cheap to verify visually on the user's own data.
- No source tests whether profile-detected zones (HVN at an LVN leg origin) and candle-detected zones behave differently.

## 7. Evidence base (gaps/gap fills; wide-range bars; S/R at prior consolidation; order-flow imbalance predicting returns; tests of FVG/OB)

### Takeaway
- **Moderate academic support:** S/R levels coincide with resting liquidity and stop/take-profit clustering (Osler; Kavajecz & Odders-White). Intraday S/R levels show statistically significant bounce behavior that decays with age (Chung & Bellotti). Order-flow imbalance strongly explains *contemporaneous* and very short-horizon price changes (Cont et al.; Chordia & Subrahmanyam), but the effects in E-mini futures decay within about a second.
- **Mixed:** large opening moves in index futures tend to reverse intraday (Grant et al.), yet first-half-hour returns predict last-half-hour returns (Gao et al.). Daily gap-fill "myths" mostly fail rigorous tests (Caporale & Plastun), while small ES opening gaps filled very often in 2002–2004 data.
- **No peer-reviewed evidence:** FVG, OB, breaker, CE, and footprint stacked-imbalance rules. Blog tests with random baselines suggest FVG "fill" rates are about what random levels would show.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Gaps and gap fill**
- Caporale & Plastun, "Price gaps: Another market anomaly?" (*Investment Analysts Journal* 46(4), 2017). Gap = today's open vs yesterday's close; daily stocks, FX and commodities, 2000–2015. Behavior was mostly "not inconsistent with market efficiency", except FX, where a strategy generated abnormal profits. A 2020 follow-up on US stocks reported results contrary to "the myth that price gaps tend to get filled". [ACADEMIC] — [Brunel repository (BURA) full text](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/25828/5/FullText.pdf); [BURA handle](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/14372)
- Grant, Wolf & Yu (*Journal of Banking & Finance* 29(5), 2005). "Highly significant intraday price reversals" in US stock index futures after large price changes at the open, Nov 1987 – Sep 2002. The overreaction was more pronounced after large *positive* opens. Significance is "sharply reduced" after a bid-ask transaction-cost proxy. [ACADEMIC] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v29y2005i5p1311-1327.html); [Montclair research page](https://researchwith.montclair.edu/en/publications/intraday-price-reversals-in-the-us-stock-index-futures-market-a-1/)
- Yuan (2015) is cited in the price-gap literature for "highly significant intraday price reversals in the US stock index futures market following large price changes at the market opening". I did not retrieve Yuan's paper itself. [ACADEMIC, secondary citation] — [Brunel repository (BURA) price-gap paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/25828/5/FullText.pdf)
- ES opening-gap fills, 15 Jan 2002 – 20 Feb 2004, 528 gaps, RTH close-to-open, fill = prior close touched during the next RTH session. ≤ 1 pt: 93% filled (71/76). ~2 pt: 90%. ~3 pt: 82%. ~5 pt: 60%. ~8 pt: 54%. ~10 pt: 40% (6/15). Above 11 pt there were too few observations. [PRACTITIONER study, old sample] — [MyPivots: Fading the Gap](https://mypivots.com/gapfill)

LuxAlgo notes that "any gap-fill statistic depends entirely on the definition used". **(b) Wide-range / momentum bars and subsequent returns**
- Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum" (*JFE* 129(2), 2018). The S&P 500 ETF's first half-hour return (measured from the prior close) predicts the last half-hour return, 1993–2013. The working-paper out-of-sample R² is about 1.2%; versions differ. Proposed channels are institutional rebalancing and late-informed trading. The effect was replicated internationally and in commodity futures. [ACADEMIC] — [EconPapers: JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); [Working paper PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25481)

**(c) Support/resistance at prior levels (relates to zones)**
- Osler (2003, *JF*): take-profit clustering at round numbers and stop-loss clustering just beyond them provide a microstructure basis for reversals at S/R and acceleration after breaks. One bank, 1999–2000 FX data. [ACADEMIC] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html); [NY Fed SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)
- Kavajecz & Odders-White (*RFS* 17(4), 2004): S/R levels coincide with peaks in limit-order-book depth. The relation stems from technical rules locating depth *already in place* on the book; technical analysis is framed as revealing liquidity provision, consistent with efficiency. Follow-ups in Taiwan and Chinese A-shares found similar S/R–depth links. [ACADEMIC] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html); [Springer chapter (follow-up)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4471-4600-1_44); [sinoss.net PDF (A-shares follow-up)](https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2013/0813/20130813093423563.pdf)
- Chung & Bellotti (arXiv 2101.07410, 2021; a 2022 *Mathematics* journal listing surfaced in the same search but I did not verify that it is the published version). A heuristic finds intraday S/R levels that "reverse price trends statistically significantly". Levels with **more prior bounces are more likely to bounce again**. Bounce probability **decays over time**. The effect is not explained by AR(1) processes. The authors attribute the root cause to "a large volume of limit orders stacked at certain price levels". [ACADEMIC] — [arXiv 2101.07410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410v1); [IDEAS (journal version)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jmathe/v10y2022i20p3888-d947666.html)
- Zapranis & Tsinaslanidis (2012, *Applied Financial Economics*): support levels predict trend interruptions better than resistance, but fail to beat buy-and-hold. [ACADEMIC] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apfiec/v22y2012i19p1571-1585.html)
- A TradingView post with unconfirmed authorship reports average support bounce rates of 28.4% (SD 20.5%) and concludes that zones act as momentum, not reversal, barriers. This contradicts Chung & Bellotti and is weakly sourced. [FORUM] — [TradingView: Support and Resistance in Financial Markets](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/NQ1!/E9llteXp-Support-and-Resistance-in-Financial-Markets/)

**(d) Order-flow imbalance and short-horizon returns**
- Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014): a linear OFI–price-change relation, slope ∝ 1/depth, stable across time scales. Trade-volume-based relations are noisier. US equities. [ACADEMIC] — [arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402)
- Chordia & Subrahmanyam (2004): daily stock order imbalance (Lee–Ready signed) relates to returns through price pressure. Lagged positive relations reverse after controlling for contemporaneous imbalance. Strategies are statistically significant, but costs may erode them. [ACADEMIC] — [UPenn mirror PDF](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/stock-misbalance.pdf)
- S&P 500 E-mini at one-second resolution (arXiv 2508.06788, 2025; author given as Takahashi in search summaries, not verified). Price and flow impacts are significant at one second, broadly consistent with LOB-model predictions. Shocks "dissipate almost entirely within a second". Around macro announcements, price impact rises, flow impact declines and volatility spikes. Short-term predictability may stem from order-flow autocorrelation and other microstructure effects. [ACADEMIC, preprint] — [arXiv 2508.06788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06788v2)
- Multi-asset cross-impact of OFI "mainly manifests at short-term horizons and decays rapidly in time". [ACADEMIC, preprint] — [arXiv 2112.13213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13213v3)

### Inferences
- **Evidence grades for this dimension** (A = replicated academic; B = academic, single or indirect; C = practitioner data; D = none or vendor only):
  - S/R at levels with resting orders or stop clusters: **B+**. Osler (FX), Kavajecz & Odders-White (equities, depth), Chung & Bellotti (intraday). This supports *some* zone concept, but at levels where orders rest, which is not necessarily OTA bases.
  - Level decay with age: **B** (Chung & Bellotti). Supports a maximum-age / decaying weight.
  - "Freshness" (first touch strongest): **D, and contradicted.** Chung & Bellotti find *more* prior bounces imply *higher* bounce probability. Treat touch count as a *feature to observe*, not a rule. Do not auto-discard after one touch without the user's visual judgment.
  - Large opening move leading to intraday reversal in index futures: **B** (Grant et al.), weakened by costs. Intraday momentum (first to last half-hour): **B+** (Gao et al. plus replications). A displacement bar alone does not predict continuation vs reversal; context (time of day, location) matters.
  - Small daily opening-gap fills in ES: **C** (2002–2004 practitioner data). Generic daily gap fill: **B, against** (Caporale & Plastun).
  - OFI/trade imbalance explaining short-horizon price changes: **A** for contemporaneous/sub-second, **B** for daily stock imbalance. Predictive power for horizons relevant to retail execution (minutes) is **not established**, and E-mini evidence says decay happens in about 1 second.
  - FVG/OB/breaker/CE/IFVG: **D**. Blog tests with baselines suggest no fill-rate edge vs random levels.
  - Footprint stacked imbalance, absorption, unfinished auction: **D**. These are platform heuristics with no published validation.
- **Mechanistic reconciliation.** The academic support is for *liquidity at levels* (resting limit orders, clustered stops). OTA's story ("unfilled institutional orders left at the base") is a special case that is only partly testable. With Rithmic depth data, the user could observe whether resting depth actually sits inside detected zones. That would turn a narrative into an observable filter in the spirit of Kavajecz & Odders-White, and needs no backtest.
- **Practical implication for an automation-first user who won't backtest:** prefer triggers whose mechanism has B-level support, and treat FVG/OB/footprint labels as *descriptions* for the visual review.
  - Sweep of a swing/EQ cluster: Osler stop clusters.
  - Reaction at a level with resting depth or prior bounces: Kavajecz & Odders-White; Chung & Bellotti.
  - Time-of-day momentum/reversal context: Gao et al.; Grant et al.

### Gaps
- No study of FVG/OB on CME futures with proper look-ahead-free detection and costs was found.
- No academic test of "base/departure" zones as defined by OTA was found. Chung & Bellotti's S/R levels are defined by bounces, not bases.
- No study tests footprint diagonal or stacked imbalances on ES/NQ/CL. The E-mini OFI work uses book-event OFI at 1-second horizons.
- Chordia, Roll & Subrahmanyam's market-wide imbalance work (2002) and futures-specific trade-sign studies were not retrieved in this pass.

## 8. Automation: real-time, look-ahead-free detection of zones, FVGs, OBs and footprint imbalances; a zone registry (freshness, touch/mitigation, invalidation, merging, age, width caps); implementations worth citing

### Takeaway
Every object here is only known after a confirming event: the departure candle close (zone), candle 3's close (FVG), the BOS close plus the swing confirmation (OB), or the bar close (footprint stack). The most-used open code (python `smartmoneyconcepts`) indexes signals at the formation bar and does not check swing confirmation time. Using it naively in a historical or live system introduces look-ahead. A single registry with a common state machine handles all zone types: base zones, FVGs, OBs/breakers, stacked-imbalance zones and IFVG/flip zones. It should hold touch counts, four mitigation levels, close-based invalidation, ATR width caps, age decay and overlap merging, so every proposal can be shown for visual review with its full provenance.

OB mitigation = wick (default) or body (close_mitigation) beyond the far edge, which makes it a breaker; the breaker is removed when price exceeds the opposite edge. `join_consecutive` merges adjacent FVGs. #### Detection rules with explicit `known_at`
All detectors run on bar close events from one tick stream (Rithmic LastTrade with aggressor side) that builds all timeframes and volume bars (see sibling automation_architecture.md and volume_bars_vs_time_bars.md).

```
# --- Base/departure zone (OTA/Moreno style), per timeframe TF ---
on_bar_close(TF, i):
  ATR = atr(TF, 14) computed through bar i-1    # no peeking at bar i in its own threshold
  # departure = bar i (or bars i-1..i) is a leg candle:
  leg = body(i)/range(i) >= 0.6 and range(i) >= k_dep*ATR and close in outer 25% of range
  if leg:
     walk back j = i-1, i-2, ... while bar j is a base candle (body/range <= 0.5)
         and ranges overlap; stop at N_max (e.g., 6)
     if 1 <= n_base <= N_max:
        demand if leg is bullish, supply if bearish
        pattern = RBR/DBR/RBD/DBD from the leg-in direction (bar before the base)
        proximal/distal per the global drawing convention
        height = |proximal - distal|; reject if height > w_max*ATR or < w_min*ATR
        create zone(state=PENDING, known_at=close(i))
  # arming: Moreno "2:1 + one full candle away"
  for zone in PENDING: if max excursion from proximal >= 2*height and some later bar
        lies fully outside the zone -> state=ARMED (known_at = that bar's close)

# --- FVG (bullish shown) ---
on_bar_close(TF, i):     # candle 3 = i, candle 2 = i-1, candle 1 = i-2
  if low[i] > high[i-2] and close[i-1] > high[i-2] and (low[i]-high[i-2]) >= g_min*ATR:
     create fvg(top=low[i], bottom=high[i-2], ce=(top+bottom)/2, known_at=close(i))
  optionally merge with an FVG created at i-1 of the same sign (join_consecutive)

# --- Order block / BOS zone ---
on_bar_close(TF, i):
  S = last swing high with S.confirmed_at <= close(i)  # confirmation check (python lib omits this)
  if close[i] > S.price and not S.crossed:
     S.crossed = True
     ob_bar = argmin(low[S.idx+1 .. i-1]) (last on ties)   # same as smc.py
     create ob(top=high[ob_bar] or body-top, bottom=low[ob_bar], known_at=close(i),
               bos_level=S.price, swing_level=S.level)

# --- Footprint stacked imbalance (per bar, from aggressor-tagged prints) ---
on_bar_close(TF, i):
  for each price row p (ticks_per_row per instrument):
     buy_imb(p)  = ask[p]   >= R * max(bid[p-1], 1) and ask[p] >= vmin   # Sierra-style zero handling
     sell_imb(p) = bid[p-1] >= R * max(ask[p], 1)   and bid[p-1] >= vmin
  stacks = runs of >= 3 consecutive buy_imb (or sell_imb) rows
  create fp_zone(top, bottom of stack, known_at=close(i)); optional: discard if bar i+1 trades into it
```

#### Registry schema (one table for all zone types)
- **Identity and provenance:** `id, type {BASE, FVG, OB, BREAKER, IFVG, FP_STACK}, polarity, tf_or_barspec, instrument, contract_month/roll_id, created_bar, known_at, pattern (RBR…), origin_swing_id, bos_on_departure, swept_liquidity_before_departure, parent_id (flips)`.
- **Geometry:** `proximal, distal, ce, height, height_atr, refined_child_ids (LTF refinements)`.
- **Quality features (no weights baked in):** `n_base_bars, departure_range_atr, departure_has_fvg, fp_stack_inside, profit_margin_R (to the opposing armed zone), curve_position (0–1 from HTF zones), resting_depth_inside` (optional, from Rithmic depth snapshots).
- **State machine:** `PENDING → ARMED → TOUCHED(n) → CE_TOUCHED → FILLED → INVALIDATED (close beyond distal) | EXPIRED (age) | MERGED`, with per-transition timestamps. `touch_count` increments on each new entry into the zone after price has left it by ≥ x×height or ≥ m bars, to avoid counting jitter as touches. Use tick data for intrabar touch order.
- **Invalidation:** close beyond distal on the zone's own TF (default). Optionally a wick beyond distal + buffer. A close-through on a *lower* TF does not invalidate an HTF zone. Close-through can spawn a child IFVG/BREAKER/flip (display only by default).
- **Age:** `max_age` in bars of the zone's TF (e.g., D1 zones about 60–120 bars, H1 about 200, M5 about one or two sessions). Use a decaying `age_weight` consistent with Chung & Bellotti's decay finding. Specific numbers are design choices.
- **Width caps:** reject or flag zones with `height > w_max×ATR(TF)` (e.g., 1.0–1.5×ATR) or `< w_min×ATR` (e.g., 0.1×ATR). For ES/MES, NQ/MNQ and CL/MCL use the micro and full contracts' common price series; caps are in ATR units, not points.
- **Merging:** same polarity, same TF family, overlap ≥ 50% of the smaller zone's height (or gap between zones < 0.25×ATR) → merge into the union, or keep the freshest/best-scored and mark the other MERGED (LuxAlgo's two options). Never merge across polarity. Opposite-polarity overlap = BPR, a display-only label.
- **Futures-specific hygiene (engineering):**
  - Build zones on the **actual traded contract**, or on a back-adjusted series with zones shifted by the roll adjustment. Never let a roll gap create a fake FVG or base.
  - Decide ETH vs RTH sessions once. RTH-only charts manufacture daily "gaps" that are opening gaps, not 3-candle FVGs (MyPivots' ES gap study is RTH close to RTH open).
  - The CME daily maintenance break and weekend reopen create gaps on 24h time charts. Flag FVGs whose candle-2 spans a session break.

#### Setups with explicit, automatable rules (for the user's visual-review pipeline; none backtested here)
1. **HTF fresh zone + LTF reclaim (Seiden curve + structure).** Conditions: D1/H4 zone ARMED; curve_position ≤ 0.3 for longs (≥ 0.7 for shorts); profit_margin_R ≥ 2–3 to the opposing armed HTF zone. Trigger: on first or second touch, an LTF (M5 or volume bar) close back above an LTF swing high formed inside the zone (CHoCH), or an LTF bullish FVG formed inside the zone. Stop beyond the refined distal, failsafe beyond the HTF distal. Target the opposing HTF zone's proximal. Evidence basis: S/R-liquidity literature (B) plus vendor rules (D).
2. **Set-and-forget limit (Moreno/OTA).** A limit at the proximal of an ARMED zone with score ≥ threshold. Stop at distal + buffer. Target ≥ 2:1, or the opposing zone. Fully mechanical; the visual review happens before arming.
3. **Sweep → zone → reclaim (Osler mechanism).** An EQL/swing-low cluster lies within ≤ 0.5×ATR above a demand zone. Price trades below the cluster into the zone. Trigger: a close back above the swept level, optionally with FP absorption at the low (heavy aggressive sell volume, no extension). Stop below the zone's distal or the sweep extreme, whichever is further.
4. **Displacement + FVG CE retrace (ICT-style).** Conditions: HTF bias from the curve; an LTF displacement creates a BOS and an FVG; a limit at CE (or the proximal edge). Stop beyond the OB/leg origin. Flag it explicitly as **evidence grade D**, and as a single-leg geometry event (do not add OTE as extra confluence).
5. **Stacked-imbalance retest (order flow).** A ≥ 3-row stacked buy imbalance forms during a departure from, or inside, an HTF demand zone. On first retest of the stack with no opposite stack or absorption, enter with a stop beyond the stack. Grade D, but it uses the Rithmic aggressor data the user already has.
6. **Opening-gap context (ES/NQ RTH).** Small RTH opening gaps (well under ATR) are candidates for fill toward the prior close, per old ES data (C). Large opening moves have reversal tendencies (Grant et al., B but cost-sensitive), and first-half-hour direction predicts the close (Gao et al., B+). These conflict, so use them as *context filters* for the zone setups, not standalone triggers.

#### Review/visualization payload
For each proposal, render the zone rectangle (proximal/distal/CE) on its own TF and on the trigger TF. Include the base and departure bars, the swing that was broken, and touch history. Add the footprint rows inside the zone, if any, plus the registry's state-transition log with `known_at` times. This lets the user verify that nothing was known before it could have been.

- Parameter values above (k_dep, N_max, w_max, g_min, R, vmin, max_age, merge overlap) are reasoned defaults from the cited conventions, not validated thresholds.
- I did not find a production-grade open-source C++/C#/Rust zone registry for CME data to cite. Implementations seen are Pine (LuxAlgo), python (`smartmoneyconcepts`) and closed platform features (Sierra, NT, ATAS, Quantower).
