# Multi-Timeframe (MTF) Price Action Frameworks and Automatable Setups/Triggers (Futures: ES/NQ/CL; seconds/ticks up to a 1-week cap)

Scope note for the report writer: these notes cover (1) how traders stack timeframes, (2) how to define and resolve HTF/LTF alignment objectively, (3) price action setups and triggers precise enough to code, (4) the evidence base, and (5) how to synchronize multi-series data in code without look-ahead. Swing point definitions are covered in depth by a separate researcher; here they are referenced only. Source quality varies widely in this domain. Primary/peer-reviewed and official platform documentation are marked as such. Practitioner/vendor claims are flagged. Where I propose an operational rule definition myself (for automation), it is placed under "Inferences" and labeled as a proposal, not a sourced fact. Research date: November 2026. Note on tool limits: several primary pages (Brooks glossary, CMC/Shannon interview, O'Reilly Elder excerpt, JBF abstract) returned 404/403 during this session, so some framework details rest on secondary summaries, flagged below.

## 1. Established MTF frameworks and recommended timeframe ratios

### Takeaway
The only widely cited, explicitly ratio-based MTF framework is Elder's Triple Screen, which uses a "factor of five" between adjacent timeframes (long-term chart sets direction, intermediate chart finds pullbacks against it, short-term chart times entry). Other frameworks (Shannon, Brooks, Murphy, Wyckoff) share the same hierarchy (HTF context -> MTF setup -> LTF timing) but give no hard ratio, and even Elder-style practitioners report that the exact factor is subjective.

### Cited Findings
- Elder's rule: each timeframe relates to the next larger and smaller one by approximately a factor of five. The justification is calendar-based: ~4.5 weeks per month, exactly 5 trading days per week, ~5-6 hours per (equity RTH) trading day — [ForexOp: Multiple Timeframe Trade Analysis](https://forexop.com/technical/multiple-timeframe-trade-analysis/); [Commodity Charting Tools blog: Triple Screen](https://commoditychartingtools.blogspot.com/2009/01/triple-screen-trading-system.html) (secondary summaries of Elder; the exact wording was not verified against Elder's book in this session).
- Triple Screen structure: Screen 1 = trend-following indicator on the long-term chart to set direction (e.g., the slope of a 13-week EMA can serve as the first screen); Screen 2 = oscillators on the intermediate chart to find pullbacks against the Screen-1 trend; Screen 3 = short-term chart used to time the entry — [ForexOp](https://forexop.com/technical/multiple-timeframe-trade-analysis/); [A Discounted View blog](https://adiscountedview.blogspot.com/2016/01/the-triple-screen-trading-system.html) (secondary).
- Origin: Elder used Triple Screen in his own trading from 1985 and first presented it publicly in April 1986 in Futures Magazine; the main book source is *Trading for a Living* — [Scribd copy of "Triple Screen Trading System" article](https://es.scribd.com/document/6630691/Tsd) (copy of an Elder article; provenance not independently verified).
- Practitioner pushback on the ratio: a NexusFi user reported the proportion between timeframes is subjective and that a factor of ~16 worked better for him in EUR trading — [NexusFi thread](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=57069) (anecdotal).
- Brian Shannon (*Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes*, Alphatrends): core idea is to identify the prevailing trend on a higher timeframe and use lower timeframes for entries/exits; uses at least two, often three or more timeframes; a recurring rule attributed to him is that the lower timeframe should never override the higher timeframe's trend — [CMC Markets Opto: "I trade using multiple timeframes" by Brian Shannon](https://cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/opto/i-trade-using-multiple-timeframes-by-brian-shannon) (page returned 404 when fetched; content here comes from low-quality search summaries, LOW CONFIDENCE). Shannon's own free material: [Alphatrends Swing Trading Guide (PDF)](https://alphatrends.net/wp-content/uploads/Alphatrends-Swing-Trading-Guide1.pdf) (exists; not read in this session).

### Inferences
- The "factor of five" is a calendar convenience for equity RTH, not a law. For 23-hour CME Globex futures, daily-to-hourly is ~23x (or ~6.5x if only RTH is used), so the user must decide whether the "daily" layer means the full Globex session or RTH. Daily-to-240m on a 23h session is ~5.75x, which preserves the factor-of-five spirit better than daily-to-60m.
- A ratio of roughly 3x-6x between adjacent layers is the defensible practical band: below ~3x the layers are nearly redundant (they show the same swings); above ~8-10x the lower layer's swings are too small to map onto the upper layer's structure, so a pullback on the setup layer may comprise only a couple of bars on the bias layer. This is reasoning, not a tested result.
- All the named frameworks reduce to the same three roles: Bias (direction you are allowed to trade), Setup (where, i.e., a pullback/level/pattern), Trigger (when, i.e., the bar or break that enters). Mapping each candidate strategy explicitly to these three roles makes rules auditable for visual review.

### Gaps
- Shannon's own stage definitions (stage 1-4 basing/advancing/topping/declining, his MA choices, AVWAP use) could not be verified from a primary source in this session (CMC page 404; book mirrors were spam).
- Al Brooks' explicit guidance on higher-timeframe context (e.g., his commentary that a pullback on the 5m is often just a bar on the 60m/daily), John Murphy's top-down approach (*Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*), and Wyckoff higher-timeframe phases (accumulation/markup/distribution/markdown, schematics) were not retrieved from citable sources in this session. They should be treated as background knowledge needing citation by the report writer, or omitted.
- No study was found that tests which inter-timeframe ratio performs best.

## 2. Concrete timeframe stacks for scalping and for intraday/swing (weekly = highest allowed)

### Takeaway
No authoritative source prescribes specific futures stacks; they follow from the factor-of-five idea. Proposed stacks below are derived, not sourced. The layer roles (bias / levels / setup / trigger) matter more than the exact minutes.

### Cited Findings
- Elder's hierarchy: long-term chart = trend/direction, intermediate = pullback against trend (oscillator), short-term = entry timing — [ForexOp](https://forexop.com/technical/multiple-timeframe-trade-analysis/) (secondary).
- Shannon-style usage: at least two, often three or more timeframes simultaneously, with the HTF defining the trend and LTFs used for precise entries/exits — [CMC Markets Opto (404 on fetch; low confidence)](https://cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/opto/i-trade-using-multiple-timeframes-by-brian-shannon).
- NinjaTrader warns that multi-series synchronization accuracy is limited by timestamp granularity ("the granularity of the timestamps will dictate how accurately NinjaTrader can synchronize the bars"), and that to reproduce real-time event sequencing historically you need Tick Replay — relevant to tick/second-based trigger layers — [NinjaTrader 8 Help: Multi-Time Frame & Instruments (official)](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).

### Inferences (proposed stacks; derived, not sourced)
- Scalping stack A (ES/NQ intraday): 60m = bias (structure + position vs prior day high/low/close and session open); 15m = setup/levels (pullback location, opening range, IB); 3m = trigger (signal bar break). Ratios 4x/5x.
- Scalping stack B (faster): 15m = bias; 5m = setup (Brooks-style bar counting is traditionally done on the 5m); 1m or a tick/volume bar = trigger. Ratios 3x/5x. Tick/volume bars (e.g., ES 1500-2000 tick, or volume bars) normalize activity so the trigger layer is not dominated by quiet overnight bars; they have no fixed time ratio, so the "ratio" should be judged by average bars per setup-layer bar.
- Intraday-to-swing stack: Weekly = bias (13-week EMA slope / weekly swing structure / position vs prior week high/low and weekly open); Daily = setup (pullbacks, NR4/NR7/inside day, 2B at prior swing); 240m or 60m = trigger. For the 23h Globex day, Daily->240m ~6x, 240m->60m = 4x.
- Swing alternative: Daily = bias, 240m = setup, 60m or 30m = trigger, with Weekly used only as a "location" filter (where in the weekly range price sits) rather than a fourth gate, to avoid over-filtering.
- What each layer contributes: Bias layer = allowed direction (and "no-trade" when mixed); Levels (can be computed from the bias or a still-higher layer) = prior period H/L/C, period opens, overnight H/L, IB; Setup layer = a defined pullback or pattern at/near a level; Trigger layer = a stop-entry beyond a signal bar or a break/close beyond a micro-structure point, which also defines the initial stop.
- Cap at weekly means monthly levels (monthly open, prior month H/L) can still be computed from daily/weekly data as reference levels without using monthly candles as a decision layer; the user should decide whether that respects the cap.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that recommends specific minute-based stacks for ES/NQ/CL. No evidence on whether time bars vs tick/volume bars improve trigger quality in futures.

## 3. Defining "trend alignment" across timeframes objectively

### Takeaway
Alignment can be made objective with a small set of binary tests per timeframe (MA slope, price vs MA, swing structure, price vs prior-period high/low/close, price vs period open, premium/discount within HTF range). The best academic support is for simple trend definitions on longer horizons (time-series momentum, 1-12 months) and for the sign of the early-session return intraday; there is no academic support specifically for the "price above weekly/daily open" or "premium/discount" filters.

### Cited Findings
- Elder's Screen 1 can be as simple as the slope of a 13-week EMA (up-slope = only long setups) — [Commodity Charting Tools blog](https://commoditychartingtools.blogspot.com/2009/01/triple-screen-trading-system.html); [ForexOp](https://forexop.com/technical/multiple-timeframe-trade-analysis/) (secondary).
- Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012, JFE 104(2):228-250): in 58 liquid futures/forwards (equity indices, currencies, commodities, bonds), a security's own past return predicts its future return; persistence over 1-12 months that partially reverses at longer horizons — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250); [AQR data page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data). AQR reports robustness across sub-samples, look-back and holding periods; its dataset uses a 12-month lookback / 1-month hold, Jan 1985-Dec 2009 — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data).
- Intraday momentum (Gao, Han, Li & Zhou 2018, JFE 129(2):394-414): the first half-hour return of the day, measured from the previous day's close (i.e., including the overnight move), predicts the last half-hour return in SPY, 1993-2013; R-squared ~1.6%; predictability rises with volatility and volume — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); [working paper PDF (nd.edu)](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary of an earlier draft, 1999-2012 sample](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/).
- Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000) caution that identifying extrema (the basis of swing-structure definitions) has historically required analyst judgment; they replace it with kernel-regression smoothing to make pattern detection algorithmic — [MIT paper page](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/techanal.html); [NBER w7613](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w7613/w7613.pdf).

### Inferences (proposed objective alignment tests; for each timeframe TF, each test returns +1 / -1 / 0)
- MA slope: sign of EMA(N)[closed bar] - EMA(N)[k bars ago], with a dead-band (e.g., |slope| < 0.1 ATR => 0) to avoid flicker. Elder-style 13-period EMA on the bias layer is the simplest documented choice.
- Price vs MA: closed-bar close above/below EMA(N), again with an ATR-scaled buffer.
- Swing structure (defer exact swing definition to the swing-point researcher): +1 if last confirmed swing high > prior swing high AND last confirmed swing low > prior swing low; -1 for the mirror; 0 otherwise. Only use swings that are confirmed (i.e., the confirming bars have closed) to avoid look-ahead.
- Prior-period reference: +1 if price > prior period high, -1 if < prior period low, 0 if inside prior range (optionally sub-scored vs prior close).
- Period-open filter ("HTF candle direction"): +1 if price > current period open (daily/weekly), -1 if below. This is effectively the sign of the developing HTF candle. The Gao et al. result is the nearest evidence for "early-session direction persists" intraday, but it is a specific window (first 30 min from prior close -> last 30 min), not a general "above the open" rule.
- Time-series-momentum filter for the weekly cap: sign of close(now) - close(N weeks ago) on weekly bars (e.g., N = 13, 26, or 52). This is the bias definition with the strongest published support for futures, albeit for diversified, monthly-rebalanced portfolios, not single-instrument intraday entries.
- Location (premium/discount): position = (price - HTF range low) / (HTF range high - HTF range low), using the most recent completed HTF swing range or prior-week range; longs only below 0.5 ("discount") in an uptrend, shorts only above 0.5 ("premium") in a downtrend. This is an ICT/practitioner convention with no evidence found; it is coherent as a "buy pullbacks" location filter.
- Composite: Alignment score = sum of chosen tests across bias + setup layers; require bias-layer score to be non-zero and the trade direction to match it. Keep the number of tests small (2-3) to limit implicit curve fitting, since the user will not backtest.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study found that tests "price above weekly open / daily open" or "premium/discount within HTF range" as filters in futures.
- No study found that directly tests multi-timeframe alignment filters (e.g., whether requiring HTF and LTF trend agreement improves LTF signal quality). This appears to be a genuine gap in the academic literature (or at least not found in this session).

## 4. Resolving conflicts between timeframes (bias vs timing precedence)

### Takeaway
The consistent rule across frameworks: the higher timeframe has precedence for direction (bias), the lower timeframe has precedence for timing. A counter-trend move on the setup layer within an intact HTF trend is not a conflict but the setup itself (the pullback). A conflict that should block trading is when the bias layer itself is neutral/transitioning.

### Cited Findings
- Triple Screen is built on this precedence: trade only in the direction of the long-term trend, and use the intermediate oscillator to find moves against that trend (i.e., the "conflict" on the intermediate chart is the entry opportunity) — [ForexOp](https://forexop.com/technical/multiple-timeframe-trade-analysis/) (secondary).
- Attributed to Shannon: the lower timeframe should never override the higher timeframe's trend — [CMC Markets Opto (404 on fetch; low confidence)](https://cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/opto/i-trade-using-multiple-timeframes-by-brian-shannon).
- Brooks-style practitioners treat a two-legged counter-trend move (a pullback) in a trend as the entry opportunity (High 2 in bull trend, Low 2 in bear trend) — [NexusFi: Two-Legged Pullback Trading](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/two-legged-pullback-trading); [The Right Trader: two-legged pullback](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/two-legged-pullback) (secondary).
- Time-series momentum shows partial reversal beyond ~12 months — i.e., the HTF trend signal is itself horizon-limited — [EconPapers: Moskowitz et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250).

### Inferences (proposed conflict-resolution state machine)
- State A (Aligned trend): Bias layer +1, setup layer +1 => only take continuation triggers (breakout-pullback, H1 in strong trend); do not short.
- State B (HTF trend, MTF pullback): Bias +1, setup -1 => this is the primary setup state; wait for trigger-layer reversal back in the bias direction (H2, 2B of the pullback low, bullish engulfing/pin at a level, inside-bar break up).
- State C (HTF neutral/range): Bias 0 => either stand aside or switch to range rules (fade prior-day high/low with failed-breakout/2B triggers), with reduced size and targets inside the range.
- State D (HTF transition): Bias flipping (e.g., weekly structure +1 but weekly EMA slope -1) => no new swing trades; intraday scalps only in the direction of the setup layer with tight targets.
- Precedence: Bias determines allowed direction; setup determines location; trigger determines entry and initial stop; the trigger layer is never allowed to change direction permission. Exits can be managed on the setup or trigger layer.
- Define "HTF pullback depth" limits so a pullback that becomes a reversal is detected: e.g., if the setup-layer pullback closes beyond the last bias-layer confirmed swing low, bias resets to 0 (State C/D).

### Gaps
- No empirical work found comparing conflict-resolution rules. Precedence rules here are framework conventions, not tested results.

## 5. Automatable price action setups and triggers (definitions, layer fit, failure modes)

### Takeaway
Most classic setups can be coded unambiguously if (a) thresholds are fixed in advance (body/wick ratios, lookback N), (b) "trend context" is supplied by the HTF layer rather than by the pattern itself, and (c) entry is a stop order beyond the signal bar. Published definitions for Crabel's NR4/NR7/ID and Sperandeo's 2B are precise enough; Brooks' H1/H2 counts are debated even among his students, so any coded version is one interpretation.

### Cited Findings
- NR4 = day whose high-low range is the smallest of the last 4 days; NR7 = smallest of the last 7; every NR7 is also an NR4, not vice versa; inside day = high and low inside the prior day's range — [LuxAlgo: NR4/NR7 narrow-range bars](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/nr4-nr7-narrow-range-bars/) (secondary, attributing to Crabel 1990).
- ID/NR4 = inside day that is also the narrowest range of the last 4; entry via buy stop above the high (sell stop below the low for shorts); the bar is neutral until price breaks one side — [Trading Setups Review: Inside Day NR4](https://tradingsetupsreview.com/inside-daynr4) (secondary).
- Crabel's own opening range breakout articles appeared as a multi-part series in Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities, vol. 7 (1989) — [Traders.com store: ORB Part 5](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c04-orb-pdf.html); [ORB Part 6](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c05-orb-pdf.html) (primary articles exist; not read in this session).
- A table attributed to Kaufman reportedly shows ORB win rates improved with an inside-day filter in bonds and soybeans but got worse in the S&P 500 (68% -> 61%) — [Learning-TA: Opening Range Breakout](https://learning-ta.pages.dev/learn/opening-range-breakout) (secondhand, unverified; flag as anecdotal).
- Sperandeo's 2B: price makes a new high (low) beyond the prior swing high (low), fails to follow through, and falls back below (above) the prior extreme — a reversal formation of two adjacent highs/lows; it is distinct from his 1-2-3 trendline-break reversal, in which a short-term exceeding of point 1 (a false breakout) must be followed by a strong pullback — [RoboForex: Trading like Sperandeo: 1-2-3 Reversal and 2B](https://roboforex.com/blog/education/trading-like-sperandeo-1-2-3-reversal-and-2b-pattern/) (secondary); original is Victor Sperandeo, *Trader Vic: Methods of a Wall Street Master* (book; not read in this session).
- Brooks' High 1 / High 2: H1 is the first attempt to resume an uptrend after a pullback begins; H2 is the second attempt after a two-legged pullback; L1/L2 are the bear mirror. Entry is a stop above the signal bar after leg two, stop beyond the second leg — [NexusFi: Two-Legged Pullback Trading](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/two-legged-pullback-trading); [The Right Trader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/two-legged-pullback) (secondary).
- A simpler bar-based H1 rule: during a pullback, a bar whose high goes above the prior bar's high = H1; the second occurrence in the same pullback = H2 — [Elite Trader thread](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/al-brooks-method.296854/post-5816507); [Scribd: Brooks setups sheet](https://www.scribd.com/doc/305118912/Brooks-Setups) (secondary; note the Elite Trader post phrases it as "closes above", the setup sheet as "high above previous bar" — conflicting).
- Labeling of H1/H2 is debated even on Brooks' own course forum — [Brooks Trading Course forum: High 1-2 / Low 1-2](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/high-12-low-12/). An open-source coded interpretation exists — [TradingView: Al Brooks Second Entry](https://www.tradingview.com/script/YUixSlPL-Al-Brooks-Second-Entry/).
- Bulkowski statistics (US stocks, daily bars): bullish engulfing reverses 63% of the time, ranked 22 of 103 candle patterns, frequency rank 12 — [ThePatternSite: Bullish Engulfing](https://thepatternsite.com/BullEngulfing.html); bearish engulfing ~79% reversal rate, but the subsequent move "runs out of steam almost immediately" — [StockCharts Insider (citing Bulkowski)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/stockcharts-insider-every-candlestick-scan-in-the-library-ranked-and-why-the-popular-ones-may-fall-short/); [ThePatternSite: Candle reversal rankings](https://www.thepatternsite.com/CandleReverse.html). A "reversal" in Bulkowski's sense is about direction of the next breakout, not profitability, and the data are equities, not futures.

### Inferences (proposed coded definitions; thresholds are conventions to fix in advance, not optimized values)
Notation: O,H,L,C of the closed signal bar; R = H - L; Body = |C - O|; UW = H - max(O,C); LW = min(O,C) - L; ATR = ATR(14) of the same layer.

- Pin bar / hammer (bullish): LW >= 2 x Body AND LW >= 0.6 R AND UW <= 0.25 R AND R >= 0.8 ATR (to exclude tiny bars) AND L <= a reference level (+/- buffer) or L is the lowest low of the last N bars. Shooting star / bearish pin = mirror. The "lower shadow >= 2x body, little or no upper shadow" convention is the commonly cited Nison-style hammer definition (not verified against Nison in this session). Layer: setup layer (pattern at HTF level), entered on trigger layer via stop above the pin high or a trigger-layer break. Failure modes: pins inside ranges (no level), pins against HTF bias, and in futures, pins on time bars that span the RTH open/close or news minute.
- Engulfing (bullish): prior bar bearish (C1 < O1), current bar bullish, Body_current > Body_prior AND real body engulfs prior real body (O <= C1 and C >= O1; for continuous futures with no gaps, use >= / <= with tick tolerance). Stricter variant: current range engulfs prior range. Layer: setup or trigger. Failure mode: Bulkowski's note that the bearish engulfing move fades fast suggests using it as a trigger with a near target, not as a swing signal.
- Inside bar: H < H1 AND L > L1 (strict) or <= / >= (inclusive; choose one). Inside-bar breakout: buy stop 1 tick above H (or above mother-bar high, a stricter variant), sell stop 1 tick below L; trade only the side matching bias, or OCO both sides in State C. Failure mode: false break then reversal through the opposite side (which is itself the "failed inside-bar breakout" trap setup). "ii" (two consecutive inside bars) = tighter compression.
- Outside bar: H > H1 AND L < L1. Direction = close location (close in top/bottom third of range). Layer: setup; often marks a failed breakout on one side. Failure: in-range outside bars are noise.
- NR4 / NR7 / ID-NR4 (Crabel): R = min(R over last 4 or 7 closed bars) [+ inside condition]. Entry: stop orders 1 tick beyond the NR bar's high/low (or beyond the next session's opening range). Layer: daily (classic) or applied to the setup layer intraday (NR7 on 15m/60m). Failure modes: whipsaw both sides; low-range days preceding scheduled news (CPI/FOMC/EIA for CL).
- Opening range breakout: ORH/ORL = high/low of first X minutes of RTH (e.g., 5, 15, 30; Crabel used short ranges). Long on break of ORH by k ticks (or k% of average OR), short on ORL; optional filter = only in bias direction and/or only after NR/ID prior day. Futures nuance: define "open" as RTH 09:30 ET for ES/NQ (or 09:00 ET for CL pit-session convention) vs Globex 18:00 ET; results will differ. Failure modes: OR breaks that immediately fail (the trap), low-volatility days, and very wide ORs (cap OR width vs ATR).
- Breakout-pullback (breakout retest): (1) a closed setup-layer bar closes beyond a level (prior swing high, PDH, ORH, range high) by >= k ATR; (2) within M bars, price pulls back to within j ATR of the level without closing back inside by more than j ATR; (3) trigger = trigger-layer bar breaking back in breakout direction (H1/H2 style). Stop beyond pullback extreme. Failure: deep pullback that closes back inside = failed breakout.
- Failed breakout / trap / 2B: (1) price trades beyond a reference extreme (prior swing high, PDH/PWH, ORH) by >= 1 tick; (2) within M bars (e.g., 1-3 on the setup layer) a bar closes back inside the prior extreme; (3) entry = stop below the low of the closing-back-inside bar (for a bearish 2B), stop = 1 tick above the false-break high. The M-bar window must be fixed (Sperandeo's 2B is typically a quick failure). Microstructure rationale: stops cluster just beyond widely watched levels (see Osler, Section 7), so a run through them that fails is a plausible "trap." Failure: genuine breakouts that retest; trading 2B against a strong HTF trend.
- H1/H2 (and L1/L2) as an algorithm: In a bias-layer uptrend, a pullback starts when a setup-layer bar makes a lower high than the prior bar. Count +1 each time a bar's high exceeds the prior bar's high while the pullback is active (one interpretation), or count completed down-legs (two-leg interpretation; requires a swing-leg definition from the swing-point researcher). Signal bar = the bar that creates the H2; entry = buy stop 1 tick above its high; stop = 1 tick below the pullback low. Reset the count if price makes a new trend high or if the pullback breaks the bias-layer swing low. Since definitions conflict, pick one and document it for visual review.
- Wedge / three-push: three successive swing extremes in the same direction (e.g., three higher highs) where each push's extension (distance beyond prior push) shrinks, optionally with a converging trendline through the lows; trigger = break of the most recent minor swing low after the third push (a 2B of the third push is a stricter version). Requires the swing-point definition; risky to automate because "push" depends on swing granularity.
- Prior day/week high-low interactions: treat PDH/PDL/PWH/PWL as the reference extremes in either breakout-pullback (trend days, aligned bias) or failed-breakout/2B (range regimes, State C). The bias state decides which of the two opposing setups is eligible, which avoids taking both.

### Gaps
- No primary-source statistics found for pin bar ratios; ratio thresholds above are conventions, not evidence.
- Brooks' own exact definitions (glossary page returned 404) and any published win rates for H2/L2 were not found; secondary sources say no reliable published win rate exists ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/two-legged-pullback-trading)).
- Crabel's own ORB rules and statistics (book long out of print) were not read; only secondary definitions.
- A commentary in search results claimed Sperandeo intended 2B for shorter-term reversals rather than full trend changes; the specific source could not be confirmed.

## 6. The higher-timeframe candle as a lens; "Power of Three" (AMD) and "Candle Range Theory" (critical view)

### Takeaway
Monitoring the developing HTF candle (its open, current range, and which side of the open price is on) is a coherent, codable lens; it is mathematically equivalent to tracking the sign and shape of the current daily/weekly bar. ICT's "Power of Three" (accumulation-manipulation-distribution) is largely a narrative description of candle anatomy (open, opposite-side wick, close), with inconsistent timing definitions across sources and no evidence of predictive power found. "Candle Range Theory" returned no citable sources.

### Cited Findings
- PO3 as described by proponents: a period consolidates around its open (accumulation), runs stops on the wrong side of the open (manipulation), then expands in the "true" direction into the close (distribution) — [FXOpen: ICT Power of 3 explained](https://fxopen.com/blog/en/what-is-ict-po3-and-how-do-traders-use-it/); [MQL5 blog: ICT PO3 guide](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/771729) (proponent/vendor content).
- Each HTF candle (daily, 4h) is read as that three-stage sequence; the pattern may also be spread across 2-3 candles — [LuxAlgo: Accumulation-Manipulation-Distribution](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/accumulation-manipulation-distribution/); [TradingView PO3 ideas](https://www.tradingview.com/ideas/powerofthree/).
- Timing definitions conflict: one indicator sets accumulation 19:00-01:00 EST, manipulation 01:00-07:00, distribution 07:00-13:00 — [LuxAlgo/TradingFinder PO3 indicator](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/5thZ7SVC-power-of-3-ict-01-amd-ict-smc-accumulations/); others map Asia = accumulation, NY open = manipulation, NY session = distribution — [FXOpen](https://fxopen.com/blog/en/what-is-ict-po3-and-how-do-traders-use-it/).
- Even a vendor teaching AMD answers "No" to whether every trading day follows the sequence — [LuxAlgo AMD concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/accumulation-manipulation-distribution/).
- Closest rigorous evidence for "the day's early direction tends to carry into the close": Gao et al. (2018) — first-half-hour return (from prior close) predicts last-half-hour return in SPY, R-squared ~1.6% — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414). Note this is continuation of the early move, which is the opposite of PO3's "early move is the fake-out" claim.

### Inferences
- Critical point: any bullish daily candle with a lower wick has, by construction, traded below its open and then closed above it. PO3 therefore "explains" every such candle after the fact; as usually stated it is not falsifiable. A testable/automatable version would be conditional: "if by time T price has traded at least X below the session open and then reclaims the open, the probability of closing above the open exceeds the unconditional rate." That is essentially a failed-breakdown (2B) of the open, which can be coded with the Section 5 failed-breakout rule using the period open as the reference level.
- Gao et al.'s continuation result and PO3's reversal claim point in opposite directions for the opening move. The user should not adopt PO3's "first move is manipulation" as a default bias without evidence.
- A codable "HTF candle lens" (proposal): for the current daily and weekly candle, compute on every trigger-layer bar: (a) side of open (sign of price - open), (b) % of current HTF range position, (c) whether the prior period's high/low has been taken, and (d) expected remaining range = ATR(HTF) - current HTF range. Use (d) as a target/no-trade filter (e.g., do not initiate a with-trend scalp when the daily range already exceeds ~1.2x daily ATR). These are reasonable risk filters, not proven edges.
- This lens uses the developing (unclosed) HTF bar intentionally; in code it must be computed from LTF data (session open, running high/low) rather than from an HTF series' current bar, to keep historical and real-time behavior identical (see Section 9).

### Gaps
- "Candle Range Theory" (CRT): no citable sources returned. No independent tests of PO3/AMD found.

## 7. Key reference levels across timeframes (prior day/week H/L/C, overnight H/L, weekly/monthly open, initial balance) and evidence on their significance

### Takeaway
Practitioner consensus treats prior-period highs/lows/closes, overnight extremes, period opens and the initial balance as the core reference levels. The strongest rigorous evidence that widely watched levels matter comes from FX: published support/resistance levels interrupt intraday trends more often than random levels, plausibly because stop and limit orders cluster there (Osler). Futures-specific peer-reviewed evidence for PDH/PDL etc. was not found; academic opinion on S/R is described as unsettled.

### Cited Findings
- Osler (2000, FRBNY Economic Policy Review, "Support for Resistance: Technical Analysis and Intraday Exchange Rates"): S/R levels published by six FX firms predicted intraday trend interruptions better than randomly chosen artificial levels, using 1-minute quotes during NY hours; predictive power varied across currencies and firms — [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.html); [PDF](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf).
- Osler (2001, FRBNY Staff Report 125, "Currency Orders and Exchange-Rate Dynamics: Explaining the Success of Technical Analysis"): clustering of stop-loss and take-profit orders (including at round numbers) provides a microstructure explanation for why such levels work — [New York Fed Staff Report sr125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.html).
- Chung & Bellotti (arXiv 2101.07410): no consensus in the academic community on whether support/resistance has predictive value; both sides have "equally numerous and convincing" evidence — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410v1).
- NinjaTrader (broker/platform educational content): prior-day levels are common intraday reference points; confluence of levels from different methods (prior-day high, pivot, VWAP band) is often treated as stronger S/R — [NinjaTrader blog](https://ninjatrader.com/Futures/Blogs/How-to-Identify-Intraday-Support-and-Resistance-Levels-in-Your-Trading) (practitioner description, not a test).
- Edgeful (vendor) claims ~67% of the time when YM breaks the previous day's high, the session closes green — [Edgeful blog](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/previous-days-range-trading-mistake) (vendor statistic; no published sample, method or significance test; UNVERIFIED).
- Gao et al. (2018) define the first half-hour return from the previous close, implicitly treating the prior close as a reference for the overnight + opening move — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414).

### Inferences
- Osler's mechanism (order clustering at watched levels) is the best theoretical justification both for breakout-continuation through a level (stops triggered => momentum) and for failed-break reversals (stops run, liquidity absorbed). Which dominates is regime-dependent, which is why Section 4's bias state should pick one.
- For CME futures, define levels unambiguously in code: "prior day" = prior full Globex session (18:00-17:00 ET) or prior RTH session (09:30-16:00 ET for ES/NQ); overnight (ON) high/low = Globex session from 18:00 ET to RTH open; "weekly open" = Sunday 18:00 ET Globex open; initial balance (IB) = first 60 minutes of RTH (Market Profile convention). These session times should be verified against current CME hours and the user's data feed's session template (Rithmic/Tradovate session templates differ by platform).
- Level significance probably decays with distance and age: prior day and prior week extremes are the most watched; levels older than the weekly cap can still be computed from daily data but should be few.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed futures-specific study found on PDH/PDL/ONH/ONL, weekly open, or IB significance. No evidence found for initial balance statistics beyond practitioner lore (Market Profile literature, e.g., Dalton, was not retrieved).

## 8. Evidence base: candlestick predictability, trend persistence, intraday momentum, MTF filtering

### Takeaway
Honest summary: (1) Candlestick patterns, tested mechanically as stand-alone signals in large liquid US stocks, mostly do not survive rigorous (bootstrap) testing; positive results come from older samples, smaller/emerging markets, or new data-mined patterns. (2) Trend persistence is robust at 1-12 month horizons in futures (TSMOM), and intraday the early-session return modestly predicts the late-session return. (3) There is essentially no rigorous evidence on multi-timeframe filtering per se. The defensible design is: use the robust evidence (trend persistence) for the bias layer, and treat candlestick/price-action triggers as execution/risk tools (they define a stop and an entry), not as the source of edge.

### Cited Findings
- Marshall, Young & Rose: 28 candlestick patterns on DJIA stocks, 1992-2002, trades entered at the close the day after the signal and held 10 days; under normality some patterns looked significant, but bootstrap tests (described as the more appropriate method) found no evidence that any signal predicts price; "neither bullish nor bearish candlestick signals reliably generate abnormal returns in the expected direction"; robust to next-day-open entry and subperiods — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/candlesticks-fiddlesticks/). Published version: "Candlestick technical trading strategies: Can they create value for investors?", Journal of Banking & Finance 30(8):2303-2323 (2006) — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:30:y:2006:i:8:p:2303-2323). (Note: CXO's summary refers to the SSRN working paper "Market Timing with Candlestick Technical Analysis"; I could not retrieve the JBF abstract to confirm the two are identical, though designs described match the 28-pattern DJIA study.) Critique (Greg Morris): mechanical tests ignore the short-term trend context required to identify patterns — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/candlesticks-fiddlesticks/).
- Caginalp & Laurent (1998, Applied Mathematical Finance 5(3-4):181-205): daily OHLC for S&P 500 stocks, 1992-1996; standard definitions of three-day reversal patterns, non-parametric test; out-of-sample significance reported at ~36 standard deviations and profit of almost 1% over a two-day holding period — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:apmtfi:v:5:y:1998:i:3-4:p:181-205). Caveat: short, older sample; I did not verify transaction-cost treatment.
- A study of German index futures tested 19 candlestick patterns and found negative results (reported in a literature review) — [IJEFI review article (econjournals.com)](https://econjournals.com/index.php/ijefi/article/download/9298/pdf/23008) (secondary citation; original paper not retrieved; likely Fock, Klein & Zwergel 2005 but unconfirmed).
- Lu, Shiu & Liu (2012, Review of Financial Economics 21(2):63-68): two-day bullish/bearish patterns on Taiwan Top 50 stocks, Oct 2002-Dec 2008, with out-of-sample and bootstrap tests; three bullish reversal patterns were profitable — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:revfin:v:21:y:2012:i:2:p:63-68); [NCCU repository](https://irtest.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/61660). Lu & Shiu (2012, Emerging Markets Finance and Trade): existing two-day patterns not profitable, two new patterns were — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:mes:emfitr:v:48:y:2012:i:0:p:41-57). Lu (2014, Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 26:65-78): four single-line patterns profitable after transaction costs, with bootstrap checks — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:pacfin:v:26:y:2014:i:c:p:65-78).
- Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000, Journal of Finance 55(4):1705-1765): kernel-regression pattern recognition on US stocks 1962-1996; conditional return distributions after several patterns (e.g., head-and-shoulders, double bottoms) differ from unconditional ones, so some patterns "provide incremental information" — this is distributional information, not a demonstrated trading profit — [MIT paper page](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/techanal.html); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:55:y:2000:i:4:p:1705-1765).
- Bulkowski (practitioner, large-sample but non-academic, equities): bullish engulfing 63% reversal; bearish engulfing ~79% reversal but follow-through fades quickly — [ThePatternSite](https://thepatternsite.com/BullEngulfing.html); [StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/stockcharts-insider-every-candlestick-scan-in-the-library-ranked-and-why-the-popular-ones-may-fall-short/).
- Time-series momentum: 1-12 month persistence across 58 futures/forwards, partial reversal at longer horizons; robust across lookbacks/holding periods — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data).
- Intraday momentum: first half-hour (from prior close) predicts last half-hour in SPY 1993-2013, R-squared 1.6%, stronger with volatility and volume — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414); explanations offered include investors rebalancing in the first and last half-hours and late-informed traders acting on morning information — [nd.edu working paper](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf).
- Osler (2000): published S/R levels beat random levels at predicting intraday trend interruptions in FX — [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.html).

### Inferences
- The pattern of results (fails in large liquid US stocks under bootstrap; works in older or less efficient samples or with newly mined patterns) is what one would expect if candlestick edges are small, regime-dependent, and partly data-mined. ES/NQ are among the most liquid markets in the world, so stand-alone candlestick edges should be presumed absent there.
- Marshall et al.'s tests were context-free (no trend filter). That is precisely the gap the MTF approach fills (bias from HTF trend, pattern only at a level). This is a hypothesis, not evidence: no rigorous study found tests candlesticks conditioned on HTF trend + level.
- For a user who will not backtest, the most evidence-aligned design is: bias = simple trend/momentum on the highest layers (weekly/daily, consistent with TSMOM), intraday direction informed by the opening move (Gao et al.), location = widely watched levels (Osler mechanism), trigger = a simple stop-entry pattern whose main job is defining risk.
- Gao et al. is a timing-window effect in SPY; ES futures track SPY closely, so the effect plausibly transfers, but this was not verified for futures in this session.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study found specifically on multi-timeframe filtering (e.g., "LTF signal only in HTF trend direction") in any market.
- No rigorous intraday candlestick study on CME index or crude futures retrieved; a Duvinage, Mazza & Petitjean intraday DJIA candlestick paper appeared in search (UCLouvain repository) but was not read.
- The often-cited "12 of 16 markets" out-of-sample confirmation of intraday momentum appeared in search summaries, but the specific paper could not be confirmed, so it is not cited.

## 9. Automation specifics: synchronizing multiple timeframes without look-ahead; platform handling

### Takeaway
Rule of thumb: decisions use only closed HTF bars (offset by one), or explicitly compute the developing HTF bar from LTF data. TradingView's official fix is `request.security(..., expr[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)`; NinjaTrader adds series via `AddDataSeries` in `State.Configure`, routes events by `BarsInProgress`, processes the primary series first on shared timestamps, and treats a bar as closed only when the next bar's first tick arrives; Sierra Chart generally requires a separate chart per timeframe and references it via Study/Price Overlay or chart references, with reported synchronization limits.

### Cited Findings
TradingView (official Pine Script docs, "Repainting"):
- On historical bars, `request.security()` returns only confirmed values from the requested context; on realtime bars it can return unconfirmed values that later change, so the script "will repaint them when it restarts its execution" — [TradingView Pine docs: Repainting](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/).
- Recommended non-repainting HTF request: `request.security(symbol, timeframe, expression[1], lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on)`; "Neither can be removed without compromising the integrity of the function" — [TradingView Pine docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/).
- `lookahead_on` without the `[1]` offset "will return data from the future on historical bars, which is dangerously misleading" — [TradingView Pine docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/).
- Strategies with `calc_on_every_tick = true` run on every realtime update but historical bars are processed on close, so they "will most probably not generate the same order executions" (repaint); `barstate.isnew` usage repaints while `barstate.isconfirmed` can avoid it; `timenow`-based logic necessarily repaints; realtime intrabar (lower-TF) logic that relies on detecting the first intrabar only works historically — [TradingView Pine docs](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/repainting/).
- Community guidance also recommends blocking requests for timeframes equal to or lower than the chart's (using `timeframe.in_seconds()` checks) — [TradingView: Higher timeframe requests (publication)](https://www.tradingview.com/script/W1YpYcOI-Higher-timeframe-requests/).

NinjaTrader 8 (official help guide):
- `AddDataSeries()` "should ONLY be called from the OnStateChange() method during State.Configure"; arguments should be hardcoded, and adding a series dynamically "is NOT guaranteed" — [NT8 Help: Multi-Time Frame & Instruments](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).
- `BarsInProgress` identifies which Bars object called `OnBarUpdate()`; index 0 = primary, added series follow in order; other series accessible via `Closes[1][0]`, `BarsArray[n]` — [NT8 Help: BarsInProgress](https://ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/barsinprogress.htm); [NT8 Help: Multi-Time Frame](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).
- "In circumstances where multiple bars share the same exact timestamps, your primary bars series will always be processed first, followed by the secondary bars series" — [NT8 Help](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).
- With `Calculate.OnBarClose`, "Bars are not considered closed until the first tick of the following bar comes in"; with `OnEachTick`/`OnPriceChange`, the forming bar is used and "the OHLCV data you access in real-time is different than on historical data"; real-time event order "is NOT guaranteed to be the same sequence" as historical unless Tick Replay is enabled — [NT8 Help](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).
- Check `CurrentBars[n]` for every series before processing ("the CurrentBars starting value will be -1 until all series have processed the first bar"); for backtests, submit orders to the most granular series because orders submitted in a higher-timeframe context can delay fills — [NT8 Help](https://static.ninjatrader.com/support/helpGuides/nt8/multi-time_frame__instruments.htm).
- 2016 NT8 beta forum reports: with `OnBarClose` and an added daily series, users saw the primary series not driving `OnBarUpdate` in real time as expected; a workaround was adding a third series matching the primary; no confirmed resolution in thread (OLD; verify in current build) — [NinjaTrader forum thread 91294](https://forum.ninjatrader.com/forum/historical-beta-archive/version-8-beta/91294-adddataseries-and-onbarupdate).

Sierra Chart (support board; official docs not retrieved):
- Referencing another timeframe typically means opening a separate source chart at that timeframe and pulling its study/price into the destination chart with the Study/Price Overlay study; Sierra Chart engineering stated in 2014 that "a separate chart is absolutely required" to reference a different period; users report overlay data is effectively "static" and comparisons with the overlay's previous bar may not evaluate as expected, and studies built on overlaid data can drift out of sync — [Sierra Chart Support Board thread 12439](https://www.sierrachart.com/SupportBoard.php?ThreadID=12439); [thread 24016](https://www.sierrachart.com/SupportBoard.php?ThreadID=24016); [thread 75318](https://www.sierrachart.com/SupportBoard.php?ThreadID=75318) (forum content; which thread contains which quote was not individually verified; check Sierra's official "Study/Price Overlay" and "Referencing Other Charts" docs).

### Inferences (implementation rules)
- Closed-bar rule: every HTF value used in a decision must come from the last completed HTF bar. In NT8 with `OnBarClose`, note the primary-first ordering: at 10:00 on a 5m primary, the 60m bar closing at 10:00 has not yet been processed, so `Closes[1][0]` still reflects the 09:00 bar. This is conservative (no look-ahead) but introduces a one-HTF-bar lag at boundaries; acceptable, but it must be consistent historically and live. To use the just-closed 60m value at 10:00, act in the `BarsInProgress == 1` event instead.
- Developing HTF bar rule: compute developing daily/weekly O/H/L (session open, running high/low) from the trigger series itself (or from a 1-minute series) using explicit session boundaries, rather than reading the HTF series' current bar. This keeps historical and live behavior identical and is the clean way to implement the "HTF candle lens" (Section 6).
- Session template alignment: daily bars for CME futures depend on the platform's session template (Globex 17:00 ET close vs RTH); weekly bars depend on week-start convention. Hardcode and document the template; mismatches between Rithmic/Tradovate feeds and the charting platform are a common source of "levels don't match" bugs.
- Swing confirmation lag: a swing high on an HTF is only known after its confirming bars close (e.g., N bars to the right). Plotting swings at the pivot bar is fine visually, but signals must reference them only from the confirmation bar onward — otherwise the chart will look better in review than the strategy behaved live. This is the single most common look-ahead error in visually reviewed price action systems.
- Entry orders: place stop-entry orders on the most granular series (NT8 guidance) and evaluate triggers on closed trigger-layer bars; intrabar (OnEachTick) triggers will differ between historical display and live behavior unless Tick Replay (NT8) or bar magnifier/intrabar handling (TradingView) is used.
- Visual-review aid: plot each layer's state (bias +1/0/-1, setup active, trigger level) as separate indicator rows so the user can audit every proposal without backtesting.

### Gaps
- Sierra Chart's official documentation for Study/Price Overlay, "Referencing Other Charts," and ACSIL multi-chart access (`sc.GetChartBaseData`, etc.) was not retrieved; current behavior should be verified.
- Tradovate's native platform and Rithmic R|Trader do not appear to offer multi-timeframe automated strategy scripting on their own (they are typically connected to NinjaTrader, Sierra Chart, TradingView, etc.); this was not verified in this session.
