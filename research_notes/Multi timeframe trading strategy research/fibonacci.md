# Fibonacci Retracements, Extensions and Projections (incl. Multi-Timeframe Confluence and Automated Anchoring)

Scope note for the report writer: this file covers Fibonacci as its own dimension. Fib depends entirely on swing-point definitions (covered by the swing-theory researcher) and works best in confluence with volume nodes (covered by the volume-profile researcher); those dependencies are flagged inline. Research date: November 2026. Most practitioner sources are secondary (vendor/education sites); primary books (Boroden, DiNapoli, Miner, Fischer, Carney, Brown) were not available online in full and are cited only where a reachable source quotes or summarises them. Claims that rest only on backtests or vendor statistics are flagged.

## 1. Levels and their origins (retracements, extensions, projections; "true" Fibonacci vs convention; terminology)

### Takeaway
Only a handful of commonly used levels are actual powers/roots of the golden ratio (0.236, 0.382, 0.618, 0.786, 0.886, 1.272, 1.618, 2.618, 4.236); 50% is a convention, 1.414 is the square root of 2, and ICT's 0.62/0.705/0.79 and the "golden pocket" 0.65 are rounded or arbitrary conventions. Terminology splits into two families: "retracement" (fraction of the last leg measured back from its end), "extension" (ratio of the same leg beyond its origin, e.g. 1.272/1.618 of a correction), and "projection/expansion" (ratio of a prior leg A–B added to a new point C, DiNapoli's COP/OP/XOP, AB=CD).

### Cited Findings
- Standard retracement grid described across practitioner sources: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%; extensions/projections commonly 127.2% and 161.8% — [LuxAlgo Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- A typical academic description of the tool (Pakistan study) divides the vertical distance into 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 100% — [Shaker, Asad & Zulfiqar 2018, IJEFR](https://arpgweb.com/pdf-files/ijefr4(6)159-164.pdf)
- DiNapoli uses only two retracements, 38.2% ("F38") and 61.8% ("F61"), computed per reaction: F38 = focus − 0.382 × (focus − reaction); F61 = focus − 0.618 × (focus − reaction), where the "focus number" is the extreme of the move being retraced and the "reaction" is the pullback swing it is measured against — [LuxAlgo DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/dinapoli-levels.md)
- DiNapoli "objective points" (profit-target projections): COP (Contracted Objective Point) = C + 0.618 × (B − A); OP (Objective Point) = C + 1.000 × (B − A); XOP (Expanded Objective Point) = C + 1.618 × (B − A), where A–B is the initial thrust and C is the end of the reaction — [LuxAlgo DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/dinapoli-levels.md); CQG ships this as the "Expansion (DNExp)" study (its help page misspells COP as "Contacted") — [CQG Expansion (DNExp)](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/expansiondnexp.htm)
- Some sources add an SXOP at 2.618 and describe the COP/OP/XOP set as sufficient for take-profit placement with partial exits at each level — [ForTraders: Two Types of Fibonacci Extensions](https://fortraders.org/en/indicators/two-types-fibonacci-extensions.html); [TradingView DiNapoli Objective Points script](https://fr.tradingview.com/script/NzIARSQk-Dinapoli-Objective-Points-OPs)
- Sources disagree on where the DiNapoli projection is anchored: LuxAlgo projects from pivot C (end of reaction), while other descriptions measure "from the end of a corrective wave B" — users must check which convention their platform uses — [LuxAlgo DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/dinapoli-levels/); [CQG DiNapoli Studies](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/dinapolistudies.htm)
- Boroden distinguishes retracements (swing high-to-low or low-to-high), "internal price extensions" of the prior corrective move (her first Type-1 objective is "the 1.272 extension of the prior swing"), and projections of prior corrective swings ("symmetry"); if limited to one ratio she would keep .618 — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Carolyn Boroden on Fibonacci Price Clusters](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- ICT's "Optimal Trade Entry" uses 0.62, 0.705 and 0.79 as its band, where classical Fib tools use 0.618 and 0.786; 0.705 is simply the midpoint (62 + 79)/2 — [Equiti: OTE vs Fibonacci](https://www.equiti.com/jo-en/news/trading-ideas/ote-vs-fibonacci-ict-trading/); [LuxAlgo Optimal Trade Entry](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry.md)
- The "golden pocket" is defined as the band between the 0.618 and 0.65 retracement; the LuxAlgo implementation offers no rationale for 0.65 (user-adjustable default) — [LuxAlgo Golden Pocket indicator](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/golden-pocket/)
- Harmonic ratios (Carney/Pesavento) include 0.886, 1.13, 1.27, 1.618, 2.24, 2.618, 3.618; Gartley's original 1935 pattern reportedly used thirds (1/3, 2/3), with Fibonacci ratios assigned later by Carney and Pesavento (secondary claim, not verified against Gartley's book) — [LuxAlgo Harmonic Patterns](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/harmonic-patterns.md); [Admiral Markets: Harmonic Trading Patterns](https://admiralmarkets.com/education/articles/forex-strategy/harmonic-trading-patterns)

### Inferences
- Computed (arithmetic, φ = 1.6180340): 1/φ = 0.618034; 1/φ² = 0.381966; 1/φ³ = 0.236068; √(1/φ) = 0.786151 (so 0.786 = √0.618); φ^(−1/4) = 0.886652 (so 0.886 = √0.786); √φ = 1.272020 (= 1/0.786); 1/0.886 = 1.128 (the harmonic "1.13"); φ² = 2.618034; φ³ = 4.236068; √5 = 2.236 (the harmonic "2.24"); φ + 2 = 3.618. By contrast 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 are simple fractions/multiples; 1.414 = √2 and 0.707 = 1/√2 are not φ-derived; 0.65 and ICT's 0.62/0.705/0.79 are rounded conventions.
- Because the retracement grid between 23.6% and 78.6% has a level roughly every 12–15% of the leg (and up to 7 levels when 88.6% is included), almost any pullback ends "near a Fib level" — this matters for the evidence section (see §6).
- Terminology to standardise in the final report: (a) Retracement R(x) = End − x·(End − Start) for x < 1; (b) Extension E(x) = End − x·(End − Start) for x > 1 (beyond the origin of the leg being retraced; Boroden's "1.272 / 1.618 extension"); (c) Projection/expansion P(x) = C ± x·|B − A| (AB=CD at x = 1.0; DiNapoli COP/OP/XOP at 0.618/1.0/1.618; Boroden's "symmetry" when a prior corrective leg is projected from a new swing).
- For automation, the "true vs conventional" distinction is irrelevant to execution but relevant to honesty: there is no mathematical reason markets should respect φ ratios; any effect would be behavioural (self-fulfilling order placement) — see §6.

## 2. Anchoring rules — choosing the swing, and making anchors objective for automation

### Takeaway
Fibonacci output is only as objective as the swing detector feeding it; every automated Fib tool reviewed is a ZigZag/pivot detector (depth in bars + deviation threshold, Donchian rule, or ATR-multiple reversal) followed by a grid on the latest confirmed leg. To automate, fix (1) the swing definition (N-bar pivot or ATR-multiple reversal) per timeframe, (2) wick vs close anchoring, (3) re-anchor and invalidation rules, and (4) treat levels as zones.

### Cited Findings
- TradingView community "Fib Retracement (AUTO)" uses a ZigZag to find significant highs/lows; "Deviation" controls pivot sensitivity (higher = fewer pivots) and "Depth" is the minimum number of bars between pivots; it has a reverse option and optional extensions below 0% — [TradingView: Fib Retracement (AUTO)](https://www.tradingview.com/script/HdVXYTjV-Fib-Retracement-AUTO)
- "Auto Fib Retracement [Syafiq.Jr]" identifies a new pivot when deviation exceeds a threshold, then draws between the previous and current pivot — [TradingView: Auto Fib Retracement Syafiq.Jr](https://in.tradingview.com/script/b0LuOg1X-Auto-Fib-Retracement-Syafiq-Jr)
- "Cap's Dual Auto Fib Retracement" uses ZigZag pivots with default deviation 2 and depth 10, and advises reducing depth if no pivots are detected — [TradingView: Cap's Dual Auto Fib](https://in.tradingview.com/script/4aTOhLkL-Cap-s-Dual-Auto-Fib-Retracement)
- "Martyv Auto Fib Retracement" states it is a modification of TradingView's out-of-the-box Auto Fib (adding log-scale support); its release notes change deviation/depth and lookback settings across versions — [TradingView: Martyv Auto Fib with Logarithmic Support](https://in.tradingview.com/script/OEcKDPrO-Martyv-Auto-Fib-Retracement-with-Logarithmic-Support)
- "Auto Structure Fibonacci" confirms a swing high only once price has fallen from it by ATR × multiplier (default ATR 14 × 3.0) — [TradingView: Auto Structure Fibonacci](https://www.tradingview.com/script/VqR24zzI-Auto-Structure-Fibonacci/)
- "Auto Fib Retracement [victhoreb]" exposes a configurable ZigZag and an explicit repainting option — [TradingView: Auto Fib Retracement victhoreb](https://in.tradingview.com/script/Pv9yc2mz-Auto-Fib-Retracement-victhoreb)
- A ProRealTime community indicator finds the latest leg with a Donchian-style ZigZag and draws Fib levels on it — [ProRealCode: Automatic Fibonacci levels on the latest swing](https://www.prorealcode.com/prorealtime-indicators/automatic-fibonacci-levels-on-the-latest-swing/)
- LuxAlgo's Golden Pocket tool confirms swings with "Swing Length" (default 20 bars each side of the pivot); larger values anchor to slower, more significant legs that appear later; smaller values track noisier anchors; invalidation at the 0.786 retracement or the swing origin; zone tests register on wicks but breaks/invalidations only on closes — [LuxAlgo Golden Pocket](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/golden-pocket/)
- LuxAlgo cluster guidance: each draw should use its own genuine pivots; retracements apply to corrective legs, extensions/projections to impulse legs; practical selection is the last two or three significant legs on the trading timeframe plus the dominant leg one timeframe up — [LuxAlgo Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- The academic test by Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos built an algorithm to identify Fibonacci support/resistance levels automatically and construct zones around them, explicitly to reduce the subjectivity of hand-drawn levels — [RiuNet record](https://riunet.upv.es/handle/10251/195083?show=full); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421012495)
- Forexop's large FX test defined swings recursively: start with the largest high-to-low wave, split by its largest counter-move, and recurse until waves fall below per-pair minimum sizes (e.g., EURUSD 100 pips with 15-pip minimum correction) — a fully objective, non-repainting (hindsight) segmentation — [Forexop: Fibonacci Retracement: A Myth or Reality?](https://forexop.com/?p=7920)
- Boroden draws retracements from swing highs to lows (resistance) or lows to highs (support) and uses 2- or 5-minute charts for trend, 15/60-minute and daily for higher-timeframe clusters — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- In Elliott Wave usage, a hard rule is that wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of wave 1 (i.e., a >100% retracement invalidates the count) — [TradingView: Elliott Wave explanation](https://il.tradingview.com/chart/CRUDEOIL1%21/u6QWFgV0-Elliot-Wave-Explanation-I-live-Example-Crudeoil)

### Inferences
- Proposed objective anchor specification (design proposal, not sourced as a tested rule):
  1. Swing detector per timeframe: a swing high is confirmed when price closes at least k×ATR(n) below the highest high since the last confirmed swing low (ATR-reversal ZigZag, e.g. k = 2–3, n = 14), optionally also requiring an N-bar pivot (e.g., 5–20 bars each side). The ATR rule auto-scales across ES/NQ/CL and across 1-minute to weekly bars; an N-bar pivot alone does not scale with volatility.
  2. Anchors: retracement grid on the most recent confirmed impulse leg whose size is ≥ m×ATR (filters noise); optionally also the leg one timeframe up.
  3. Wick vs close: anchor to wicks (extremes) by default, since all reviewed tools and Boroden/DiNapoli measure from swing extremes; record close-anchored variants only as a secondary cluster contributor (gap: no source compared the two).
  4. Re-anchor rule: while the impulse is still extending (new extreme beyond the end anchor, before a counter-swing is confirmed), the grid's end anchor moves with the new extreme — this is the repaint risk. Only fire entries off a grid whose end anchor is confirmed (counter-move ≥ k×ATR), or accept that the zone moves and re-quote orders each bar.
  5. Invalidation: a close beyond the 100% level (origin) kills the grid (consistent with the Elliott wave-2 rule and LuxAlgo's "swing origin" invalidation); a close beyond 0.786 is an earlier "deep" invalidation option used by the golden-pocket tool.
  6. After invalidation, the swing detector's new leg defines a new grid; never "shop" for a different anchor to rescue a failed setup.
- Fib inherits all swing-definition ambiguity: a different depth/deviation/ATR multiple gives a different leg and therefore different levels; the report should cross-reference the swing-theory notes and pick one swing engine shared by Fib, structure and targets.
- Forexop's recursive "largest wave first" segmentation is objective but uses hindsight (the largest wave is only known after the fact), so it is suitable for review/visual labelling but not for live triggering.

(Unverified recollection, to be checked: built-in defaults are Deviation 3 and Depth 10, with the deviation threshold scaled by an ATR-based percentage.)

## 3. Multi-timeframe Fibonacci confluence / clusters (Boroden, DiNapoli, Fischer, Miner, Elliott)

### Takeaway
The practitioner consensus is that single Fib levels are weak and that tradable zones are where ≥3 independent Fibonacci relationships (retracements, extensions, projections from different swings and/or timeframes) fall within a tight band; Boroden gave explicit absolute bands for index futures (~2 ES points ideally, 3–4 NQ points), which should be rescaled (e.g., to a fraction of ATR) for current price levels.

### Cited Findings
- Boroden's cluster definition: "at least three Fibonacci price relationships" converging in a tight range; spread guidelines of about 2 points at most in the S&P (ideally) and 3–4 points in Nasdaq (ND) futures; symmetry projections of at least one prior corrective rally count toward a cluster — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Boroden's three trade types: Type 1 (highest probability) — with the immediate trend (2- or 5-min chart), enter at the cluster with no trigger, stop "just a few ticks" beyond the extreme of the cluster zone; Type 2 — a higher-timeframe (e.g., 60-min) cluster against the immediate trend, requires a lower-timeframe trigger; Type 3 — a two-step (ABC, "Gartley-style") pattern into a cluster against the short-term trend — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Boroden's triggers (Type 2, optionally Type 3): violation of a prior swing high/low, moving-average crossover, Haggerty 1-2-3 pattern, or a double top/bottom into the cluster confirmed by breaking the middle swing; entry on the break or on a pullback after it; if price clears the zone without a trigger she stays out; move stop to breakeven as soon as possible, then trail — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- Boroden's setup taxonomy (secondary summaries): Price Cluster (≥3 relationships), Symmetry (a projection of a prior corrective swing; a single symmetry projection counts as a setup in trend direction, strengthened by additional relationships), Two-Step pattern (a cluster within a zig-zag), and Time Clusters (Fib ratios of time between swing pairs projected forward, looking for several cycles converging) — [TradingMarkets: How I Identify Powerful Setups Using Symmetry](https://tradingmarkets.com/recent/how_i_identify_powerful_setups_using_symmetry-664959); [TradingMarkets: Using Fibonacci Time and Price Relationships in All Time Frames](https://tradingmarkets.com/recent/using_fibonacci_time_and_price_relationships_in_all_time_frames-671446)
- DiNapoli: overlapping F38/F61 levels from different reactions form a "confluence area," treated as the prime entry zone (no numeric overlap tolerance given); "single penetration" setup = first pullback into the 38.2% of a fresh thrust, with the trend, stop beyond the 61.8% of the same reaction — [LuxAlgo DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/dinapoli-levels.md)
- Robert Fischer's 1990s books are credited with formalising multi-swing measurement grids — [LuxAlgo Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- Generic cluster algorithm: collect levels from M significant swings; cluster requires ≥k coincident levels (k commonly 3) from ≥2 different swings; tolerance w in ticks or percent of price, recommended to be "a fraction of the recent average range"; cross-timeframe clusters weighted higher; confluence with non-Fib references (prior period levels, floor pivots, S/R, supply/demand) treated as stronger — [LuxAlgo Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- Elliott-wave Fibonacci guidelines (secondary sources; "guidelines, not rules"): wave 2 commonly retraces 50–61.8% of wave 1 (sometimes to 78.6%); wave 3 commonly 1.618 × wave 1 projected from the end of wave 2, with 2.618 the next reference when wave 3 extends; wave 4 commonly ~38.2% of wave 3 (range given as 38.2–61.8%); wave 4 may not overlap wave 1 (rule) — [The Right Trader: Elliott Wave Fibonacci Ratios](https://therighttrader.com/learn/elliott-wave-and-cycles/elliott-wave-fibonacci-ratios); [LuxAlgo Fib Wave Relationships](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-wave-relationships.md); [TradingView: Elliott Waves Ch. 4 Fibonacci Ratios](https://it.tradingview.com/chart/BTCUSD/JW9LFJKW-Elliot-Waves-Complete-Guide-Chapter-4-4-Fibonacci-Ratios); [FXStreet: Elliott Waves in a Nutshell](https://learningcenter.fxstreet.com/education/learning-center/unit-2/chapter-3/elliott-waves-in-a-nutshell/index.html); [Wikipedia: Elliott wave principle](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_wave)

### Inferences
- A cluster is the only Fib construct with a defensible rationale for automation: it combines independent anchors so the result is less sensitive to any single anchor choice ("removes anchor-shopping"), and it naturally incorporates the higher-timeframe leg.
- Suggested cluster specification (design proposal): contributors = {retracements 0.382/0.5/0.618/0.786 of the last 2–3 confirmed legs on the execution TF and the last 1–2 legs on each higher TF up to weekly; 1.272/1.618 extensions of the most recent correction; 0.618/1.0/1.618 projections of the prior same-direction leg from the latest swing (AB=CD/OP)}; tolerance w = 0.15–0.25 × ATR(14) of the execution timeframe (Boroden's 2 ES points is an absolute band from an era of much lower index prices; an ATR-relative band preserves the "tight" intent); require ≥3 contributors from ≥2 distinct anchors; score = Σ weights (higher TF > lower TF; projections and retracements from different legs > multiple ratios of the same leg); add bonus weight for coincident prior swing high/low or high-volume node (cross-reference volume-profile notes).
- Boroden's Type 1/Type 2 split is a clean, automatable rule: with-trend clusters can be limit-entered; counter-trend clusters need a lower-TF structure trigger (break of prior swing / double-bottom middle break). This maps directly onto a multi-timeframe workflow (higher TF supplies the zone, lower TF supplies the trigger).
- Elliott ratios are useful as a vocabulary for "normal" pullback/extension depths (2 at 50–61.8, 4 at 38.2, 3 at 1.618) but depend on wave counting, which is not objectively automatable; better to use them as contributors in a cluster than as standalone rules.

## 4. Popular modern setups (golden pocket, ICT OTE, 50% equilibrium/premium–discount, harmonic patterns, extension targets)

### Takeaway
Modern setups are repackagings of "buy a 0.5–0.79 pullback after a strong impulse, stop beyond the leg origin, target the prior extreme and 1.272/1.618 extensions"; their distinctive content is the structural prerequisite (e.g., ICT requires a displacement and market-structure shift before the retracement counts), not the specific ratios. Harmonics are the most rule-dense Fib application but their positive evidence is vendor/backtest-only.

### Cited Findings
- Golden pocket: band 0.618–0.65 of a measured leg; LuxAlgo's implementation draws pockets on every confirmed swing (Swing Length 20), keeps the 3 most recent, tracks status (fresh/tested/broken/invalidated), invalidates at 0.786 or the swing origin, and makes no performance claims — [LuxAlgo Golden Pocket](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/golden-pocket/)
- ICT OTE: retracement band 0.62–0.79 centred on 0.705, valid only after a Market Structure Shift (MSS) or Break of Structure (BOS) accompanied by aggressive displacement; execution typically waits for a lower-timeframe MSS inside the zone; stop commonly at the 100% level — [LuxAlgo Optimal Trade Entry](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry.md); [ChartingLens: ICT OTE](https://chartinglens.com/blog/ict-optimal-trade-entry-ote); [Equiti: OTE vs Fibonacci](https://www.equiti.com/jo-en/news/trading-ideas/ote-vs-fibonacci-ict-trading/); [InnerCircleTrader.net: ICT Fibonacci levels](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-fibonacci-levels/)
- There is no reliable published win-rate for OTE; the OTE nickname and 62–79 window are retail teaching; performance claims on ICT sites are not independently verified — [Equiti: OTE vs Fibonacci](https://www.equiti.com/jo-en/news/trading-ideas/ote-vs-fibonacci-ict-trading/); [The Right Trader: Optimal Trade Entry](https://therighttrader.com/learn/smart-money-concepts/optimal-trade-entry)
- ICT "premium/discount": the 50% of a dealing range divides "premium" (above, sell zone) from "discount" (below, buy zone) — practitioner blog only — [Premium and Discount Zones (blog)](https://dhanith-trading-blog.vercel.app/blog/premium-and-discount-zones)
- Harmonic definitions (Carney; third-party summaries, sources partially disagree): Gartley B = 0.618 XA, C = 0.382–0.886 AB, BC projection 1.272–1.618, D = 0.786 XA; Bat D = 0.886 XA (B given as 0.382–0.50 by some, ≤0.618 by others); Butterfly B ≈ 0.786 XA, D ≈ 1.27 XA (some give 1.27–1.618); Crab D = 1.618 XA (Carney's most extreme extension pattern); Deep Crab B ≈ 0.886 XA; Butterfly sometimes credited to Bryce Gilmore with Carney supplying ratios — [LuxAlgo Harmonic Patterns](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/harmonic-patterns.md); [LuxAlgo Bat](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/bat.md); [LuxAlgo Crab](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/crab.md); [Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/education/articles/forex-strategy/harmonic-trading-patterns); [IG: Top 7 harmonic patterns](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/top-7-harmonic-patterns-every-trader-should-know-210608)
- Harmonic evidence is vendor-level: a Nasdaq-100 backtest reports the bullish Gartley hit its target 60% of the time with 4.46% average return per trade (blog/vendor, not peer reviewed) — [LiberatedStockTrader: Gartley Pattern](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/gartley-pattern/); [LiberatedStockTrader: 6 Harmonic Patterns Tested](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/best-harmonic-trading-patterns/); other sites claim >70% or >85% success with no verifiable source — [TradeAlgo: Harmonic Patterns](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/technical-analysis/harmonic-patterns-how-to-trade-gartley-butterfly-bat-and-crab-formations); a skeptical site argues no reliable statistical evidence supports harmonics (no paper cited) — [AlgoStorm: Harmonic Patterns Debunked](https://algostorm.com/harmonic-chart-patterns/)
- Extension targets: Boroden's first objective on Type 1 trades is the 1.272 extension of the prior swing; symmetry/extension targets are not always met so she trails stops — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586); DiNapoli COP/OP/XOP (0.618/1.0/1.618 projections) used as staged take-profits — [ForTraders](https://fortraders.org/en/indicators/two-types-fibonacci-extensions.html)
- OTE target conventions vary by source; one lists stop at 100% and targets at the 0, −1 and −1.5 extensions of the measured leg — [The Right Trader: Optimal Trade Entry](https://therighttrader.com/learn/smart-money-concepts/optimal-trade-entry) (attribution within search snippet uncertain)

### Inferences
- The robust common core across golden pocket, OTE, DiNapoli and Boroden: (1) a strong, objectively measured impulse (displacement ≥ m×ATR, ideally breaking prior structure); (2) a first pullback into a 0.5–0.786 zone (DiNapoli's shallower 0.382 for very strong thrusts); (3) entry with a lower-timeframe structure trigger or a limit at a cluster; (4) stop beyond 0.786 or the 100% origin; (5) targets at the prior extreme (0%), then 1.272/1.618 extensions or the 1.0 projection (AB=CD/OP). This is automatable and visually reviewable.
- ICT's displacement + MSS prerequisite is the useful part (it filters for impulsive legs); the 0.62/0.705/0.79 numbers add nothing over 0.618/0.786 and should not be presented as special.
- Premium/discount at 50% is a re-label of "buy below the midpoint of the range" — a simple, objective location filter that can be combined with cluster zones, but has no specific evidence.
- Harmonics are fully rule-defined (ratio tolerances per leg) and so are automatable, but they require 5 confirmed swings (X, A, B, C, D), so they are highly sensitive to swing-detector settings and the D point repaints until confirmed; given the absence of peer-reviewed evidence, they are best treated as "pre-labelled cluster" generators (D completion = cluster of XA retracement + BC projection + AB=CD) rather than standalone signals.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of harmonic patterns was found in this session.
- Scott Carney's own published ratio tables (harmonictrader.com / *Harmonic Trading* Vol. 1–2) were not reached; the B-point range for the Bat in particular is inconsistent across secondary sources.
- ICT's own primary material (YouTube/mentorship) was not reviewed; target conventions (e.g., the "−0.27/−0.62 standard deviation" targets often attributed to ICT) are unverified here.
- No test of the golden pocket specifically was found.

## 5. Fibonacci time zones / time ratios

### Takeaway
Fibonacci time zones (vertical lines at 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21… bars from a swing) and Boroden/Miner-style time ratios (Fib multiples of prior swing durations) exist in most platforms, but the only rigorous test found (Batchelor & Ramyar, Dow 1914–2002, duration ratios included) found no more significant ratios than chance; recommend excluding time as a trigger and at most using it as a low-weight "time window" annotation for visual review.

### Cited Findings
- Fibonacci time zones are vertical lines spaced at Fibonacci intervals (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34…) from a chosen starting bar, usually a significant swing high/low — [Babypips: Fibonacci Time Zones](https://www.babypips.com/forexpedia/fibonacci-time-zones); [Fidelity: Fibonacci Time Zones](https://fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/technical-analysis/technical-indicator-guide/fibonacci-time-zones); [MultiCharts: Fibonacci Time Zones](https://www.multicharts.com/trading-software/index.php/Fibonacci_Time_Zones)
- The first few lines bunch near the start and are often ignored; some suggest starting from ~13 or 21 periods out — [SuperMoney: Fibonacci Time Zone](https://www.supermoney.com/encyclopedia/fibonacci-time-zone); [FinLogix](https://finlogix.com/blog/95594b9d-be8d-4203-a23b-c457af5ea32e); CQG's implementation starts at the 3 interval and omits 1 and 2 — [CQG: Fibonacci Time Zones (FiboTZ)](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/21/Documents/fibonaccitimezonesfibotz.htm)
- Babypips claims about 70% predictive power for time zones (unverified, single-source) — [Babypips](https://www.babypips.com/forexpedia/fibonacci-time-zones)
- Boroden's time cycles measure time between key swing high/low combinations and project forward with Fib ratios, looking for clusters of cycles in a short time range; in one example a time window plus price hit with reversal signals defined a pivotal high — [TradingMarkets: Using Fibonacci Time and Price Relationships](https://tradingmarkets.com/recent/using_fibonacci_time_and_price_relationships_in_all_time_frames-671446); [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586); [MetaTrader Mentors podcast: Boroden on Fibonacci time analysis](https://metatradermentors.libsyn.com/12-fibonacci-queen-carolyn-boroden-on-the-secrets-of-fibonacci-time-analysis)
- Batchelor & Ramyar tested whether ratios of both the length and the duration of successive Dow trends cluster around round fractions or Fibonacci ratios (turning points via business-cycle-style dating, stationary block bootstrap): "A few significant ratios appear, but no more than would be expected by chance" — [City Research Online: Magic numbers in the Dow (2006)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/16276/)

### Inferences
- Time projections multiply the anchoring problem (two anchors for each duration) and produce wide windows; with several cycles, some window almost always lies near any given turn, inviting the same selective-observation bias seen with price levels.
- If included at all, time should be a visual annotation ("this cluster coincides with a 1.0/1.618 time ratio of the prior swing") with zero weight in automatic triggering.

### Gaps
- Robert Miner's time methods (alternate time projections, time-band ratios) were not retrieved.
- No futures-specific test of Fibonacci time zones was found.

## 6. Evidence base (academic and quasi-academic tests; self-fulfilling prophecy)

### Takeaway
The weight of evidence does not support Fibonacci ratios as a standalone edge: the most rigorous tests (Batchelor & Ramyar 2006 on the Dow; Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos in *Expert Systems with Applications* on three equity markets; Forexop's 40k-correction FX histogram) find no excess reactions at Fib levels versus chance. The one notable positive study (Shanaev & Gibson SSRN working paper) reports predictive power and Fama-French alpha, but its abstract highlights the 0% and 100% levels (the swing extremes themselves) and 50% among the most important. The plausible mechanism for any effect is order clustering at widely watched prices (Osler), which argues for using Fib as a standardised pullback-depth and confluence tool rather than a magic level.

### Cited Findings
- Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos ("Automatic identification and evaluation of Fibonacci retracements: Empirical evidence from three equity markets," *Expert Systems with Applications*, 2021/2022) propose an algorithm that identifies Fib support/resistance and constructs zones; their empirical results provide no support for Fibonacci retracements as a standalone technical rule; they note this absence of evidence is surprising given practitioner usage; they find a positive relationship between Fibonacci zone width and bounce probability — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354702159_Automatic_identification_and_evaluation_of_Fibonacci_retracements_Empirical_evidence_from_three_equity_markets); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421012495); [RiuNet (UPV) record](https://riunet.upv.es/handle/10251/195083?show=full)
- Batchelor & Ramyar (Cass Business School discussion paper, Sept 2006), Dow Jones Industrial Average: tested whether ratios of length and duration of successive trends cluster at round fractions or Fib ratios using a stationary bootstrap; "A few significant ratios appear, but no more than would be expected by chance" — [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/16276/); a press summary (The Economist's Buttonwood, reproduced) describes the sample as 1914–2002 and the finding as no evidence Fibonacci numbers work in US stock markets (sample period not verified against the paper) — [Scribd reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/505062350/numbers)
- Shanaev & Gibson, "Can Returns Breed Like Rabbits? Econometric Tests for Fibonacci Retracements" (SSRN working paper, 2022): reports predictive power of Fib retracements for international stock indices and FX rates; the abstract (as returned) names 0%, "38.1%", 50%, "61.2%" and 100% as the levels that matter most (figures as returned, possibly typos for 38.2/61.8); an S&P 500 strategy going long (short) stocks closer to retracement support (resistance) earns positive, statistically significant alpha in Fama-French multi-factor models with market-timing properties, not explained by calendar anomalies or return reversals — [SSRN 4212430](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4212430) (full text not accessed; publication status unknown)
- Forexop test: 1-minute Dukascopy bid data, 9 G10 USD pairs, Oct 2003–May 2016, 40,243 corrections (>15% depth) identified by recursive largest-wave segmentation; ~83% of corrections fall between 15% and 61.8% (only ~17% exceed 61.8%); distributions are log-normal with no peaks at Fib levels; the author concludes Fib retracements "do not represent a real phenomenon" and attributes belief to selective observation. Caveat: no formal significance test or randomised-level comparison; FX only — [Forexop](https://forexop.com/?p=7920)
- Blog test across 102 stocks/indexes ("1,136 years of data") reports Fib retracement failed to predict a turning point 63% of the time (not peer reviewed) — [LiberatedStockTrader](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/how-to-use-fibonacci-retracement/)
- Shaker, Asad & Zulfiqar (IJEFR 2018): 4 Pakistani cement stocks, Q1 2017 closing prices; of 63 support levels 17 (27%) and of 66 resistance levels 24 (36%) "followed Fibonacci retracements"; authors accept the hypothesis "to some extent" — tiny sample, no random-level baseline — [IJEFR](https://arpgweb.com/pdf-files/ijefr4(6)159-164.pdf)
- Gupta (2011, Macalester College economics honors project) "Fibonacci Retracements and Self-Fulfilling Prophecy" builds a stylised model in which traders who see Fib levels work use them more, reinforcing predictive power (undergraduate thesis; only summary seen) — [Macalester Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.macalester.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?httpsredir=1&article=1041&context=economics_honors_projects)
- Osler (*Journal of Finance*, 2003): currency stop-loss and take-profit orders cluster; take-profit orders cluster strongly at round numbers (consistent with reversals at S/R), and stop-loss orders cluster just beyond round numbers (consistent with accelerated moves after breaks) — [IDEAS/RePEc: Osler 2003 JoF 58(5):1791–1819](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html); underlying data ≈9,700 orders at one large dealing bank, Sept 1999–Apr 2000 — [NY Fed Staff Report 125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.html); executed stop-loss sells cluster just below, and stop buys just above, round numbers; take-profits cluster more tightly at exact round numbers — [NY Fed Staff Report 150 (stop-loss orders and price cascades)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.html); Osler notes statistics cannot prove causality — [NY Fed SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Tsinaslanidis finding that wider zones have higher bounce probability is what one expects mechanically (a wider target catches more turns), which underlines that any Fib "hit rate" claim is meaningless without a same-width random-level baseline.
- Forexop's distribution (most corrections between 15% and 61.8%, log-normal) is itself useful: it gives a base rate for pullback depth. Fib levels can therefore be reframed as standardised quantiles of pullback depth — e.g., a stop beyond 78.6/100% sits beyond the bulk of normal corrections (~17% of corrections in that sample exceeded 61.8%), which is a legitimate risk-placement rationale independent of φ.
- Shanaev & Gibson's emphasis on 0% and 100% (the swing extremes) and 50% is consistent with classic support/resistance at prior highs/lows and range midpoints rather than φ ratios specifically — supporting the hypothesis that Fib "works" mainly via confluence with prior structure.
- Osler's order-clustering evidence is about round numbers, not Fib levels; by analogy, a self-fulfilling effect at Fib levels would require that many participants anchor the same grid — plausible for obvious higher-timeframe swings, implausible for idiosyncratic intraday anchors. This argues for higher-timeframe, obvious anchors and for tolerance zones (stops "just beyond," per Osler, implies sweeps slightly through levels).
- For a user who will not backtest: present Fib as (a) a consistent ruler for pullback depth and target spacing and (b) a confluence contributor, never as an independent edge.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specific to futures (ES/NQ/CL) Fibonacci reactions was found.
- Full texts of Tsinaslanidis et al. and Shanaev & Gibson were not accessible (403), so exact sample periods, zone widths, effect sizes and robustness checks are not reported here.
- Older studies seen only by title (not read): Riedel, "Do stock prices reflect Fibonacci ratios?" (*Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities* V.7:12) — [Traders.com store](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c12-dofibon-pdf.html); "A computational exploration of the efficacy of Fibonacci Sequences in Technical analysis and trading" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/27827147_A_computational_exploration_of_the_efficacy_of_Fibonacci_Sequences_in_Technical_analysis_and_trading).
- No academic test of ICT OTE, golden pocket, or DiNapoli setups was found.

## 7. Automation (auto-Fib algorithms, cluster detection, repainting, zones, stops, targets)

### Takeaway
Auto-Fib is simple: swing engine → grid on latest confirmed leg → zones with lifecycle states → alerts. The hard parts are repainting (an unconfirmed end anchor moves the whole grid) and cluster scoring (pool levels from multiple anchors/timeframes, bin within an ATR-relative tolerance, require ≥3 hits from ≥2 anchors). All of this is deterministic and suits a "propose, visually review, then auto-trigger" workflow on Rithmic/Tradovate.

### Cited Findings
- Auto-Fib tools are ZigZag/pivot detectors plus a grid: TradingView scripts use Depth (min bars between pivots) and Deviation (sensitivity) — [TradingView: Fib Retracement (AUTO)](https://www.tradingview.com/script/HdVXYTjV-Fib-Retracement-AUTO); ATR-reversal confirmation (ATR 14 × 3.0) — [TradingView: Auto Structure Fibonacci](https://www.tradingview.com/script/VqR24zzI-Auto-Structure-Fibonacci/); Donchian ZigZag — [ProRealCode](https://www.prorealcode.com/prorealtime-indicators/automatic-fibonacci-levels-on-the-latest-swing/); ZigZag "period" where higher values give fewer, larger swings — [LuxAlgo Fibonacci Retracement indicator](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/ySq5zWb7-fibonacci-retracement/)
- Some auto-Fib scripts expose a repainting option explicitly — [TradingView: Auto Fib Retracement victhoreb](https://in.tradingview.com/script/Pv9yc2mz-Auto-Fib-Retracement-victhoreb)
- Zone lifecycle design: pockets labelled fresh/tested/broken/invalidated; tests on wicks, breaks and invalidation on closes; invalidation at 0.786 or swing origin; dashed line marks where zone-based stops belong; keep the 3 most recent zones — [LuxAlgo Golden Pocket](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/golden-pocket/)
- Cluster detection algorithm: compute every qualifying draw, pool and sort all levels, group levels within tolerance w, keep groups with ≥k levels (k commonly 3) from ≥2 swings, mark each zone from lowest to highest grouped level, score by count, draw diversity and timeframe weight — [LuxAlgo Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters.md)
- Boroden's execution rules translate directly: with-trend cluster = limit entry, stop a few ticks beyond the cluster's extreme; counter-trend/HTF cluster = wait for lower-TF trigger (swing break, MA cross, 1-2-3, double top/bottom middle break); no trigger = no trade; breakeven ASAP then trail; first target 1.272 extension — [TradingMarkets TraderTalk: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)
- DiNapoli single penetration: entry at F38 of a fresh thrust, stop beyond F61 of the same reaction; targets COP/OP/XOP — [LuxAlgo DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/dinapoli-levels.md)
- Stops commonly at the 100% level for OTE — [LuxAlgo Optimal Trade Entry](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry.md)
- Order-flow rationale for stop buffers: stop-loss orders cluster just beyond salient levels, producing cascades when crossed — [NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.html)

### Inferences
- Reference algorithm (design proposal for the final report; parameters are starting points for visual review, not optimised):
  1. Swing engine per TF (e.g., 5m, 15m, 60m, 4h, D, W): ATR-reversal ZigZag (k = 2–3 × ATR(14) of that TF), optionally with an N-bar pivot confirmation. Store each leg (start, end, start time, end time, size in ATR, confirmed flag).
  2. Grids: for each TF, on the last 2–3 confirmed legs with size ≥ 3×ATR: retracements {0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786}; for the latest correction: extensions {1.272, 1.618}; for the latest swing: projections of the prior same-direction leg {0.618, 1.0, 1.618}.
  3. Clusters: pool all levels, bin with w = 0.2 × ATR(14) of the execution TF (or Boroden-style absolute ticks rescaled), keep bins with ≥3 levels from ≥2 anchors; score with TF weights (e.g., W/D = 3, 4h/60m = 2, execution TF = 1) plus bonus for coincident prior swing extreme or volume node.
  4. Repaint handling: only legs with a confirmed end anchor generate executable zones; an unconfirmed (still-extending) leg can be displayed as a "provisional" grid for visual review, but orders are never placed off it; when the leg extends, provisional zones are recomputed and any resting orders cancelled/re-quoted.
  5. Entry zones not exact prices: zone = [min, max] of clustered levels, expanded by a small buffer; with-trend: scaled limit orders inside the zone; counter-trend: require lower-TF trigger (close back through a micro swing, or break of the micro double-bottom middle).
  6. Invalidation/stops: stop beyond the cluster extreme plus buffer (Boroden), or beyond 0.786/100% of the anchor leg plus buffer (DiNapoli/OTE/golden-pocket conventions); add an ATR-fraction buffer to avoid the stop-cluster sweep zone described by Osler.
  7. Target ladder: T1 = prior swing extreme (0%), T2 = 1.272 extension (Boroden first objective) or COP, T3 = 1.618 extension / OP / XOP; move to breakeven after T1; trail thereafter.
  8. Lifecycle: zones expire after first test or after N bars; grids die on a close beyond 100%.
- Rithmic/Tradovate implementation implication: the logic is bar-close deterministic and can run in a separate engine that computes zones on completed bars and places/cancels bracket orders; the user's visual review step should show the anchor legs, every contributing level, and the cluster score, so anchor choice is auditable.
- Clusters spanning many timeframes up to weekly will often be wide; cap cluster width (e.g., ≤0.5 × ATR of execution TF) or split into sub-zones so the stop distance remains tradeable for scalps.
