# Give swings, volume and Fibonacci separate jobs

Swing structure, volume profile and Fibonacci are not three independent signals. They are closer to two and a half. Every Fibonacci level is arithmetic on two or three swing points, so a Fib retracement and the swing it was drawn from record the same geometric fact. Volume-at-price is the only one of the three that measures something swings cannot: where contracts actually changed hands. The workable combination therefore gives each dimension one job on one layer. Higher-timeframe swing structure supplies CONTEXT. A volume node found inside a Fibonacci depth band supplies LOCATION, with the profile picking the level and Fib only labelling how deep the pullback is. A lower-timeframe price-action event (sweep-and-reclaim, BOS, 1-2-3) supplies the TRIGGER. Structure (leg origin, sweep extreme) sets invalidation, and profile levels or extensions that land on them set targets. The evidence supports the mechanism under this design, but narrowly. Published support/resistance levels interrupted FX trends **60.8% of the time versus 56.2% for arbitrary levels** ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). Stop orders cluster just beyond salient prices and speed up moves for hours ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)), and S/R coincides with order-book depth ([Kavajecz & Odders-White 2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html)). No peer-reviewed study tests Fibonacci ratios, volume-profile nodes or multi-method confluence in futures, and the rigorous Fib tests are negative. That makes discipline about independence and pre-commitment worth more than any extra line on the chart: one pre-declared condition per role, tolerances scaled to ATR, and rules frozen before review. Bailey and colleagues show that false discoveries rise with every configuration tried, and refining rules by eyeballing past charts is the same process without the bookkeeping. Scalping and swing trading use the same role map with different weights. Scalps lean on session profiles and volume-bar triggers and reduce Fib to a coarse band. Swings lean on weekly and daily structure and multi-week profiles, where Fib anchors are obvious enough that other traders plausibly watch them.

This file covers the interactions only. Each dimension is treated on its own in [01 Swing points across timeframes](01%20Swing%20points%20across%20timeframes.md), [02 Multi timeframe price action](02%20Multi%20timeframe%20price%20action.md), [03 Volume profile across timeframes](03%20Volume%20profile%20across%20timeframes.md), [04 Fibonacci retracements and extensions](04%20Fibonacci%20retracements%20and%20extensions.md) and [05 Volume bars versus time bars](05%20Volume%20bars%20versus%20time%20bars.md). All names (SWING(L), STRUCTURE(L), PROFILE(P), FIB(A→B), VB-A/B/C, G1–G3) follow the shared conventions in [00 Index and reading guide](00%20Index%20and%20reading%20guide.md). An optional fourth input, options dealer gamma exposure and equity-market internals for intraday ES/NQ, is covered separately in [12 Options gamma and equity context](12%20Options%20gamma%20and%20equity%20context.md) and is not evaluated here. If a strategy uses it, it belongs in the CONTEXT role under the same rule of one pre-declared condition per role, and it should not be counted as an extra location vote. Every numeric default below is a pre-committed starting value taken from the research notes. None has been optimized. Statements labelled *design proposal* or *inference* are synthesis, not sourced findings.

## Three dimensions, but only one adds information the swings lack

The three dimensions measure different things, and that difference decides which role each can fill. Swing structure records the order of confirmed extremes: whether the last swing in each direction held or broke. That makes it naturally about direction and persistence. Its rigorous support is indirect. Osler's FX test used levels that practitioners typically derive from prior peaks and troughs. She quotes Murphy: "a support level is identified beforehand by a previous reaction low" ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). Trend persistence over one to twelve months is robust across 58 futures markets ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)). A volume profile records the distribution of traded contracts by price, which separates prices the auction accepted (POC, HVN) from prices it passed through quickly (LVN, single prints). TradingView's own documentation calls the profile reactive: it shows past activity and does not forecast ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000502040-volume-profile/)). Fibonacci records only the ratio of a pullback or extension to a prior leg. Practitioner sources themselves say "the ratios themselves have no mechanical force" ([LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)), and "depth is a measurement, not a signal by itself" ([TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/deep-vs-shallow-pullback)).

| Dimension | What it measures | Built from | Roles it fits best | Must never supply | Grade of underlying mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swing structure / price action | Order of confirmed extremes; whether protected swings held | OHLC of layer L via SWING(L), STRUCTURE(L) | CONTEXT (higher layers), TRIGGER (lower layers), invalidation | The location itself (an LTF pattern is not a reason to be at a price) | A for swing extremes as reaction levels; B for structure as bias |
| Volume profile | Where contracts changed hands over a chosen window | Tick prints at 1-tick rows, PROFILE(P) | LOCATION, targets, the acceptance test that separates sweeps from breakouts | TRIGGER or direction on its own | B (indirect, via the S/R–depth link) |
| Fibonacci | Ratio of the current move to a prior leg | Two or three confirmed swing prices, FIB(A→B) | Depth label, target spacing, a stop placed beyond normal pullbacks | CONTEXT, TRIGGER, or "confirmation" of the swing it was drawn on | D as a standalone level; B as a measurement convention |

The asymmetry in the last column drives the rest of this document. Swing levels have the best mechanism support and Fib levels the worst, yet Fib is drawn entirely from swings. Its usefulness therefore lies in standardizing measurement, not in adding evidence. The profile is the one input whose agreement with geometry carries new information. In CME futures it is also directly measurable from the tape, not inferred.

## Fibonacci is a ruler laid on the swings, not a second witness

The swing–Fib pair is the most dependent of the three. Every retracement and extension is a deterministic function of the confirmed points A and B, plus C for projections. So "61.8% plus the prior swing low" counts one fact twice unless that low comes from a *different* leg. Practitioner methods that look different (DiNapoli's F38/F61, Elliott's wave ratios, ICT's OTE band, Sperandeo's 1-2-3, Brooks' measured moves) are all rule-sets laid on the same anchors. ICT teaching requires the impulse to break structure and says "a drifting, overlapping move is not an impulse," with invalidation beyond the leg's origin ([LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)). DiNapoli measures from the "focus number" back to each qualifying reaction, and a longer swing parameter picks larger reactions ([LuxAlgo: DiNapoli Levels](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)). The practical consequence is that Fib inherits every ambiguity of the swing detector. A different ZigZag threshold gives a different leg and a different grid. All three dimensions must therefore share one swing engine: SWING(L) with k = 2.0 × ATR(L), frozen at the extreme, using confirmed swings only.

What Fib adds is a common ruler, and the ruler's most defensible use is classifying the regime of a pullback, not timing an entry. The FX distribution study gives the base rate. Across **40,243 corrections in nine G10 pairs, about 83% ended between 15% and 61.8%, with no peaks at Fibonacci levels** ([Forexop](https://forexop.com/?p=7920)). A stop beyond 0.786 or 1.0 therefore sits beyond the bulk of normal corrections, a risk-placement argument that does not depend on φ at all. Practitioner readings of depth contradict one another. Some call 61.8% the strongest continuation level; others read a pullback past 61.8% as fading momentum ([LuxAlgo: Fib Retracement](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-retracement/); [TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/trend-analysis/deep-vs-shallow-pullback)). DiNapoli's codified rule is that a 61.8% penetration "warns the setup is reaching the zone where it is usually abandoned" ([LuxAlgo: DiNapoli](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)), while ICT treats 0.62–0.79 as the preferred zone. A fixed band table removes the argument by deciding in advance.

| Pullback depth on FIB(A→B) of the setup leg | Label (design proposal) | Eligible action |
|---|---|---|
| Shallower than 0.382 | Strong trend | Only the shallow location: DiNapoli-style first F38 pullback, or the leg POC if it sits high in the leg |
| 0.382–0.618 | Normal | Standard location band |
| 0.618–0.786 | Deep | Still eligible, but the thesis is weakening; no add-ons |
| Beyond 0.786 | Degraded | Stand aside from continuation; only reversal templates (1-2-3, spring) may fire |
| Close beyond A | Grid invalid | Discard the grid; the structure engine will usually register a CHoCH; never re-anchor to rescue the setup |

Extensions do the opposite job and supply MANAGEMENT. Boroden's first objective in a with-trend trade is the **1.272 extension of the prior swing** ([TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)). DiNapoli's COP/OP/XOP project 0.618, 1.0 and 1.618 of the thrust from the end of the reaction ([LuxAlgo: DiNapoli](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)). Brooks' Leg 1 = Leg 2 is the same 1.0 projection, and the hard part is locating where each leg starts ([Brooks forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/es/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/measured-move-from-failed-leg-1-leg-2/)). One academic result keeps the target logic honest. In the directional-change framework, a reversal of size θ is followed on average by an overshoot of about θ, but the authors note that the same result appears in a binomial random walk ([Glattfelder, Dupuis & Olsen 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.1040)). A 1.0 projection is therefore a neutral reference distance, not a forecast.

The one positive econometric Fib study points the same way. Shanaev and Gibson's working paper reports predictive power for retracements, but its abstract names **0%, 50% and 100%** among the levels that matter most ([SSRN 4212430](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4212430)). Those are the swing extremes and the range midpoint, which is structure, not φ. The same logic suggests caution about reversals at all. Bulkowski's close-based 1-2-3 trend change reached a 20% move in **74 of 101 bullish cases (73%) but only 29 of 67 bearish cases (43%)** on daily stocks ([ThePatternSite](https://www.thepatternsite.com/123tc.html)). That is small-sample equity data, but it argues that bearish structure-reversal setups in upward-drifting index futures deserve a lower prior.

| Swing + Fib mechanism | Rule form (design proposal unless cited) | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Depth classifies the pullback regime | Band table above, measured on the last confirmed leg of the setup layer | B |
| Extensions and projections give target spacing | 1.272/1.618 extension of A→B; C ± 1.0×\|B−A\| projection; used only when they meet another dimension (see below) | C |
| Structural invalidation | Close beyond A kills the grid; stops go beyond the leg origin or the sweep extreme, never at a Fib line | B (stop-cluster mechanism, Osler) |
| Multi-anchor clusters are partly independent | FIB-CLUSTER = ≥3 levels from ≥2 distinct anchors within 0.2 × ATR(L); counts as one geometric vote, not three | C |

## Swing anchors tell the profile where to look; the profile tells the swing which prices were accepted

The swing–profile pair is complementary, not redundant. Structure decides where a profile starts and ends. The profile then ranks the prices inside that window by how much trade they attracted. Practitioner guides tell traders to draw a fixed-range profile from one swing point to the next, or "the last major swing high to swing low" for swing trading ([ForexTester](https://forextester.com/blog/fixed-range-volume-profile/); [FuturesHive](https://www.futureshive.com/blog/volume-profile-trading-strategy-2025)). Open-source scripts already build one profile per completed swing leg with no manual anchoring ([TradingView: Swing Volume Profile Pro](https://id.tradingview.com/script/7yozLqCv-Swing-Volume-Profile-Pro-WillyAlgoTrader/)). In the shared conventions this is PROFILE(ANCH(A→B)) between two confirmed SWING(L) points. One automation rule matters here. The accumulation starts at the pivot bar, but the profile may drive decisions only from the confirmation bar onward, as auto-anchored swing VWAP tools already do ([LuxAlgo: Auto-Anchored Swing VWAPs](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/yGQMVJ90-auto-anchored-swing-vwaps/)). Anchors must come from the single pre-declared detector, never from whichever pivot makes the profile look best.

Inside the anchored leg, high-volume nodes read as acceptance zones that act as support or resistance, while low-volume nodes are "areas of rejection where price moved through quickly" ([TradersAgency](https://tradersagency.com/blog/volume-profile-analysis); [LuxAlgo: FRVP](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/4r2CZGol-fixed-range-volume-profile/)). An LVN plays two roles depending on structure (inference from practitioner heuristics). With the trend intact, a pullback that slices into an LVN tends to run to the next HVN or POC below, so the LVN is a poor buy and the HVN beneath it is the location. On a breakout from a range, the LVN between old and new value is an air pocket that should not be re-accepted; volume building inside it means the breakout is failing. Regime has to be classified first, because the two uses point in opposite directions. The leg POC works as the "fair price of the impulse" and is a natural pullback location. The sources give no consistent entry, stop or target rules and no backtests ([Ironbeam](https://www.ironbeam.com/?p=11196)).

The most useful interaction is that the profile settles the sweep-versus-breakout question structure alone cannot answer. Osler's order data shows stop-loss buys clustering just above round numbers and sell stops just below, with the stop response larger and longer-lasting than the take-profit response ([Osler SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf); [Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). The finding concerns round numbers; extending it to obvious swing extremes is an inference. Profile edges (VAH/VAL, poor highs) mark where the auction ran out of opposite-side participation. **A sweep beyond a swing extreme that is also beyond a value-area edge, followed by a fast return inside value, is the cleanest structure-plus-profile reversal**: the stops fire, and no acceptance forms outside. The mirror case is a BOS where volume builds outside the old value area and value migrates, which is continuation. Value migration compares today's value area with yesterday's, and a POC moving with the value area strengthens the read ([Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/value-migration-explained-how-markets-shift-from-one-area-of-acceptance-to-another)). Once both readings are written as rules, an ambiguous chart becomes a testable condition. For example: *accepted* = at least two M30 closes beyond the edge plus a dPOC shift beyond it; *rejected* = back inside value within three trigger-layer bars with no new HVN outside. The notes give no canonical threshold for acceptance, so these values are proposals.

Two definitional traps belong in code. A Market Profile *poor high* (two or more TPOs at the session high, no single-print tail) is not a price-action equal high. The source is blunt: "do not rename a double top as a poor high because the highs match" ([TheRightTrader](https://therighttrader.com/learn/volume-and-order-flow/poor-highs-and-lows)). Context also changes the reading: "a poor high far above value is a different trip than a poor high sitting on the value-area high of a balanced day" ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/market-structure/single-prints-poor-highs-poor-lows-market-profile)). The equal-highs tolerance in this set (0.15 × ATR(L)) and the poor-high test (TPO count at the extreme) should therefore stay separate flags that sometimes coincide.

| Swing + profile mechanism | Rule form (design proposal unless cited) | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Swings anchor the profile | ANCH(A→B) from confirmed SWING(L); active from confirm time | B (platform practice) |
| Profile ranks prices inside the leg | Leg POC/HVN = candidate location; LVN = transit zone, not a location | C |
| Acceptance separates sweep from breakout | Sweep + back inside value fast = reversal; closes outside + dPOC shift = continuation | B (stop-clustering mechanism) / C (rule) |
| Value migration confirms structure | STRUCTURE(D1) BOS with WEEK or RTH value higher = aligned; BOS with overlapping value = suspect | C |
| Contradiction flag | Structural level inside an LVN = weak level; poor high just overhead = fading below it is lower quality | C |

## Volume at price is the only independent vote in the location role

The profile–Fib pair is where the independence argument is strongest. A Fib level is pure geometry from two prices, and a profile is built from where volume actually traded. A retracement that lands on a POC, HVN or naked POC is therefore a genuine two-dimension agreement. The basic check is whether "the POC of the swing aligns with a key Fib level," because a Fib level "is theoretical" and volume "helps verify it" ([LuxAlgo: Auto-Anchored Fibonacci Volume Profile](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/z8gaWHWQ-auto-anchored-fibonacci-volume-profile/)). The combination is mechanizable. One open-source tool pairs ATR-filtered pivot detection with a bar-by-bar profile to "automatically identify and score every Fibonacci retracement level" ([TradingView: PhenLabs](https://de.tradingview.com/script/8EJkZCuY-Fibonacci-Volume-Confluence-Engine-PhenLabs/)). The standard practitioner checklist measures both over the same leg, looks for a POC or HVN in the 0.618–0.65 band, and treats LVN valleys as zones price may cross fast ([TradingView script KPUDW7dp](https://www.tradingview.com/script/KPUDW7dp/)).

The highest-value rule is to let the profile choose the Fib level. A five-level grid offers five chances to claim a "hold" after the fact. A rule such as "the active level is the one Fib level inside the depth band with the highest volume within tolerance, provided it sits in an HVN and not an LVN" leaves one location per leg (design proposal). That also shrinks the multiple-comparisons problem, which matters because the grid is dense: retracements between 0.236 and 0.786 sit roughly every 12–15% of the leg, so almost any pullback ends "near" some Fib level. The profile also predicts where the two will disagree (inference). If the leg POC sits near 0.236–0.382 (volume concentrated near the top, as in a grind-up after a gap), the shallow level is the location and the deeper Fibs sit in thin territory. A break below the POC then tends to slide to the next HVN, often near the leg origin. If the POC sits low in the leg (a breakout from a base), the 0.618–0.786 zone coincides with value and the shallow levels sit in LVNs that price should cross. In both cases the profile decides *which* level and Fib only provides the depth label.

For targets, the same logic runs in reverse. An extension (1.272 or 1.618 of the leg, or a DiNapoli OP/XOP) that lands within tolerance of a prior week's naked POC, VAH/VAL or a composite HVN is a stronger take-profit candidate than either level alone. Osler found that take-profit orders cluster *at* salient prices and act as "partially reflecting barriers" ([Osler SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)). That is a mechanism for exits at such levels, and it extends to round numbers, which are a cheap and fully independent fourth source. In Osler's data about **8.7% of orders sat at levels ending in 00, with no special tendency at 25/75** ([Osler SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.pdf)). The futures analogue (whole handles and 50/100-point levels in ES/NQ, whole dollars in CL) is an untested inference.

The independence is partial in one case, and the literature for this pair is thin. When the same swing anchors define both the Fib grid and an anchored profile, the two share *time* anchors while measuring different things. That is less dependence than Fib plus swing levels, but it is not full independence. WEEK, MONTH, COMP or prior-RTH profiles that are not anchored to the current swing are the more independent vote. The literature consists almost entirely of indicator descriptions. The best-known Fib-cluster practitioner, Boroden, explicitly does *not* use volume ([TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)). The best-known volume-profile educator, Trader Dale, pairs volume clusters with a weekly POC and resistance-turned-support flips, and the search found **no Dale material using Fibonacci, so attributing "VP + Fib" to Dale is not supported** ([Trader Dale, Oct 2025](https://trader-dale.blogspot.com/2025/10/trade-of-week-perfect-confluence-volume.html)). Claims that the golden pocket is "mathematically the highest probability reversal zone," and a quoted "71% win rate with 3.1:1 R:R," have no verifiable source. A NexusFi confluence score with a "minimum 4.0 required to trade" could not be checked because its weights were unretrievable ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/fibonacci-confluence-cluster-zones)). One academic Fib study adds a warning about zone width: wider Fib zones showed higher bounce probability ([Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421012495)). That is what a wider net does mechanically, so any hit rate means nothing without a same-width random-level baseline.

| Situation | Swing structure says | Profile says | Fib says | Pre-declared resolution (design proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prior swing low (turned support) sits inside an LVN | Support | No acceptance there | Irrelevant | Not a location; skip, or wait for the next HVN below |
| Leg POC above the 0.382 level in a strong trend | Trend intact | Value is high in the leg | Deeper levels "available" | Use the shallow POC; deeper Fib levels are transit zones |
| Leg POC near the origin (breakout from a base) | Trend intact | Value is low in the leg | Shallow levels look like support | Use the 0.618–0.786 band where it meets value; shallow levels are LVN transit |
| Sweep beyond swing high and beyond VAH, back inside fast | Possible 2B | Rejected, no acceptance | Irrelevant | Reversal templates eligible; continuation blocked |
| Close beyond swing high, closes stay outside VAH, dPOC shifts | BOS | Accepted, value migrating | Old grid obsolete | Continuation templates only; fades blocked |
| Poor high just above price, no structural reason to fade | Neutral | Unfinished auction overhead | Irrelevant | Do not fade below it; treat as a target |
| W1 DOWN, D1 UP | CONFLICT | — | — | Stand aside, or failure templates only at W1 levels (choose one per strategy) |
| Pullback beyond 0.786, structure not yet broken | Weakening | — | Degraded | Continuation blocked; reversal templates only after the structure engine confirms |

## Five roles across five layers: how the three dimensions divide the work

The combination that avoids blending the three into one indicator gives each a different job on a different timeframe layer. This decomposition is a synthesis; no single source states it. The named methods reviewed below are each a partial version of it, and the reasons for each assignment come from the evidence. CONTEXT needs persistence, and persistence is the best-supported property of trends: time-series momentum over 1–12 months in futures ([Moskowitz et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250)) and, intraday, a first-half-hour return that predicts the last half hour with an R² of about 1.6% ([Gao et al. 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). LOCATION needs liquidity, and S/R levels coincide with order-book depth peaks: technical rules partly work by "locating depth already in the book" ([Kavajecz & Odders-White](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html)). In CME futures, volume at price measures that directly from Rithmic tick prints, which is why the profile should break ties between geometric levels. TRIGGER needs a new, time-stamped event, and price action supplies one. Its job is timing and risk definition, not edge: 28 candlestick patterns tested context-free on DJIA stocks showed no predictive value under bootstrap tests ([Marshall, Young & Rose 2006](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:30:y:2006:i:8:p:2303-2323)). MANAGEMENT splits two ways. Stops belong to structure, because stops cluster just beyond salient levels and your own should sit beyond that cluster, not at it ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). Targets belong to the profile and to extensions that meet it, because take-profits reflect at salient prices.

| Role | Swing structure / price action | Volume profile | Fibonacci |
|---|---|---|---|
| CONTEXT | **Primary**: STRUCTURE(L) state on the highest gate layer; bias changes only on G3 events; G2 = stand aside | Secondary: value migration (WEEK vs prior WEEK; RTH vs prior RTH), open location relative to prior value | Forbidden |
| LOCATION | Secondary: prior swing extremes, LEVELS (PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, ONH/ONL), round numbers | **Primary**: POC, VAH/VAL, HVN, nPOC from WEEK, MONTH, COMP, prior RTH, or ANCH(A→B) | Secondary: the depth band that tells the profile where to search |
| SETUP | **Primary**: setup layer in pullback phase; an untaken swing extreme available to sweep | Acceptance/rejection state at the level, which decides whether reversal or continuation templates are eligible | Depth label (strong / normal / deep / degraded) |
| TRIGGER | **Primary**: sweep-and-reclaim, BOS/CHoCH, 1-2-3 on the trigger layer | Forbidden (it would restate the location) | Forbidden (a touch of 0.618 is not an event) |
| MANAGEMENT | **Primary for stops**: beyond the sweep extreme, leg origin A or zone far edge, plus buffer; trail on confirmed swings | **Primary for targets**: opposing VA edge, next HVN, nPOC | Target spacing (1.272/1.618, projections) only where it meets a profile node, prior swing or round number; grid dies on a close beyond A |

Layer spacing follows the practitioner convention of roughly 4–6× between adjacent layers. A multi-timeframe structure guide warns that beyond two or three timeframes "extra charts mostly add delay and contradiction rather than clarity," and that lower-timeframe breaks against the higher trend should be read as "internal, corrective movement until the higher timeframe's own structure actually breaks" ([LuxAlgo: MTF Structure Alignment](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/multi-timeframe-structure-alignment/)). The same page concedes that "nothing about alignment is guaranteed." The design rule (proposal) is at most three state gates per strategy. Any further layer becomes a level provider (its swing prices and profiles drawn on the chart) and never a gate. W1 and D1 bars and all time-based LEVELS stay on clock time. Volume bars serve the intraday tiers.

| Role | Swing-trade stack (holds hours to days) | Scalp stack (holds seconds to minutes) |
|---|---|---|
| CONTEXT | STRUCTURE(D1) as the gate, STRUCTURE(W1) as a veto; WEEK value vs prior WEEK as secondary read | STRUCTURE(VB-A), or STRUCTURE(H1) for a clock-time variant; price relative to prior-RTH POC |
| LOCATION | PROFILE(WEEK), MONTH, COMP(4) node or naked WEEK POC inside the D1 leg's FIB(A→B) 0.382–0.786 band | Prior-RTH VAH/VAL/POC, ON-profile POC, naked RTH POCs (last 5 sessions), or ANCH POC of the last VB-B leg, inside that leg's 0.5–0.786 band |
| SETUP | H4 in pullback phase while price is in the zone; D1 depth label not "degraded" | VB-B in pullback phase while price is in the zone |
| TRIGGER | H1 (or VB-A) sweep below the latest SWING(H1) low, reclaim, then H1 BOS | VB-C sweep below the latest SWING(VB-C) low, reclaim, then VB-C BOS |
| MANAGEMENT | Stop beyond sweep low or zone far edge (whichever is farther) plus buffer; T1 = B; T2 = 1.272 extension only if it meets a WEEK/MONTH/COMP node or a round number | Stop beyond sweep low plus buffer; T1 = B; T2 = next profile node above B; fixed bar-count time stop |
| Ratio check | D1→H4 ≈ 5.75 on a 23-hour Globex day; H4→H1 = 4 | VB-A→VB-B ≈ 5; VB-B→VB-C ≈ 4 |

### Worked swing template: a daily pullback into weekly value

This template is illustrative. It is a long-side design proposal; shorts mirror it, with the lower prior discussed above. The steps are written so the template can be drawn by hand on a chart before any code exists.

1. **Calibrate.** Use the ETH (Globex) session template for W1, D1, H4 and H1 bars, and compute ATR(L) as the 14-period ATR on each layer's own bars. Build profiles from tick prints at 1-tick rows with the single-row 70% value-area method.
2. **CONTEXT (one condition).** On D1, run SWING(D1) with k = 2.0 × ATR(D1) and mark each swing at its extreme and at its confirmation bar. The condition is STRUCTURE(D1) = UP, established by a G3 event (a D1 close above the last confirmed swing high), *and* STRUCTURE(W1) ≠ DOWN. If W1 shows a G2 event against the trade (a D1 close beyond the W1 protected swing that the W1 bar has not yet confirmed), stand aside.
3. **Draw the leg.** Let A be the protected D1 swing low that launched the last D1 BOS, and B the last confirmed D1 swing high. Draw FIB(A→B) with 0.382/0.5/0.618/0.786. Shade the 0.382–0.786 band and note the current depth label from the band table.
4. **LOCATION (one condition).** Overlay PROFILE(WEEK) for the last two completed weeks, PROFILE(MONTH), PROFILE(COMP(4 weeks)) and every naked WEEK POC. The condition is that at least one POC, VAH, VAL, HVN centre or naked WEEK POC from these profiles (not anchored to A→B) lies inside the shaded band, within 0.25 × ATR(D1). If several qualify, the location is the highest-volume node; the profile picks, Fib does not. Draw the zone as that node ± 0.25 × ATR(D1). If no node qualifies, there is no trade, however many Fib lines are in the band.
5. **SETUP.** Price trades into the zone while STRUCTURE(H4) is DOWN or TRANSITION (the D1 pullback seen from below) and the D1 depth label is not "degraded."
6. **TRIGGER (one condition).** Inside the zone, an H1 bar trades at least one tick below the most recent confirmed SWING(H1) low, and within three H1 bars an H1 bar closes back above that low (the sweep and reclaim). Then an H1 bar closes above the last confirmed SWING(H1) high (H1 BOS back in the D1 direction). Decide in advance whether entry is at that close or on a limit at the first pullback to the broken H1 swing high. A VB-A trigger layer is the volume-bar alternative.
7. **Invalidation before entry.** Cancel the setup if a D1 bar closes below 0.786, if any close prints below A, or if price leaves the zone downward without a trigger. Boroden's rule fits: if price clears the zone without a trigger, stay out ([TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)).
8. **MANAGEMENT.** The stop sits below the lower of the sweep low and the zone's lower edge, minus a buffer of max(2 ticks, 0.15 × ATR(H1)). The buffer is deliberate, because stops cluster just beyond obvious levels. T1 = B, the prior D1 swing high. T2 = the 1.272 extension of A→B, *only* if it lies within 0.25 × ATR(D1) of a WEEK/MONTH/COMP HVN, VAH or naked POC, or of a round 00 handle; otherwise exit everything at T1. After T1, move the stop to entry and trail beyond confirmed SWING(H4) lows.
9. **Refresh.** Recompute WEEK/COMP levels and the volume-bar sizes every weekend. Level effects decay (see the evidence section), so a setup carried over from an old profile should be re-qualified, not assumed.

### Worked scalp template: a volume-bar reclaim at a session node

This is also illustrative, long side, RTH only. The volume bars are sized from the instrument's 20-session ADV, recalibrated weekly, and break at session start. Partial bars are flagged and excluded from swing counting.

1. **Pre-session.** Compute PROFILE(RTH) for the prior session (POC, VAH, VAL) and PROFILE(ON) for the overnight. List naked RTH POCs from the last five sessions and mark ONH/ONL and PDH/PDL. All of these are clock-time objects computed from ticks, never from "the first K volume bars."
2. **CONTEXT (one condition).** STRUCTURE(VB-A) = UP from confirmed SWING(VB-A) with k = 2.0, and the last trade is above the prior-RTH POC. H1 can stand in for VB-A as a clock-time variant.
3. **Draw the leg.** On VB-B, take the last confirmed impulse leg A→B whose size is at least 3 × ATR(VB-B), so that tiny legs are ignored. Draw FIB(A→B) but shade only the coarse 0.5–0.786 band; on small legs, anchor noise makes individual ratios meaningless.
4. **LOCATION (one condition).** At least one prior-RTH VAH/VAL/POC, ON POC or naked RTH POC lies inside the band, within 0.25 × ATR(VB-B). The ANCH(A→B) POC is allowed as a pre-declared alternative, but it shares the leg's anchors, so it is weaker evidence. The zone is that node ± 0.25 × ATR(VB-B).
5. **SETUP.** Price pulls into the zone with STRUCTURE(VB-B) in pullback phase and no close below A.
6. **TRIGGER (one condition).** A VB-C bar trades at least one tick below the most recent confirmed SWING(VB-C) low inside the zone, and within three VB-C bars a VB-C bar closes back above it. Then a VB-C bar closes above the last confirmed SWING(VB-C) high. The trigger must be a *new* event that happens after the level existed, not the level restated, because the profile and the trigger are built from the same intraday prints.
7. **MANAGEMENT.** The stop sits below the sweep low minus max(2 ticks, 0.15 × ATR(VB-C)). T1 = B (the leg extreme). T2 = the next profile node above B (prior VAH, next HVN or a naked POC), if one exists before the next opposing swing; otherwise exit at T1. Fix a bar-count time stop before forward testing; the notes give no value. Stop-cascade effects last hours, not days ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)), so a scalp that has not worked within its window has lost its reason.
8. **Clock guards.** Cancel a pending trigger if the VB-C trigger bar took unusually long to form (deep overnight), and suspend entries around scheduled releases until several VB-C bars have printed afterwards. Volume time cannot be converted back into clock time in advance (see the scalping section), so every deadline needs a clock.

Both templates leave several things out on purpose. Fibonacci time zones are excluded, because Batchelor and Ramyar found duration ratios on the Dow "no more than would be expected by chance" ([City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/16276/)). Standalone candlestick patterns are excluded, as are weighted scores and any second Fib line used as a second confirmation. The proposal files [10 Proposals Fibonacci](10%20Proposals%20Fibonacci.md) and [11 Proposals confluence](11%20Proposals%20confluence.md) build their combined setups on this skeleton, and [07 Automation architecture](07%20Automation%20architecture.md) covers the confirmed-only data plumbing it assumes.

DiNapoli's F38 entry versus F61 abandonment split is an objective depth rule. Bulkowski's close-based 1-2-3 is an objective swing-break trigger. The VSA test-bar definition can be adapted to volume bars. Dalton's poor-high and value-migration concepts supply profile-side context and targets. ICT's sequence describes a stop-run reversal in a way consistent with Osler's stop-cascade mechanics, and the plain restatement in the table keeps the testable core. Generic Fib-cluster practice has **no standard for swing selection, ratio set or grouping tolerance**, so different implementations produce different zones ([LuxAlgo: Fib Clusters](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fib-clusters/)). That is why this document set fixes one definition (FIB-CLUSTER) and uses it everywhere.

## Confluence pays only for independent evidence fixed in advance

Confluence helps under two conditions: the agreeing signals are independent, and the rule is fixed before anyone looks at outcomes. Both are routinely violated. The first violation is counting lines instead of dimensions. Five Fib lines inside a two-point zone are still one geometric fact, and one Fib zone plus one profile node plus one LTF trigger covers three dimensions. The second violation is adding conditions until past charts look good. That is the overfitting process Bailey and colleagues formalized: the probability of a spurious in-sample result rises with the number of configurations tried, and readers cannot assess it because the number of trials is rarely disclosed ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2014](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/)). Under memory effects, overfitting can make expected out-of-sample performance *negative*, not merely zero ([Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)). A secondary summary puts it concretely: **about 45 configurations tried on five years of data can almost guarantee an in-sample Sharpe near 1.0 when the true Sharpe is zero** ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/insidiousness-of-overfitting-investment-strategies-via-iterative-backtests)). The original paper should be checked for the exact figures.

### Count dimensions, not lines

Each candidate confirmation can be sorted by how much it shares with the others (classification is inference from the notes).

| Dependence class | Members | How to count |
|---|---|---|
| Same anchors: one geometric vote | FIB(A→B) levels; A and B themselves; extensions of the same leg; AB=CD from the same pivots; an equal high at B | One vote, however many lines |
| Partially independent | FIB-CLUSTER levels from different legs; AVWAP anchored at the same swing (shares the time anchor, weights by volume); ANCH(A→B) profile nodes; clock-time LEVELS (PDH/PDL, ONH/ONL) that often coincide with swings | At most one geometric vote plus a weak second vote if the classes differ |
| More independent | Volume nodes from profiles not anchored to the current swing (WEEK, MONTH, COMP, prior RTH); round numbers | The volume vote; round numbers as a separate tie-breaker |
| Independent in time | Trigger-layer events: sweep-and-reclaim, BOS, VSA-style test | The TRIGGER role, never counted as location |

Within LOCATION, the honest maximum is two votes: one geometric and one from volume. Anything beyond that adds sample attrition, not evidence.

### Require one condition per role instead of scoring

Weighted confluence scores look rigorous but are harder to audit and easier to fit than required conditions. A score has as many free parameters as it has weights, plus a threshold. A rule of "one pre-declared condition per role, all roles required" has one parameter per role and no trade-offs between roles. The evidence also argues against expecting anyone to rank level strength well. In Osler's data, the firms agreed on levels only about 30% of the time, and **"none of the firms correctly assessed the relative likelihood of trend interruptions at the different levels,"** so their own strength ratings had no value ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). If a score is shown at all, it should be display-only on the review chart. If one must gate trades, use equal weights for independent dimensions, a cap of four or five factors, and weights fixed before review.

### Scale every tolerance to the ATR of the layer that defines the zone

Tolerances in use are inconsistent. Boroden wants cluster width ideally ≤2 points in ES and 3–4 points in NQ and called a 2-point near-miss "within reason" ([TradingMarkets: Boroden](https://tradingmarkets.com/?p=661586)). The DiNapoli implementation groups levels within 0.5 × ATR(14) ([LuxAlgo: DiNapoli](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/dinapoli-levels/)). A fixed tick band drifts with volatility: a 2-point ES zone is tight on a 60-point-ATR day and loose on a 15-point day. This document set states each tolerance once, as a fraction of ATR(L) on the layer that defines the zone, with the same multiples for every instrument: **0.2 × ATR(L) for FIB-CLUSTER grouping, 0.15 × ATR(L) for equal highs/lows, and 0.25 × ATR(L) for a profile node to count as inside a Fib band.** The last value matches the ±0.25-ATR zone in the research notes' swing template and the 0.25–0.5-ATR range consistent with DiNapoli's default. Per-instrument tuning is a fitting channel and should be refused.

### Rare setups and the eyeball backtest

Every extra condition shrinks the sample. If four roughly independent filters each pass 30% of candidate bars, the joint pass rate is **about 0.8% (0.3⁴)**. A high-confluence D1 setup may then appear only a few times a year per instrument. That is too few to judge, and it invites remembering only the winners. Swing-trading rules should run identically across ES, NQ and CL (and across further liquid futures if the sample is still too thin), so that instances accumulate under one rule set. The base rate is the second trap. In Osler's test, rates bounced off *arbitrary* levels 56.2% of the time ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)), so "the level held" means little without a chance baseline. Wider zones mechanically catch more turns ([Tsinaslanidis et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421012495)).

The third trap matters most for a trader who will not backtest. **Visual review is itself a fitting loop.** Each "that one looks wrong, let me nudge k" is a configuration tried, and Bailey's logic applies whether or not a computer counted the trials. Three mitigations follow. Freeze the rules before reviewing a batch of charts. Record every instance the rule fires, including the ugly ones, not only exemplars. Judge the rule only on forward instances that arrive after the freeze. In the forward-validation protocol in [07 Automation architecture](07%20Automation%20architecture.md), every rule change increments a strategy version and restarts the sample. That bookkeeping turns "not optimizing" into actual out-of-sample evidence.

## The evidence base is a few points of edge, and none of it is about Fibonacci or profile nodes

Credible evidence supports three narrow claims, and all three concern support/resistance in general. Osler's FX test covered daily levels published by **six firms from January 1996 to March 1998**. The published levels beat arbitrary ones in **all 16 firm-currency pairs**, with about a 1.8% chance of the month-by-month outcome under no information, and their predictive power lasted **at least five business days** ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). Order data from roughly **9,700 stop-loss and take-profit orders** at one dealing bank showed take-profits clustering at round numbers, which explains reversals, and stops clustering just beyond them, which explains faster moves after breaks ([Osler 2003, J. Finance](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html)). Stop-loss cascades were statistically significant "for hours, although not for days" ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). In equities, support and resistance coincide with limit-order-book depth peaks ([Kavajecz & Odders-White 2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html)). A preprint adds that levels with more prior bounces are more likely to bounce again, and that the bounce probability decays over time ([Chung & Bellotti 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07410v1)).

The absences matter as much. The most rigorous Fib tests find nothing beyond chance. An algorithmic study across three equity markets found "no support for Fibonacci retracements as a standalone technical rule" ([Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421012495)). Successive Dow trend ratios showed "a few significant ratios… but no more than would be expected by chance" ([Batchelor & Ramyar](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/16276/)). The FX correction histogram has no peaks at Fib levels ([Forexop](https://forexop.com/?p=7920)). No peer-reviewed test of POC, HVN, LVN or value-area behaviour in futures was found. No study tests whether levels flagged by several independent methods outperform single-method levels. No rigorous study tests multi-timeframe alignment filters in any market. The "self-fulfilling" story for Fib and profile nodes is plausible but unproven. A self-fulfilling Fib effect would also require many participants to anchor the *same* grid. That is plausible for obvious D1/W1 swings and implausible for idiosyncratic intraday anchors (inference).

| Claim used by this design | Grade | Best evidence | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Published S/R interrupts trends more than arbitrary levels | A | Osler 2000: 60.8% vs 56.2%, 16/16 pairs | FX, 1996–98; small effect |
| Stops cluster just beyond salient levels; crossing triggers fast moves lasting hours | A (round numbers); B (swing extremes) | Osler SR125/SR150, J. Finance 2003 | One bank's FX orders, 1999–2000 |
| S/R levels sit where book depth is | A | Kavajecz & Odders-White 2004 | NYSE equities |
| Repeatedly tested levels bounce more; effects decay | B | Chung & Bellotti 2021 | Preprint; full methods not reviewed |
| HTF trend persistence justifies the CONTEXT layer | B | Moskowitz et al. 2012; Gao et al. 2018 | Supports trends generally, not MTF swing gating |
| Fib ratio levels beat chance | D | Tsinaslanidis et al.; Batchelor & Ramyar; Forexop | One positive working paper emphasizing 0/50/100 |
| Profile nodes predict reactions in futures | C | None; mechanism only via the S/R–depth link | Practitioner consensus only |
| 80% rule as stated | D | Informal ES test, 39–40% full fills | 41 triggers, 2008 |
| More confluence means higher probability | C | None | Untested anywhere found |
| Standalone candlestick triggers | D | Marshall, Young & Rose 2006 | DJIA stocks, context-free tests |

The transfer caveat applies to all of it. Osler's data is FX from one bank around 2000, and Kavajecz–Odders-White is equities. Neither is CME futures, and both predate today's HFT-dominated books. The mechanisms (order clustering, stop cascades, depth at salient levels) are plausible for ES, NQ and CL but not measured there. What the evidence does license is specific. Single levels are small edges, so a trigger is required. Your own stops belong beyond the obvious cluster plus a buffer. Recent levels should outweigh stale ones, so swing-trade levels are refreshed weekly. Where geometry offers several candidate levels, measured volume should break the tie, because volume is the closest observable proxy for the depth that makes levels work.

## Scalps run on session value and volume-bar triggers; swings run on weekly structure and composite value

The roles stay fixed across horizons, but the inputs and their reliability change. In scalping, the session or leg profile and the immediate trigger dominate. Swings on small legs are noisy, so Fib on tiny legs is at most a coarse band. Stop-cascade dynamics operate on the hours scale where Osler's evidence is strongest. In swing trading, W1/D1 structure and multi-week profiles dominate. Fib on large, obvious daily and weekly legs is more plausibly watched by others, and extensions meeting HTF nodes are the main target logic. One practitioner claim says retracements "work somewhat better on shorter timeframes" because of wide adoption. No data supports it ([Engineer the Forex](https://sites.google.com/view/engineer-the-forex/fibonacci-retracements)), and it conflicts with the anchor-salience argument above.

| Aspect | Scalping | Swing trading |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant dimensions | Session profile + volume-bar trigger | W1/D1 structure + WEEK/MONTH/COMP profiles |
| Fib's job | Coarse 0.5–0.786 band on the VB-B leg only | Full depth label on the D1 leg; extensions as candidate targets |
| Profiles used | Prior RTH, ON, dPOC, naked RTH POCs, ANCH of the last leg | WEEK, MONTH, COMP(n weeks), naked WEEK POCs |
| Trigger layer | VB-C | H1 or VB-A |
| Tolerance scale | 0.25 × ATR(VB-B) | 0.25 × ATR(D1) |
| Sample size | Many instances per week; noise and costs dominate | Few per year per instrument; run identical rules across instruments |
| Closest evidence | Stop cascades (hours) | Level persistence (≥5 days), time-series momentum |
| Main pitfalls | Profile and trigger built from the same prints; thin ETH profiles; news compression | Contract roll shifts profiles and pivots; CL gaps through levels on inventory/OPEC news; stale composites |

### Volume bars change the swings and the trigger, not the profile

Volume bars matter mainly for scalping, and they change the interactions in one specific way. The profile is built from tick prints at 1-tick rows, so it is identical whatever bars sit underneath. Volume bars change the *swings* and the *trigger*. The statistical case for the volume clock is real but partial. On E-mini S&P futures, sampling every 1/50 of average daily volume removed most intra-session seasonality and gave "a partial recovery of Normality" ([Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara 2012](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)). A 2026 crypto preprint found normality gains "regime-dependent and secondary," with calendar bars a competitive baseline ([Fayyaz et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26158v1)). Bar-pattern reading carries over to constant-volume charts, unlike range charts ([Galen Woods](https://www.tradingsetupsreview.com/trading-charts-without-time-range-tick-volume/)). Brooks' course staff answered "Yes, and yes" when asked whether trendlines and signal bars transfer to tick charts ([Brooks forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/general-trading-discussion/tick-chart/)). Both are practitioner opinion (grade C).

On VB-C, a SWING(VB-C) low is confirmed after a fixed number of contracts rather than minutes. A sweep-and-reclaim therefore means price was rejected within a fixed participation budget, which arguably makes such events more comparable across the session than on M1 bars (inference). Bar range becomes price displacement per fixed volume, a price-impact proxy. Bar duration becomes a free activity measure. An engulfing bar no longer implies "more volume," because both bars hold the same volume. The VSA test-bar rule needs translating, because raw volume per bar is constant by construction. "Lower volume than the prior two bars" has to become *slower* (longer duration) and *narrower* (smaller range per bar) than recent VB-C bars. That adaptation is inference, not sourced, and it should be one pre-declared variant, not a second trigger stacked on the sweep. Mandelbrot's caveat applies directly: local time "must flow at random," so knowing the right clock does not help predict when the next N contracts will print ([Mandelbrot 1973, quoted in Easley et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)). Every deadline (time stops, news blackouts, session close, weekly bar close) must stay on the clock. ETH profiles have more LVN noise because overnight ES/NQ volume is thin, so the RTH versus ETH choice has to be fixed per strategy before review.

### Swing trading lives with small samples and slow levels

Sample size binds hardest on swing setups (see the 0.8% arithmetic above), and the remedy is breadth across instruments, not looser rules. Levels also need maintenance. Osler's ≥5-day persistence and the decay in Chung and Bellotti argue for refreshing WEEK and COMP levels weekly and expiring naked POCs by age. Contract rolls shift composites and swing anchors by the roll spread, so profiles should be built from the front month actually traded. CL's sensitivity to inventory and OPEC news means profile levels can be gapped through, which argues for wider buffers or skipping report days.

### The cleanest multi-timeframe confluence crosses the horizons

The most independent combination available uses the swing layer's location as a filter for scalps (inference). Take scalp longs only while D1 structure is UP and price is at or inside a W1/D1 location zone, and trigger on VB-C. The HTF level and the LTF trigger are then independent in both construction and timescale. That is exactly the kind of agreement confluence is supposed to reward.

## Conclusion

Thinking in roles turns "confluence" from a counting exercise into an accounting one. The trader has two kinds of evidence about *where* (geometry and traded volume) and one about *when* (a new price-action event). Fibonacci contributes no third kind; it contributes a ruler. Its most valuable outputs are probably negative ones: a pullback beyond 0.786 says stop trading continuation, and a stop beyond that depth sits beyond most normal corrections. The profile's most valuable output is tie-breaking. Among several geometric candidates it picks the one where the market actually transacted, and in CME futures that is a direct measurement where swings and ratios are only proxies.

For someone who will not backtest, the main risk is less a bad concept than a good concept slowly tuned until past charts look perfect. The defence is procedural, not statistical: declare one condition per role, scale tolerances to ATR, freeze the rules, log every firing, and let only post-freeze instances count. The weakness of the evidence base (a few percentage points of edge for levels in general, nothing specific for Fib or profile nodes in futures) is the reason to keep the conditions few and fixed. The design principles below are the commitments the proposal files 08–11 rely on.

## Design principles for keeping the dimensions separate but cooperating

1. **One job per dimension.** Swing structure supplies CONTEXT, TRIGGER and invalidation. The volume profile supplies LOCATION and targets. Fibonacci supplies the depth label and target spacing. No dimension may answer another's question: Fib never sets bias, the profile never triggers, and a trigger never justifies a location.
2. **One pre-declared condition per role, all roles required.** A condition may have clauses (for example "D1 UP and W1 not DOWN"), but it is written down before review. Weighted scores are for display only.
3. **Count dimensions, not lines.** In LOCATION, allow at most one geometric vote (swing level, FIB(A→B) band or FIB-CLUSTER) and one volume vote (a PROFILE node). Levels that share anchors are one vote.
4. **The profile picks the level; Fib labels the depth.** Inside the pre-declared Fib band, the trade level is the qualifying profile node. A Fib level in an LVN is not a location.
5. **Prefer profiles not anchored to the current leg.** When claiming two-dimension agreement, WEEK, MONTH, COMP or prior-RTH nodes count fully. ANCH(A→B) nodes share the Fib's anchors and count as the weaker alternative.
6. **Context comes from above the trigger.** Space layers about 4–6× apart, with at most three state gates; other layers only provide levels. Bias changes only on G3 events, and G2 means stand aside.
7. **Confirmed inputs only.** Swings, Fib grids, anchored profiles and AVWAPs drive decisions only from their confirmation time. HTF values come from closed bars. Time-based LEVELS and W1/D1 bars stay on clock time.
8. **Invalidation is structural.** Stops go beyond the sweep extreme, the leg origin A or the zone's far edge, plus max(2 ticks, 0.15 × ATR of the trigger layer). They never sit at a Fib line. A close beyond A kills the grid, and no new anchor may be chosen to rescue the setup.
9. **Targets need a second dimension.** An extension or projection becomes a target only when it lands within tolerance of a profile node, a prior swing or a round number. Otherwise the target is the prior swing extreme.
10. **Tolerances are ATR fractions of the layer that defines the zone.** Use 0.2 × ATR(L) for FIB-CLUSTER, 0.15 × ATR(L) for equal highs/lows and 0.25 × ATR(L) for node-in-band coincidence, the same for every instrument, with no per-instrument tuning.
11. **Disagreements are resolved by rule, not by judgment in the moment.** The disagreement table above, or a strategy's own version of it, is part of the strategy's definition.
12. **Freeze, log, forward-test.** Every rule change increments the strategy version and restarts the sample. Every firing is logged, not only exemplars. Visual review checks that the rule did what it says, not whether the outcome was pleasing.
13. **Breadth over looseness.** Run identical rules across ES/MES, NQ/MNQ and CL/MCL to build samples. Recalibrate volume-bar sizes and refresh weekly and composite levels on a fixed weekly schedule, never intraday.
